## STAT-QL-084 — Classical probability

A fair 12-sided die has 5 outcomes in a specified event. Find the probability of the event.

A. 0.42
B. 0.37
C. 0.52
D. 0.47

**Answer:** A. 0.42

**Explanation:** For equally likely outcomes, P(A) = favourable outcomes / total outcomes = 5/12 ≈ 0.42.

## STAT-QL-085 — Empirical probability

An event occurred 24 times in 80 repeated trials. Find its empirical probability.

A. 0.25
B. 0.35
C. 0.3
D. 0.4

**Answer:** C. 0.3

**Explanation:** Empirical probability is observed frequency / number of trials = 24/80 = 0.3.

## STAT-QL-086 — Complement rule

If P(A) = 0.35, find P(not A).

A. 0.65
B. 0.6
C. 0.75
D. 0.7

**Answer:** A. 0.65

**Explanation:** The complement rule gives P(not A) = 1 − P(A) = 1 − 0.35 = 0.65.

## STAT-QL-087 — Union of mutually exclusive events

Events A and B are mutually exclusive, with P(A) = 0.25 and P(B) = 0.4. Find P(A or B).

A. 0.65
B. 0.75
C. 0.7
D. 0.6

**Answer:** A. 0.65

**Explanation:** Mutually exclusive events have P(A∩B) = 0. Thus P(A∪B) = P(A)+P(B) = 0.25+0.4 = 0.65.

## STAT-QL-088 — General addition rule

For events A and B, P(A) = 0.45, P(B) = 0.35, and P(A∩B) = 0.15. Find P(A∪B).

A. 0.7
B. 0.65
C. 0.75
D. 0.6

**Answer:** B. 0.65

**Explanation:** Use P(A∪B) = P(A)+P(B)−P(A∩B) = 0.45+0.35−0.15 = 0.65.

## STAT-QL-089 — Conditional probability

If P(A∩B) = 0.2 and P(B) = 0.5, find P(A|B).

A. 0.5
B. 0.35
C. 0.4
D. 0.45

**Answer:** C. 0.4

**Explanation:** P(A|B) = P(A∩B)/P(B) = 0.2/0.5 = 0.4.

## STAT-QL-090 — Intersection from conditional probability

If P(A|B) = 0.25 and P(B) = 0.4, find P(A∩B).

A. 0.15
B. 0.1
C. 0.05
D. 0.2

**Answer:** B. 0.1

**Explanation:** P(A∩B) = P(A|B)P(B) = 0.25 × 0.4 = 0.1.

## STAT-QL-091 — Test independence of events

Events A and B have P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5, and P(A∩B) = 0.2. Are they independent?

A. Independent
B. Mutually exclusive
C. Not independent
D. Cannot be determined

**Answer:** A. Independent

**Explanation:** Independence requires P(A∩B) = P(A)P(B). Here P(A)P(B) = 0.4 × 0.5 = 0.2, compared with P(A∩B) = 0.2; the events are independent.

## STAT-QL-092 — Intersection of independent events

A and B are independent, with P(A) = 0.4 and P(B) = 0.5. Find P(A∩B).

A. 0.2
B. 0.3
C. 0.15
D. 0.25

**Answer:** A. 0.2

**Explanation:** For independent events, P(A∩B) = P(A)P(B) = 0.4 × 0.5 = 0.2.

## STAT-QL-093 — Law of total probability

A population consists of groups A and B with P(A) = 0.25 and P(B) = 0.75. An event E has P(E|A) = 0.6 and P(E|B) = 0.2. Find P(E).

A. 0.25
B. 0.35
C. 0.4
D. 0.3

**Answer:** D. 0.3

**Explanation:** By total probability, P(E) = P(A)P(E|A)+P(B)P(E|B) = 0.25×0.6+0.75×0.2 = 0.3.

## STAT-QL-094 — Bayes theorem

A population consists of groups A and B with P(A) = 0.4 and P(B) = 0.6. An event E has P(E|A) = 0.8 and P(E|B) = 0.2. Given that E occurred, find P(A|E).

A. 0.83
B. 0.68
C. 0.78
D. 0.73

**Answer:** D. 0.73

**Explanation:** Bayes' theorem gives P(A|E) = P(A)P(E|A)/P(E). The denominator is 0.4×0.8+0.6×0.2. Thus P(A|E) = 0.73.

## STAT-QL-095 — Complement of a compound event

For events A and B, P(A) = 0.45, P(B) = 0.35, and P(A∩B) = 0.15. Find the probability that neither A nor B occurs.

A. 0.35
B. 0.45
C. 0.3
D. 0.4

**Answer:** A. 0.35

**Explanation:** P(neither A nor B) = 1 − P(A∪B). First P(A∪B) = 0.45+0.35−0.15 = 0.65. Therefore the required probability is 0.35.
