# PNL-CP-004 Distractor Contract

Status: FREEZE CANDIDATE

Distractors must represent a traceable transaction-chain misconception. Arbitrary numerical offsets are prohibited.

## Approved misconception families

- Adding successive percentages instead of multiplying stage factors.
- Applying every rate to the original cost price rather than the current trader's purchase price.
- Reversing a forward chain without dividing by the stage multiplier.
- Treating a loss stage as a profit stage, or vice versa.
- Omitting one transaction from a two-stage or three-stage chain.
- Using the final selling price as the percentage base for a trader's profit or loss.
- Reporting an intermediate price as the final selling price.
- Comparing stage percentages instead of stage-wise rupee gains or losses.
- Assuming equal percentage changes cancel in a mixed profit-loss chain.
- Calculating commission on cost price instead of gross selling price.
- Adding commission to gross proceeds instead of deducting it.
- Treating gross selling price as net receipt.
- Ignoring buyer expense when computing effective cost.
- Computing profit on purchase price alone after an additional expense is stated.
- Returning the required net receipt as the gross selling price in a commission inverse.
- Applying commission before the sale-price target when the question specifies commission on gross selling price.

## Option-quality rules

- Four options must be distinct after formatting.
- The correct answer must not be identifiable by length, units or wording alone.
- Direction-bearing answers must include profit, loss or no change when the question asks for the nature of the result.
- Amount and percentage options must not be mixed unless the exam format explicitly tests answer-type recognition.
- Table, caselet and statement distractors must arise from the displayed facts, not unrelated arithmetic.
