# ANA-CP-007 — Curated Word Registry Design

Status: **DESIGN CONTRACT — DATASET NOT YET POPULATED**

CP-007 requires meaningful English words, but the generator must never depend on arbitrary runtime dictionary lookups or unreviewed model-generated vocabulary. This document defines the curated lexical registry and its audit requirements.

---

## 1. Registry purpose

The registry supports word-structure analogy rules that depend on:

- vowels and consonants;
- source positions;
- complete alphabet positions;
- word length;
- repeated-letter equality patterns;
- class-dependent alphabet movement;
- class regrouping if later admitted.

It is not a semantic analogy dataset. Definitions and conceptual word relations are not used by CP-007.

---

## 2. Canonical record

```ts
export interface AnaWordStructureRecord {
  id: string;
  word: string;
  normalized: string;
  length: number;
  vowelCount: number;
  consonantCount: number;
  vowelPositions: readonly number[];
  consonantPositions: readonly number[];
  oddPositionLetters: string;
  evenPositionLetters: string;
  alphabetPositions: readonly number[];
  alphabetPositionSum: number;
  equalityPattern: readonly number[];
  distinctLetterCount: number;
  repeatedLetterCount: number;
  hasRepeatedLetters: boolean;
  lexicalBand: "COMMON" | "STANDARD_EXAM";
  partOfSpeech?: "NOUN" | "VERB" | "ADJECTIVE" | "ADVERB";
  locale: "en-IN";
  sourceRefs: readonly string[];
  editorialStatus: "REVIEWED";
  enabled: boolean;
}
```

Every derived field must be regenerated and verified during CI. A mismatch between stored metadata and independent derivation fails the dataset audit.

---

## 3. Editorial eligibility

Include words that are:

- commonly understood by SSC, Banking and Punjab-state exam candidates;
- unambiguous in spelling;
- 4–10 letters long for the main pool;
- ordinary dictionary words rather than names, brands or abbreviations;
- free of punctuation, spaces, apostrophes and hyphens;
- suitable in all three interface languages while preserved in Latin script;
- not offensive, politically sensitive or context-dependent;
- not so obscure that vocabulary knowledge becomes the tested skill.

Exclude:

- proper nouns;
- inflected variants added solely to inflate the pool;
- rare scientific or medical words unless source-backed and clearly standard exam vocabulary;
- words with multiple common spellings;
- contractions;
- romanized Hindi or Punjabi words;
- exact duplicates under case normalization.

---

## 4. Structural pool requirements

The dataset audit should report coverage by length, vowel count, consonant count, parity layout and equality pattern.

Minimum provisional goals are evidence targets, not permanent quotas:

- at least 50 reviewed words for each length from 4 through 8;
- at least 25 reviewed words for lengths 9 and 10;
- at least 100 words containing two or more vowels;
- at least 100 words containing two or more consonants;
- at least 100 words where vowels do not occupy only one parity class;
- at least 80 words with repeated letters;
- at least 30 distinct nontrivial equality-pattern groups with two or more member words;
- at least 80 words whose vowel-removal output differs from both parity extractions;
- at least 80 words whose consonant-removal output differs from both parity extractions.

These values are dataset-readiness thresholds. They do not determine QL count.

---

## 5. Family-specific indexes

Generate indexes from the canonical records rather than maintaining parallel manual lists.

### Vowel-removal index

Key fields:

- consonant-only output;
- output length;
- collision with odd/even extraction;
- duplicate output count.

### Consonant-removal index

Key fields:

- vowel-only output;
- output length;
- collision with odd/even extraction;
- duplicate output count.

### Position-extraction index

Key fields:

- odd-position output;
- even-position output;
- whether either equals the vowel-only or consonant-only output.

### Alphabet-position indexes

Key fields:

- full position sequence;
- complete sum;
- sum collision with length profiles;
- repeated-letter contribution.

### Equality-pattern index

Map canonical pattern keys such as `1-2-3-2-1` to all eligible words.

A pattern group is usable only when:

- at least two words share it;
- enough same-length near-pattern distractors exist;
- no option duplicates the answer word;
- vocabulary remains natural.

### Differential-shift index

This is generated by runtime simulation rather than stored permanently.

For every candidate word and class-shift context, record:

- output;
- wrap count;
- whether both classes visibly change;
- uniform-shift collision;
- odd/even-position collision;
- output identity or duplicate relation;
- whether the output is accidentally another source word.

---

## 6. Source and target selection

For one generated analogy:

- source and target words must be different;
- source and target should preferably differ in length where the rule permits it, unless length equality is needed for option comparability;
- they must activate every branch of the rule;
- they must not share an accidental simpler relation;
- repeated-letter tasks must use different lexical forms with the same canonical pattern;
- target words must not be visible in distractor derivations from the source;
- source and target transformed outputs must be distinct.

Avoid pairing:

- singular and plural forms of the same word;
- obvious derivational relatives;
- words differing by only one final suffix;
- near-anagrams when anagram reasoning is not intended.

---

## 7. Distractor-support metadata

The registry should support plausible distractors without storing final option text.

Useful derived errors include:

- removed the wrong letter class;
- retained one extra vowel or consonant;
- selected the opposite parity;
- reversed the extracted letters;
- omitted a repeated occurrence from an alphabet sum;
- used A=0 instead of A=1;
- counted distinct letters rather than all positions;
- used word length instead of alphabet sum;
- swapped vowel and consonant shifts;
- changed only vowels or only consonants;
- used one uniform shift;
- matched only repeated-letter count, not the full equality pattern.

Each final distractor still requires complete ambiguity validation against all eligible rules.

---

## 8. Multilingual handling

The word token remains unchanged across `en-IN`, `hi-IN` and `pa-IN`.

Localized layers translate only:

- instructions;
- rule statements;
- position names;
- vowel/consonant terminology;
- arithmetic explanation text;
- trap rejection.

The registry must not contain literal Hindi or Punjabi translations as structural tokens for this runtime.

---

## 9. Dataset audits

Required CI checks:

1. unique IDs and normalized words;
2. A–Z-only validation;
3. length and count recomputation;
4. vowel/consonant partition completeness;
5. alphabet-position sequence and sum parity;
6. equality-pattern recomputation;
7. no duplicate records;
8. lexical-band and editorial-status presence;
9. family-specific yield counts;
10. collision summaries;
11. pattern-group usability;
12. disabled records excluded from generation;
13. deterministic index generation.

The audit should emit a Markdown coverage report and machine-readable JSON summary.

---

## 10. Initial implementation sequence

1. create the derivation utility and independent validator;
2. add a small reviewed pilot registry;
3. simulate all confirmed native rules over the pilot pool;
4. measure unambiguous generation yield;
5. expand weak structural buckets deliberately;
6. rerun collision and distractor audits;
7. freeze only the rule families that show reliable yield;
8. allocate permanent QL IDs after saturation.
