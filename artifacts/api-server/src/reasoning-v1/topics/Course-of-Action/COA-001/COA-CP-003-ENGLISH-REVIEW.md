## Q1 — COA-QL-003 — Medium

**Statement:** A bank's fraud system flags an unusually large transfer from a customer's account, but the available information does not yet show whether the customer authorised it.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The bank should place a temporary hold on the flagged transfer and verify the customer's authorisation through an approved contact channel.  
II. The bank should permanently close the customer's account immediately because the fraud alert proves that the customer has misused the account.

**Answer: A — Only I follows.**

**Explanation:** A temporary hold limits possible loss while the bank checks whether the customer authorised the transfer. A fraud alert is not proof of customer misconduct, so permanent closure before verification is premature.

## Q2 — COA-QL-003 — Medium

**Statement:** Several candidates report that their attendance is missing from the examination system, but the centre still has signed attendance sheets and entry records that have not been checked.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The examination body should cancel the affected candidates' results at once because a missing electronic attendance entry means they were absent.  
II. The examination body should compare the signed attendance sheets and entry records with the electronic data before changing any candidate's result status.

**Answer: B — Only II follows.**

**Explanation:** The electronic attendance is disputed and independent records are available. Those records should be checked before any result is changed; cancellation before verification would be premature.

## Q3 — COA-QL-003 — Hard

**Statement:** A company's expense system shows repeated claims from one employee for the same travel date, but some claims may be corrected resubmissions after earlier entries were rejected.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The company should compare the claim versions, receipts and approval history before deciding whether duplicate reimbursement was actually attempted.  
II. Until the review is complete, the company should pause payment only on the disputed claims rather than block all of the employee's unrelated approved reimbursements.

**Answer: C — Both I and II follow.**

**Explanation:** The repeated entries may have a legitimate explanation, so the records should first be checked. A limited hold on only the disputed claims also protects against duplicate payment without unnecessarily blocking unrelated reimbursements.

## Q4 — COA-QL-003 — Medium

**Statement:** A website receives complaints that some accounts were accessed from unfamiliar devices, but the logs have not yet been checked to distinguish genuine compromise from normal device changes.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The service should permanently delete every reported account immediately so that no unauthorised person can continue using it.  
II. The service should take no action because an unfamiliar device can sometimes belong to the genuine account holder.

**Answer: D — Neither I nor II follows.**

**Explanation:** Deleting accounts before checking the logs is irreversible and excessive. But doing nothing is also unsuitable because the reports may indicate a real security problem. The access should first be verified and temporary safeguards used where needed.

## Q5 — COA-QL-003 — Easy

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The seller should review the dispatch and delivery-condition records promptly and then process the claim according to what the records and inspection establish.

## Q6 — COA-QL-003 — Medium

**Statement:** A clinic notices that several appointment records show the same patient identifier, but it is not yet clear whether this is a duplicate-entry error or whether records were linked incorrectly.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The clinic should merge all matching records immediately so that only one patient file remains before the records are checked further.  
II. The clinic should verify the identifiers and source records first, then correct only the entries confirmed to be duplicates or wrongly linked.

**Answer: B — Only II follows.**

**Explanation:** The matching records may not belong together. Merging first can create a new error. The identifiers and source records should be checked before any records are combined or corrected.

## Q7 — COA-QL-003 — Hard

**Statement:** A warehouse finds a mismatch between recorded and physical stock after a system migration, but both migration errors and unauthorised removals remain possible explanations.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The warehouse should reconcile pre-migration records, transaction logs and a fresh physical count before assigning responsibility for the shortage.  
II. The warehouse should temporarily tighten issue controls on the affected stock category while the mismatch is investigated, without accusing individual staff in advance.

**Answer: C — Both I and II follow.**

**Explanation:** Reconciliation can establish whether the mismatch is a data error or an actual stock loss. Temporary tighter controls are also reasonable while the cause is being checked because they reduce further unexplained movement without assigning blame.

## Q9 — COA-QL-003 — Medium

## Q10 — COA-QL-003 — Medium

## Q12 — COA-QL-003 — Medium

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The utility should replace every meter in the service area immediately on the assumption that the maintenance made all of them inaccurate.

## Q13 — COA-QL-004 — Easy

**Statement:** Visitors are missing their scheduled appointments because a government office changed room numbers but the reception desk is still using the old floor directory.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The office administration should update the reception directory and place clear temporary signs showing the new room numbers until permanent signs are installed.  
II. The office should tell visitors to arrive one hour earlier so they have enough time to search for the correct rooms themselves.

**Answer: A — Only I follows.**

**Explanation:** Updating the directory and signs directly corrects the office's information problem. Asking visitors to arrive earlier leaves the inaccurate directory unchanged and shifts the burden to them.

## Q14 — COA-QL-004 — Medium

**Courses of Action:**

II. The branch manager should use a separate quick-service queue or designated window for simple document requests during peak periods where staffing permits.

**Explanation:** Opening a new branch is a long-term step and does not address the current queue promptly. A separate quick-service arrangement is within branch control and can reduce unnecessary waiting.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The utility should keep the separate spreadsheets and tell agents to choose whichever sheet has the most recent visible timestamp before answering customers.

## CP003 review checklist

Reviewers should check:

- QL003 distinguishes verification from both premature punishment and passive inaction;
- temporary safeguards remain proportionate and do not silently assume guilt;
- QL004 actions are realistically within institutional or operational authority;
- administrative distractors are plausible incomplete, delayed or over-broad responses rather than nonsense;
- Hard questions are harder because of competing reasonable considerations, not harder English;
- explanations identify the decisive issue in simple language;
- both courses are judged independently;
- no answer can be guessed from words such as `immediately`, `verify`, `suspend`, `permanently`, or from action length;
- no question requires outside specialist knowledge.

No CP003 learner-facing surface is frozen until explicit human approval.
