## Q1 — COA-QL-009 — Hard

**Statement:** An examination body finds that one centre uploaded a result file with unusual score changes. The original response data and audit trail are available, other centres are unaffected, and the publication deadline is later the same day.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The examination body should hold publication only for the affected centre, preserve the uploaded file and audit trail, compare them with the original response data, and correct or release the centre's results after verification.  
II. The examination body should cancel every candidate's result from the centre immediately and suspend publication for all other centres until a complete examination-wide inquiry is finished.

**Answer: A — Only I follows.**

**Explanation:** Course I keeps the issue limited to the affected centre, preserves the evidence and verifies the change before altering results. Course II assumes wrongdoing before verification and also stops unaffected centres, so it is both premature and unnecessarily broad.

## Q2 — COA-QL-009 — Medium

**Statement:** A bank detects a reconciliation mismatch in one batch of ATM withdrawals. Transaction logs are intact, the affected batch can be isolated, and normal deposits and transfers are operating correctly.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The bank should temporarily freeze withdrawals and transfers for every customer until the affected ATM batch is reconciled, so no further financial discrepancy can occur during the review.  
II. The bank should isolate the affected ATM batch, compare the machine and transaction logs, correct confirmed debit errors, and keep unaffected banking services available while the review is completed.

**Answer: B — Only II follows.**

**Explanation:** The problem is limited to one ATM batch. Freezing transactions for every customer is much wider than necessary. Course II checks the affected records, corrects confirmed errors and keeps unrelated services running.

## Q3 — COA-QL-009 — Hard

**Statement:** A clinic's appointment system has sent some patients reminders for the wrong date after a data import. The master appointment register is correct, current-day appointments must continue, and the affected reminder batch can be identified.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The clinic should pause only the affected reminder batch, compare it with the master register, send corrected reminders to confirmed affected patients and test the import before restarting that reminder process.  
II. Until the reminder import is corrected, reception staff should verify the appointment date from the master register when an affected patient calls or arrives, while normal current-day appointments continue.

**Answer: C — Both I and II follow.**

**Explanation:** Course I fixes the faulty reminder process without interrupting correct appointments. Course II is a useful temporary safeguard because staff use the correct register while the technical problem is being repaired. Both actions fit the situation.

## Q4 — COA-QL-009 — Medium

**Statement:** Passengers report that a route-information app is showing an old stop sequence on one bus route after a recent update. The approved route file is available, buses are running safely, and the wrong display is causing passengers to miss stops.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The operator should suspend the route for the rest of the week and use that period to decide whether the route itself should be redesigned, rather than first checking whether the app simply loaded the old approved stop file.  
II. The operator should keep the current app display until the scheduled overnight update window, while asking drivers to verbally announce the correct stops if passengers appear confused.

**Answer: D — Neither I nor II follows.**

**Explanation:** Course I treats an information error as if the route itself were faulty and interrupts service before checking the available correct file. Course II gives some temporary help but leaves a known wrong display active even though it can be corrected now. One is too broad; the other is too weak and late.

## Q5 — COA-QL-009 — Hard

**Statement:** A permit office sees a sudden backlog after a vendor-system update. Some applications may not have synchronised, but the office can preserve logs, pause new imports briefly and process already verified applications manually.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The office should preserve the synchronisation logs, briefly pause new imports, identify which applications failed to transfer, process already verified applications through the available manual route, and resume imports after the fault is corrected and tested.  
II. The office should replace the entire permit system immediately, erase the vendor update and migration logs, and investigate the cause later from staff recollections if the backlog continues.

**Answer: A — Only I follows.**

**Explanation:** Course I first protects the evidence, contains the faulty import, keeps verified work moving and then restores the system after testing. Course II replaces the whole system before the cause is known and destroys the strongest evidence needed for the investigation.

## Q6 — COA-QL-009 — Medium

**Statement:** A portal's password-reset emails are delayed because a notification service is rate-limited. Identity checks and sign-in for users who know their passwords are working, and extra notification capacity can be enabled temporarily.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The portal should temporarily remove identity verification from password resets so requests can be completed without waiting for the delayed emails.  
II. The portal team should increase notification capacity temporarily, clear the reset-email backlog in order, monitor delivery and keep the existing identity checks unchanged while the rate limit is corrected.

**Answer: B — Only II follows.**

**Explanation:** Identity checking is not causing the delay, so removing it creates a new security risk without fixing the real problem. Course II addresses the actual notification bottleneck while keeping the working security control in place.

## Q7 — COA-QL-009 — Hard

**Statement:** A cold-chain warehouse finds that one handheld scanner recorded inconsistent temperature tags on a group of outgoing medicine cartons. The cartons can be held, independent logger data exists, and unaffected dispatch lanes are operating normally.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The warehouse should hold the affected cartons, compare their scanner records with the independent logger data, release only cartons confirmed within limits and repair or replace the faulty scanner before that lane resumes normal use.  
II. The warehouse should continue unaffected dispatch lanes, mark the suspect cartons as held from loading, and use a manual double-check against the independent logger data until the faulty scanner has been tested and replaced.

**Answer: C — Both I and II follow.**

**Explanation:** Both courses protect the suspect cartons while keeping unaffected operations running. Course I gives the full verification-and-repair sequence. Course II provides a reasonable temporary control using independent temperature evidence until the scanner problem is resolved.

## Q8 — COA-QL-009 — Easy

**Statement:** A college receives several complaints that scholarship amounts were reduced after a rules update. The complaint records do not yet show whether the new rule was applied incorrectly, and scholarships for many other students are due this week.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The college should provisionally restore every complainant to the pre-update scholarship amount before checking individual records, and adjust the awards again later if the new rule is found to have been applied correctly.  
II. The college should hold all scholarship payments for this week's payment run until the complaints about the changed rule have been checked, so no student receives an amount that might later need correction.

**Answer: D — Neither I nor II follows.**

**Explanation:** Course I changes awards before establishing that the reductions were wrong. Course II blocks payments for many students whose cases are not under doubt. The correct approach is to isolate and verify the disputed cases without delaying unaffected scholarships.

## Q9 — COA-QL-009 — Hard

**Statement:** A payroll review finds unusually high overtime entries for one team. Some entries may be legitimate emergency work, the approval trail is available, and the rest of the payroll can be processed on time.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The payroll team should pause only the disputed overtime component, compare the entries with the approval trail, correct confirmed errors, release valid overtime and allow the rest of payroll to proceed on schedule.  
II. Management should issue misconduct notices to every supervisor in the team before reviewing the approvals because unusually high overtime itself shows that the supervisors allowed false claims.

**Answer: A — Only I follows.**

**Explanation:** Course I limits the hold to uncertain entries, checks the available evidence and keeps unaffected payroll on schedule. Course II treats an unusual pattern as proof of misconduct and punishes all supervisors before verification.

## Q10 — COA-QL-009 — Hard

**Statement:** A retailer learns that expiry labels may be wrong on one production batch of a packaged product. Batch records can identify affected stock, no defect has been reported in other batches, and retailers can isolate stock by lot number.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The company should ask every retailer to remove the product from shelves temporarily while it checks the lot records, even though the alert currently points to one identifiable production batch.  
II. The company should identify and isolate the suspect batch through the lot records, verify the label error, notify retailers handling that batch and keep unaffected batches on sale unless the investigation shows a wider problem.

**Answer: B — Only II follows.**

**Explanation:** Removing every batch first is broader than the current evidence requires because the suspect lot can already be identified. Course II contains the likely affected stock, checks the records and keeps unaffected batches available unless the evidence later shows a wider issue.

## Q11 — COA-QL-009 — Medium

**Statement:** Heavy rain has damaged several drain covers near a market entrance. The drains are still carrying water, pedestrians must keep access to the market, and replacement covers can be installed later the same day.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The civic team should immediately mark and barricade the damaged cover locations while keeping a safe pedestrian path open, then replace the covers when the repair crew arrives.  
II. The authority should inspect the nearby covers for the same rain-related damage while repairing the confirmed broken ones, but keep the inspection limited to the affected market area rather than closing unrelated streets.

**Answer: C — Both I and II follow.**

**Explanation:** Course I gives an immediate safety measure, keeps required access open and then completes the repair. Course II combines the known repair with a limited nearby preventive check. Both are targeted and proportionate.

## Q12 — COA-QL-009 — Easy

**Statement:** After a firmware update, a small group of smart meters begins showing unusually high readings. Raw meter logs are available, most meters are normal, and bills for the affected accounts are due in two days.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The utility should calculate the affected bills from the new meter readings but mark them as provisional, then verify the firmware logs and issue adjustments later if the readings are found to be wrong.  
II. The utility should postpone all smart-meter billing for the service area for one cycle and schedule replacement checks on every meter model, because the firmware issue may turn out to be wider than the small group currently showing abnormal readings.

**Answer: D — Neither I nor II follows.**

**Explanation:** Course I still creates customer charges before checking readings already known to be doubtful. Course II turns a limited anomaly into a service-area-wide billing delay and universal inspection without evidence that the problem is that broad. The affected group should be verified first while normal billing continues elsewhere.

## Human review checklist

Review CP007 for:

- whether every question genuinely requires more than one reasoning dimension;
- whether any question can be moved back to QL001–QL006 or QL008 because one rule alone decides it;
- whether hard questions use plausible near-misses rather than difficult English;
- whether evidence, proportionality, continuity, constraints and order are combined naturally rather than mechanically;
- whether `Both` questions contain two independently useful responses rather than duplicate plans;
- whether `Neither` questions fail for different meaningful reasons;
- whether explanations identify the decisive combined reasoning in simple language;
- whether Course I / Course II positions remain non-predictive.
