## Q1 — COA-QL-001 — Easy

**Statement:** Residents of a housing area have not received piped water since the main pumping motor failed this morning.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The water-supply department should send a repair team to restore the motor and arrange temporary supply until normal service resumes.  
II. The department should conduct a survey of household electricity use in the same area.

**Answer: A — Only I follows.**

**Explanation:** The failed motor is the stated cause of the water disruption, so repairing it and arranging temporary supply directly addresses the problem. Household electricity use is unrelated to the failed pump.

## Q2 — COA-QL-001 — Easy

**Statement:** Customers at a bank branch are receiving unreadable deposit receipts because the receipt printer has developed a mechanical fault.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The branch should suspend deposit-counter service until all office equipment, not only the receipt printer, has been inspected.  
II. The branch should repair or replace the faulty printer and provide clear replacement receipts where required.

**Answer: B — Only II follows.**

**Explanation:** The identified problem is the receipt printer. Repairing or replacing it directly solves the fault. Suspending deposit service for a broad equipment inspection is more disruptive than the stated problem requires.

## Q3 — COA-QL-001 — Medium

**Statement:** An examination registration portal repeatedly times out during the final two days of application because the current server capacity is insufficient for the traffic.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The examination body should add temporary server capacity and correct the bottleneck before the deadline.  
II. The examination body should provide a short deadline extension for applicants affected by the repeated portal failures.

**Answer: C — Both I and II follow.**

**Explanation:** Increasing server capacity tackles the known cause of the failure. A short extension also protects candidates who could not apply because the portal failed close to the deadline.

## Q4 — COA-QL-002 — Easy

**Statement:** Several minor reversing incidents have occurred inside a bus depot because warning alarms on some buses are not working.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. Until the faulty alarms are repaired, the depot should only instruct drivers to reverse more slowly and otherwise continue normal reversing operations.  
II. The depot should permanently prohibit all reversing movements, including by buses whose warning alarms are working correctly.

**Answer: D — Neither I nor II follows.**

**Explanation:** Driving more slowly does not correct the known warning-alarm failure and is too weak as the only response. A permanent ban on all reversing is excessive because the faulty buses can instead be identified and repaired.

## Q5 — COA-QL-002 — Medium

**Statement:** Packages waiting in an open loading area are repeatedly being damaged when sudden rain begins before they can be moved indoors.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The depot should create a covered holding area or use weatherproof covers for packages waiting to be loaded.  
II. The depot should organise a general motivational programme for all employees once every month.

**Answer: A — Only I follows.**

**Explanation:** Covering the waiting packages directly reduces the repeated rain damage. A general motivational programme does not address the actual failure in the loading process.

## Q6 — COA-QL-002 — Medium

**Statement:** Students entering a school laboratory without understanding basic safety rules have caused repeated avoidable equipment incidents.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The school should replace practical laboratory sessions with teacher demonstrations for all students for the rest of the term.  
II. The school should require a short safety briefing and appropriate supervision before students use the laboratory.

**Answer: B — Only II follows.**

**Explanation:** The issue is lack of safety understanding. Removing practical work for every student for the rest of the term goes beyond the targeted correction needed. A safety briefing and supervision directly address the stated risk.

## Q7 — COA-QL-003 — Medium

**Statement:** A customer reports that contact details on an account were changed without permission, but the branch has not yet established how the change occurred.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The bank should temporarily block further sensitive profile changes on the account until the customer's identity and request history are verified.  
II. The branch should examine the relevant audit trail and authorised request records before fixing responsibility for the change.

**Answer: C — Both I and II follow.**

**Explanation:** A temporary block limits further risk without deciding guilt. Checking the records is also necessary because the branch still does not know how the change occurred.

## Q8 — COA-QL-003 — Medium

**Statement:** An examination body detects an unusual similarity pattern in one centre's answer data, but the initial alert does not by itself prove misconduct.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The examination body should immediately cancel every candidate's result from the centre without further verification.  
II. The examination body should ignore the alert completely because misconduct has not yet been proved.

**Answer: D — Neither I nor II follows.**

**Explanation:** Cancelling all results before verification is premature and disproportionate. Ignoring a material warning sign is also unreasonable; the anomaly should first be investigated.

## Q9 — COA-QL-003 — Hard

**Statement:** Several expensive tools are missing from a workshop, while the available access records are incomplete and do not identify who removed them.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The organisation should reconcile the tool inventory and review available access or issue records before taking disciplinary action against any individual.  
II. The organisation should place every employee named in the incomplete access records under disciplinary suspension before checking the inventory and issue records further.

**Answer: A — Only I follows.**

**Explanation:** The loss is established but responsibility is not. Course I verifies the available evidence first. The incomplete records do not justify disciplinary suspension before further checking.

## Q10 — COA-QL-004 — Easy

**Statement:** Passenger queues at a railway station have become unusually long because one staffed ticket counter is closed during the evening rush.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The station manager should wait for a permanent staffing sanction from headquarters before making any temporary arrangement at the closed counter.  
II. The station administration should, where trained staff are available, reassign staff temporarily to reopen the counter during the evening rush.

**Answer: B — Only II follows.**

**Explanation:** A permanent staffing decision may be useful later, but waiting for it does not address the immediate queue. A temporary reassignment of available trained staff directly deals with the current problem.

## Q11 — COA-QL-004 — Medium

**Statement:** A school's online fee portal has accepted payments but is not issuing confirmations, causing some students to appear unpaid in the school system.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The school administration should reconcile the received payments with the payment provider and correct the affected student records.  
II. The school should give affected students a temporary acknowledgement so that they are not penalised while the records are being corrected.

**Answer: C — Both I and II follow.**

**Explanation:** Reconciliation corrects the main record problem. A temporary acknowledgement is also reasonable because it protects affected students while the correction is being completed.

## Q12 — COA-QL-004 — Medium

**Statement:** Visitors to a government office are repeatedly going to the wrong rooms after several departments were moved to different floors.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The office should issue a general notice that departments have shifted, without showing the new floors or room numbers.  
II. The office should leave the old directions unchanged and wait for regular visitors to learn the new locations over time.

**Answer: D — Neither I nor II follows.**

**Explanation:** A notice without the new locations does not give visitors enough information to find the departments. Leaving old directions in place and waiting for people to learn over time also fails to solve the current navigation problem.

## Q13 — COA-QL-005 — Medium

**Statement:** A service centre has one spare biometric device, one active device has failed, and no funds for new equipment are available until next month. The centre must remain open.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The centre should move the spare device to the failed counter and adjust appointments while the faulty unit is repaired.  
II. The centre should immediately purchase new biometric devices for every counter before reopening.

**Answer: A — Only I follows.**

**Explanation:** Using the available spare keeps the centre open and respects the resource limit. Buying new devices immediately contradicts the stated lack of funds and the requirement to remain open.

## Q14 — COA-QL-005 — Medium

**Statement:** A small bank branch has only two trained counter employees today and must keep at least one cash counter open throughout business hours.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The branch should assign both trained employees to a telephone-help desk until the queue at the branch disappears.  
II. The branch should keep one employee at the cash counter and use the other to organise tokens and handle non-cash queries when possible.

**Answer: B — Only II follows.**

**Explanation:** Assigning both employees elsewhere would violate the explicit requirement to keep a cash counter open. The second action respects that requirement while using the remaining employee to reduce pressure.

## Q15 — COA-QL-005 — Hard

**Statement:** One examination room becomes unusable shortly before a test. Two spare rooms are available, and the rule of no more than thirty candidates in any room must still be followed.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The centre should redistribute the affected candidates between the two spare rooms without exceeding the thirty-candidate limit.  
II. If seating preparation is not complete, the centre should briefly hold the affected group and start them after the spare rooms are ready while keeping the same allotted test duration.

**Answer: C — Both I and II follow.**

**Explanation:** Both actions work within the room-capacity rule. The first uses the available spare space, while the second allows a short controlled delay without reducing the candidates' test time.

## Q16 — COA-QL-006 — Easy

**Statement:** A few users repeatedly create noise in one reading room of a public library despite requests to remain quiet.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The library should ban every visitor from entering the building for one month.  
II. The library should permanently remove all chairs from the reading room so that nobody can stay there long enough to make noise.

**Answer: D — Neither I nor II follows.**

**Explanation:** The problem is caused by a few users in one reading room. Banning everyone or damaging the room's basic purpose is far more severe than the situation requires.

## Q17 — COA-QL-006 — Medium

**Statement:** One office floor is using unusually large amounts of paper because many routine documents are being printed unnecessarily.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The office should introduce sensible print controls such as duplex defaults, user-level monitoring and limits where repeated misuse is found.  
II. The organisation should immediately prohibit all printing in every office, including documents that must be issued on paper.

**Answer: A — Only I follows.**

**Explanation:** Targeted controls address unnecessary printing without blocking legitimate work. A complete organisation-wide ban is excessive and would interfere with documents that still need to be printed.

## Q18 — COA-QL-006 — Medium

**Statement:** Passengers have made several complaints that the sign for one bus stop is difficult to see from the road.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The transport authority should remove the poorly marked stop from the route instead of changing or repositioning its sign.  
II. The authority should replace or reposition the unclear sign and check nearby route signs for the same visibility problem.

**Answer: B — Only II follows.**

**Explanation:** The complaint is about visibility of the sign, not the need for the stop. Removing the stop is disproportionate. Correcting the sign is direct, and checking nearby signs is a reasonable preventive step.

## Q19 — COA-QL-007 — Medium

**Statement:** A service company has a growing backlog of approved customer refunds because requests remain waiting in one final processing queue for many days.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The company should review the refund backlog daily and temporarily add trained processing support until the queue returns to a normal level.  
II. The company should give customers a clear refund status and an escalation route for cases that remain overdue.

**Answer: C — Both I and II follow.**

**Explanation:** The first action addresses the processing bottleneck itself. The second is also useful because it gives affected customers a clear status and route for overdue cases while the backlog is cleared.

## Q20 — COA-QL-007 — Medium

## Q21 — COA-QL-008 — Hard

**Statement:** An online examination system flags possible duplicate registrations, but some matches may belong to different candidates with similar names and details.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The examination body should first verify the flagged records and then merge or cancel only registrations confirmed to be duplicates.  
II. The examination body should first cancel all flagged registrations and check later whether any belonged to different candidates.

**Answer: A — Only I follows.**

**Explanation:** Verification must come before an irreversible record change. Course I uses the correct order; Course II cancels records before establishing whether they are actually duplicates.

## Q22 — COA-QL-008 — Medium

**Statement:** A school bus develops a brake-system warning during its route, and another roadworthy bus is available nearby.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The operator should keep the bus in service and wait for its scheduled monthly inspection before checking the warning.  
II. The operator should stop the affected bus, transfer passengers safely to the available bus and inspect the warned vehicle before returning it to service.

**Answer: B — Only II follows.**

**Explanation:** A current brake warning requires immediate attention, so waiting for a later routine inspection is unsuitable. The second course removes the immediate risk, keeps service available and then checks the vehicle.

## Q23 — COA-QL-009 — Hard

**Statement:** A bank identifies duplicate service fees in one batch of transactions during month-end processing. Other branch services are working and must continue.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The bank should stop the affected batch, reverse confirmed duplicate fees and test the correction before processing that batch again.  
II. The branch should keep unaffected services open and provide a clear correction status to customers whose transactions are under review.

**Answer: C — Both I and II follow.**

**Explanation:** Course I isolates and corrects the faulty batch in a sensible order. Course II respects the requirement to keep other services running while giving affected customers useful information.

## Q24 — COA-QL-009 — Hard

**Statement:** A public office receives repeated complaints that service has become slower, but it has not yet established whether the cause is staffing, process delay or a technical problem.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The office should transfer all current counter staff to other sections and replace them before checking where the service delay is occurring.  
II. The office should purchase new computer terminals for every counter on the assumption that old hardware is causing the delay.

**Answer: D — Neither I nor II follows.**

**Explanation:** The cause has not been identified. Course I assumes the staff are responsible, while Course II assumes the hardware is responsible. Both commit to a major response before finding the actual bottleneck.
