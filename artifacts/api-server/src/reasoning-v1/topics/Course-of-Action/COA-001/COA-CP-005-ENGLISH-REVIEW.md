## Q1 — COA-QL-001 — Easy
**Profile:** `TWO_ACTION_FOUR_WAY_STANDARD`

**Statement:** Streetlights on one residential lane have stopped working after the local distribution box developed a fault, while lights on nearby lanes are functioning normally.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The maintenance team should inspect and repair the faulty distribution box and check the affected streetlights before restoring the circuit.  
II. The maintenance team should replace every streetlight bulb on the affected lane before testing the faulty distribution box.

**Options:**  
A. Only Course of Action I follows  
B. Only Course of Action II follows  
C. Both Courses of Action I and II follow  
D. Neither Course of Action I nor II follows

**Answer: A — Only Course of Action I follows.**

**Explanation:** The distribution-box fault is already identified. Repairing it directly addresses the failure. Replacing all bulbs first targets components not shown to be the cause and delays the direct repair.

## Q2 — COA-QL-001 — Medium
**Profile:** `TWO_ACTION_FOUR_WAY_NEITHER_BEFORE_BOTH`

**Statement:** The electronic token display in a public service hall has failed, but the counters and token-printing system are otherwise working normally.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The office should replace every computer used by all departments before resuming the token display.  
II. The office should add more chairs to the waiting hall and continue without any temporary method of calling token numbers.

**Options:**  
A. Only Course of Action I follows  
B. Only Course of Action II follows  
C. Neither Course of Action I nor II follows  
D. Both Courses of Action I and II follow

**Answer: C — Neither Course of Action I nor II follows.**

**Explanation:** The problem is limited to the display, so replacing every computer assumes a wider fault without evidence. Extra chairs may make waiting easier but do not tell visitors when their token is called.

## Q3 — COA-QL-002 — Medium
**Profile:** `TWO_ACTION_FOUR_WAY_BOTH_FIRST`

**Statement:** A roadside drain repeatedly overflows after rain because plastic waste collects at the same inlet grates and blocks the water flow.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. After each overflow, the local body should deploy a pump to remove water but make no change to the repeatedly blocked inlet grates.  
II. The local body should schedule cleaning of the affected grates before heavy rain and use suitable screens where they can reduce repeated plastic blockage.

**Options:**  
A. Both Courses of Action I and II follow  
B. Only Course of Action I follows  
C. Only Course of Action II follows  
D. Neither Course of Action I nor II follows

**Answer: C — Only Course of Action II follows.**

**Explanation:** Pumping water reacts after the overflow but leaves the known blockage unchanged. Planned cleaning and suitable screening directly reduce the recurring cause.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The bank should run short phishing-recognition exercises and teach staff how to verify unusual login requests before entering credentials.  
II. The bank should strengthen email filtering and warning controls for messages that imitate internal login requests.

**Options:**  
A. Only Course of Action I follows  
B. Only Course of Action II follows  
C. Neither Course of Action I nor II follows  
D. Both Courses of Action I and II follow

**Answer: D — Both Courses of Action I and II follow.**

## Q5 — COA-QL-003 — Hard
**Profile:** `TWO_ACTION_FOUR_WAY_STANDARD`

**Statement:** A company's expense system shows repeated claims from one employee for the same travel date, but some claims may be corrected resubmissions after earlier entries were rejected.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The company should compare the claim versions, receipts and approval history before deciding whether duplicate reimbursement was actually attempted.  
II. Until the review is complete, the company should pause payment only on the disputed claims rather than block all of the employee's unrelated approved reimbursements.

**Options:**  
A. Only Course of Action I follows  
B. Only Course of Action II follows  
C. Both Courses of Action I and II follow  
D. Neither Course of Action I nor II follows

**Answer: C — Both Courses of Action I and II follow.**

**Explanation:** The repeated entries may have a legitimate explanation, so the records should first be checked. A limited hold on only the disputed claims also protects against duplicate payment without unnecessarily blocking unrelated reimbursements.

## Q6 — COA-QL-003 — Medium
**Profile:** `TWO_ACTION_FOUR_WAY_BOTH_FIRST`

**Statement:** A website receives complaints that some accounts were accessed from unfamiliar devices, but the logs have not yet been checked to distinguish genuine compromise from normal device changes.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The service should permanently delete every reported account immediately so that no unauthorised person can continue using it.  
II. The service should take no action because an unfamiliar device can sometimes belong to the genuine account holder.

**Options:**  
A. Both Courses of Action I and II follow  
B. Only Course of Action I follows  
C. Only Course of Action II follows  
D. Neither Course of Action I nor II follows

**Answer: D — Neither Course of Action I nor II follows.**

**Explanation:** Deleting accounts before checking the logs is irreversible and excessive. But doing nothing is also unsuitable because the reports may indicate a real security problem. The access should first be verified and temporary safeguards used where needed.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The office administration should update the reception directory and place clear temporary signs showing the new room numbers until permanent signs are installed.  
II. The office should tell visitors to arrive one hour earlier so they have enough time to search for the correct rooms themselves.

**Options:**  
A. Only Course of Action I follows  
B. Only Course of Action II follows  
C. Neither Course of Action I nor II follows  
D. Both Courses of Action I and II follow

## Q11 — COA-QL-006 — Easy
**Profile:** `TWO_ACTION_FOUR_WAY_STANDARD`

**Statement:** Five chairs in one section of a college library have broken legs, while the rest of the library furniture and reading areas are safe and functioning normally.

**Courses of Action:**  
I. The library should remove the five damaged chairs from use and repair or replace those chairs before returning them to the reading area.  
II. The college should close the entire library for a month until every item of furniture in all reading areas has been replaced with new furniture.

**Options:**  
A. Only Course of Action I follows  
B. Only Course of Action II follows  
C. Both Courses of Action I and II follow  
D. Neither Course of Action I nor II follows

**Answer: A — Only Course of Action I follows.**

**Explanation:** The defect is limited to five chairs, so removing and repairing those items matches the scale of the problem. Closing the whole library and replacing all furniture is far beyond what the facts require.
