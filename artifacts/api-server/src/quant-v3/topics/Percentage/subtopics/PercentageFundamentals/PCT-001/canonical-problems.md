# PCT-001 Canonical Problems

## CP01: Find A Percentage Of A Quantity

Topology description:
The question asks for a fixed percentage of a given number or quantity. The answer is a value, not a percentage.

Representative examples:

- Find \(25\%\) of \(240\).
- What is \(12.5\%\) of \(400\)?
- Find \(40\%\) of \(750\).

Reasoning structure:

1. Read the percent and base quantity.
2. Convert the percent into a fraction or decimal.
3. Multiply by the base quantity.
4. Write the value with the required unit if given.

Merged candidates:

- Percentage of a number.
- Percentage of a quantity.
- Finding direct percent value.
- Clean fractional percentages such as \(12.5\%\), \(33\frac13\%\), and \(37.5\%\).

Rejected candidates:

- Reverse percentage where the percentage value is known but the base is unknown.
- Successive percentage value after two changes.
- Applied marks, votes, income, or population contexts.

## CP02: Express One Quantity As A Percentage Of Another

Topology description:
Two quantities are given, and one must be expressed as a percentage of the other. The answer is a percentage.

Representative examples:

- What percent of \(200\) is \(50\)?
- \(45\) is what percent of \(180\)?
- Express \(36\) as a percentage of \(240\).

Reasoning structure:

1. Identify the part quantity.
2. Identify the reference quantity.
3. Form \(\frac{\text{part}}{\text{reference}}\times100\).
4. Simplify to get the percentage.

Merged candidates:

- One number as a percent of another.
- Quantity-to-quantity percentage.
- Direct percent relation between two numbers.

Rejected candidates:

- Percentage more than or less than.
- Comparison of two separately computed percentages.
- Contextual expenditure or marks problems.

## CP03: Convert Fraction Or Decimal To Percentage

Topology description:
A fraction or decimal is given and must be converted into percentage form.

Representative examples:

- Convert \(\frac{3}{8}\) into percentage.
- Express \(0.45\) as a percentage.
- Convert \(\frac{7}{20}\) into percent form.

Reasoning structure:

1. Read the fraction or decimal.
2. Multiply by \(100\).
3. Simplify the result.
4. Attach the percent sign.

Merged candidates:

- Fraction to percentage.
- Decimal to percentage.
- Simple mixed exact conversion.

Rejected candidates:

- Percentage to fraction.
- Percentage to decimal.
- Approximation-only conversions.

## CP04: Compare Percentage Values

Topology description:
Two or more percentage statements must be compared. The student computes or normalizes each percentage value and then selects the greater, smaller, or correct order.

Representative examples:

- Which is greater: \(20\%\) of \(150\) or \(15\%\) of \(240\)?
- Arrange \(25\%\) of \(80\), \(20\%\) of \(120\), and \(10\%\) of \(300\) in increasing order.
- Identify the smaller value: \(30\%\) of \(90\) or \(18\%\) of \(150\).

Reasoning structure:

1. Compute each percentage value.
2. Compare the resulting values.
3. Choose the greatest, smallest, or required order.
4. Preserve the asked comparison direction.

Merged candidates:

- Greater percentage value.
- Smaller percentage value.
- Increasing order.
- Decreasing order.
- Greatest or least selection.

Rejected candidates:

- Percentage more than or less than.
- Ratio comparison without percentage calculation.
- Multi-step applied comparison.

## CP05: Direct Percentage Increase Or Decrease

Topology description:
A base value is directly increased or decreased by a given percentage once. The final value or change amount is asked.

Representative examples:

- Increase \(500\) by \(20\%\).
- Decrease \(720\) by \(25\%\).
- Find the new value when \(360\) is reduced by \(10\%\).

Reasoning structure:

1. Read the base value and percent change.
2. Find the percentage change amount.
3. Add it for increase or subtract it for decrease.
4. Write the final value or change amount asked.

Merged candidates:

- Direct increase.
- Direct decrease.
- Final value after one percentage change.
- Change amount after one percentage change.

Rejected candidates:

- Successive percentage changes.
- Reverse percentage after increase or decrease.
- Percentage more than or less than comparisons.
