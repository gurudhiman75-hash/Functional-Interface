# PCT-001 Difficulty Framework

## Difficulty Principle

Difficulty depends on the number of required operations, the cleanliness of the percent value, and whether comparison is required. Bigger numbers alone do not make a question harder.

## Easy

Expected reasoning length: 1-2 operations.

Allowed CPs:

- CP01
- CP02
- CP03
- CP05

Typical triggers:

- Percent is a common value such as \(10\%\), \(20\%\), \(25\%\), or \(50\%\).
- Denominator converts cleanly to \(100\).
- Increase or decrease uses a simple base value.

Representative examples:

- Find \(25\%\) of \(200\).
- What percent of \(100\) is \(40\)?
- Convert \(\frac14\) into percentage.
- Increase \(300\) by \(10\%\).

## Moderate

Expected reasoning length: 2-3 operations.

Allowed CPs:

- CP01
- CP02
- CP03
- CP04
- CP05

Typical triggers:

- Percent is \(12.5\%\), \(37.5\%\), or \(33\frac13\%\).
- Reference quantity is not \(100\).
- Two percentage values must be compared.
- Final value after increase or decrease is asked.

Representative examples:

- Find \(37.5\%\) of \(640\).
- \(42\) is what percent of \(168\)?
- Which is greater: \(20\%\) of \(150\) or \(15\%\) of \(240\)?
- Decrease \(960\) by \(12.5\%\).

## Hard

Expected reasoning length: 3-4 operations.

Allowed CPs:

- CP04
- CP05

Typical triggers:

- Three values must be compared.
- Correct increasing or decreasing order is asked.
- Direct percentage change uses a less immediate but still clean percentage.

Representative examples:

- Arrange \(25\%\) of \(80\), \(20\%\) of \(120\), and \(10\%\) of \(300\) in increasing order.
- Choose the least among \(12.5\%\) of \(640\), \(20\%\) of \(410\), and \(15\%\) of \(540\).
- Reduce \(1440\) by \(37.5\%\).

## Reject As Out Of Scope

- Reverse percentage.
- Successive percentage.
- Population, income, expenditure, votes, marks, replacement, and transfer contexts.
- Percentage more than or less than.
