# NS-SURD-001 Difficulty Framework

## Difficulty Principle

Difficulty is based on the number of required transformations and whether the student must choose a method before calculating. Bigger radicands alone do not make a problem harder.

## Easy

Expected reasoning length: 1-2 transformations.

Allowed CPs:

- CP01 Perfect-power extraction from a surd
- CP02 Like-surd addition and subtraction
- CP03 Surd multiplication and division simplification
- CP06 Monomial denominator rationalization
- CP08 Surd identity evaluation

Typical triggers:

- One radical is simplified.
- Like surds are already visible.
- Product or quotient has compatible radicals.
- Denominator has only one radical term.
- A conjugate product is visible and can be evaluated directly.

Representative examples:

- \(\sqrt{72}\)
- \(3\sqrt{2}+5\sqrt{2}\)
- \(\sqrt{3}\times\sqrt{12}\)
- \(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\)
- \((\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{3})(\sqrt{5}-\sqrt{3})\)

Reject as too trivial:

- \(\sqrt{49}\)
- questions asking only the definition of a surd
- questions asking only to name the conjugate

## Moderate

Expected reasoning length: 2-4 transformations.

Allowed CPs:

- CP01 Perfect-power extraction from a surd
- CP02 Like-surd addition and subtraction
- CP03 Surd multiplication and division simplification
- CP04 Mixed surd expression simplification
- CP05 Surd comparison by normalization
- CP07 Binomial denominator rationalization
- CP08 Surd identity evaluation

Typical triggers:

- Surds must be simplified before they become like terms.
- Product or quotient must be simplified before addition or subtraction.
- Comparison requires squaring or cubing positive expressions.
- A binomial denominator requires conjugate use.
- A square of a surd binomial requires the middle term.

Representative examples:

- \(\sqrt{50}+2\sqrt{8}\)
- \(\sqrt{6}\times\sqrt{24}-2\sqrt{9}\)
- compare \(2\sqrt{3}\) and \(3\sqrt{2}\)
- \(\frac{1}{2+\sqrt{3}}\)
- \((\sqrt{7}-\sqrt{5})^2\)

## Hard

Expected reasoning length: 4-6 transformations.

Allowed CPs:

- CP04 Mixed surd expression simplification
- CP05 Surd comparison by normalization
- CP07 Binomial denominator rationalization
- CP08 Surd identity evaluation

Typical triggers:

- Several surd terms must be simplified and then combined.
- Comparison involves more than two expressions.
- Rationalization creates a numerator that still needs simplification.
- The expression mixes rational terms and surd terms.
- Identity use is followed by additional simplification.

Representative examples:

- \(3\sqrt{12}-\sqrt{27}+\sqrt{75}\)
- arrange \(\sqrt{18}\), \(2\sqrt{5}\), and \(3\sqrt{2}\)
- \(\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}+\sqrt{3}}\)
- \((2+\sqrt{3})^2-(2-\sqrt{3})^2\)

Reject as out of Phase A:

- nested radical identities
- radical equations with multiple roots
- approximation-heavy ordering
- proof of irrationality

## Exam Profile Fit

SSC:

- Strong fit: CP01, CP02, CP04, CP06, CP07, CP08.
- SSC often uses simplification and rationalization.

Banking:

- Strong fit: CP01, CP03, CP05, CP08.
- Banking variants should be short and speed-friendly.

Railway:

- Strong fit: CP01, CP02, CP06, CP08.
- Prefer direct simplification with clean radicands.

CDS / NDA / CAPF:

- Strong fit: CP03, CP04, CP05, CP07, CP08.
- Can use comparison and binomial rationalization more often.

Punjab PCS / State PCS:

- Strong fit: CP01, CP02, CP04, CP06, CP08.
- Prefer traditional surd simplification and rationalization forms.
