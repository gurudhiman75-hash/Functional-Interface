# ENG-008 — Reading Comprehension — Coverage Plan V1

Status: `CP001_IMPLEMENTATION_IN_PROGRESS__REVIEW_ONLY`

## Official exam baseline

ENG-008 exists because the official SSC English syllabus explicitly includes **Comprehension Passage**.

The 2026 SSC CGL notice states that comprehension may use **three or more paragraphs** and that at least one passage should be a simple book/story-style passage while other passages may be current-affairs report/editorial material. The 2025 SSC CHSL notice also explicitly lists Comprehension Passage in Tier-I English.

This chapter therefore models passage-based comprehension as its own content system. It is not treated as an extension of ENG-003 grammar fillers or ENG-004 vocabulary.

## Planned checkpoints

- **CP001 — Narrative / story-style direct comprehension**
  - explicit facts
  - sequence
  - stated reason
  - stated outcome
  - locating supporting detail
- **CP002 — Report / informational direct comprehension**
  - facts
  - data stated in prose
  - cause/result stated in the passage
  - comparison explicitly stated
- **CP003 — Main idea / central theme / suitable title**
- **CP004 — Inference and conclusion**
- **CP005 — Vocabulary in context**
- **CP006 — Reference / pronoun / phrase reference**
- **CP007 — Tone, purpose and author stance**
- **CP008 — Mixed multi-paragraph comprehension sets**
  - combines direct, inference, main idea, vocabulary, reference and tone/purpose questions
  - includes story-style and report/editorial-style passages

Checkpoint count is a coverage plan, not a quota. Additional work is justified only by an exam-relevant gap found during review/audit.

## Passage rules

- passages must be original Examtree-authored text or otherwise safe licensed/public-domain material;
- no copying of copyrighted newspaper/editorial passages;
- each passage must be coherent on its own and must not require outside knowledge;
- all keyed answers must be defensible from the passage alone;
- distractors must be plausible but contradicted, unsupported or overly broad/narrow;
- avoid trick questions based on tiny wording differences;
- passage difficulty must reflect vocabulary, syntactic density, information load and inference burden rather than length alone;
- story passages should remain natural rather than sounding like grammar exercises;
- report/editorial-style passages should remain neutral and factual unless the question is explicitly testing tone/stance;
- explanations must point to the relevant passage evidence in simple language.
