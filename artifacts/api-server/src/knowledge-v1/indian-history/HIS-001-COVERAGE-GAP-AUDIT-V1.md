# HIS-001 Indian History — Coverage Gap Audit V1

Status: **V2 EXPANSION REQUIRED — FINAL CHAPTER CLOSURE BLOCKED**  
Baseline: **HIS-001 English V1 + multilingual CP001–CP016**  
Frozen V1 core: **954 questions / 899 canonical facts / 2,862 EN-HI-PA learner surfaces**  
Runtime lifecycle: **unchanged / review-only**

## 1. Audit question

The existing History audits prove that the material already implemented is internally strong: facts are used, difficulty gates pass, explanations meet depth rules, and multilingual parity is controlled.

They do **not** answer a different question:

> Does the current corpus cover the major exam-relevant Indian History domains deeply enough that the chapter can be considered saturated?

This audit addresses that coverage question.

## 2. Reference frame

The gap review uses three layers:

1. the frozen HIS-001 CP001–CP016 scopes and canonical fact libraries;
2. the broader NIOS History 315 Indian-history sequence, especially lessons that are not represented by a dedicated HIS-001 checkpoint;
3. SSC-style General Awareness scope, where History and Culture are explicitly part of the knowledge domain.

Primary official reference map:
- NIOS History 315 index: https://digital.nios.ac.in/topic.php?id=315en
- SSC syllabus/notifications: https://ssc.gov.in/

World History remains deliberately outside HIS-001 V1 and is **not** counted as an Indian-History defect in this audit.

## 3. Overall verdict

**954 questions are a strong V1 core, but not a saturation-complete Indian History engine.**

The present package is strongest in:
- major dynastic/political chronology;
- Vedic, Buddhist/Jain, Mauryan and Gupta foundations;
- Sultanate, Vijayanagara/Bahmani, Mughal and eighteenth-century political history;
- Company expansion and colonial economic policy;
- 1857 and selected reform movements;
- the mainstream national movement from 1885 to 1947.

The largest weakness is not poor questions. It is **missing content families** that never entered the canonical fact library.

A final chapter freeze should therefore be postponed until the high-priority gaps below are implemented and audited.

## 5. Confirmed high-priority holes

The following are not merely low-frequency details; they represent whole examinable content families that are missing or materially thin:

### A. Medieval culture

NIOS History 315 Lesson 14 explicitly covers:
- Sufi orders and practices;
- Bhakti traditions and saints;
- rise of Sikhism;
- Persian, Urdu and regional literary growth;
- Sultanate/Mughal and regional architecture;
- painting;
- music and performing traditions.

HIS-001 currently has no dedicated checkpoint for this domain. Political history cannot substitute for it.

### B. Popular resistance to Company rule

NIOS History 315 Lesson 19 covers peasant and tribal resistance beyond the Revolt of 1857, including families such as:
- Sanyasi/Fakir resistance;
- Rangpur peasant resistance;
- Poligar resistance;
- Mappila resistance;
- Bhil/Kol resistance;
- Santhal resistance;
- other regional peasant/tribal challenges.

The current HIS-001 package jumps from Company expansion/economic policy into 1857 and reform, leaving this domain substantially uncovered.

### C. Colonial education and press

High-frequency factual relationships around:
- Macaulay's Minute;
- Wood's Despatch;
- Hunter Commission;
- press restrictions and the Vernacular Press Act;
- Arms Act;
- education/public-sphere institutional change

need their own canonical coverage rather than appearing, if at all, as distractors.

### D. National-movement supplementation

The current 1885–1947 sequence is strong but should be expanded for exam saturation. Candidate additions include:
- Kakori;
- additional revolutionary networks and associations;
- Haripura and Tripuri sessions;
- Wavell Plan and Simla Conference;
- Communal Award/Poona Pact sequence where source-backed;
- tighter Congress-session/president/resolution mapping;
- selected INA-trial and transfer-of-power associations.

These should be sourced and filtered for exam value before implementation.

## 7. Question-archetype gaps to add

New facts should not become 480 direct-recall rewrites. V2 should intentionally add:

- ruler/event ↔ date/region matching;
- source/traveller ↔ ruler/place association;
- text/author ↔ period association;
- monument/style ↔ dynasty/place;
- act ↔ provision;
- movement ↔ leader/region/cause;
- session ↔ president/resolution;
- chronology clusters;
- cause ↔ consequence;
- two- and three-statement reasoning;
- correctly matched pair/set;
- comparison between closely confusable institutions or policies.

Hard questions must remain hard through synthesis, not obscure trivia.

## 8. Expansion quality rules

Every new V2 checkpoint must:

- use authoritative source-backed canonical facts;
- preserve the existing fact-first architecture;
- avoid disputed claims unless carefully qualified;
- use same-class distractors;
- keep stems concise and natural;
- use simple, useful explanations;
- maintain Easy/Medium/Hard separation;
- avoid duplicating an existing V1 fact unless a genuinely new learner task needs cross-fact synthesis;
- create Hindi and Punjabi only after English approval;
- remain review-only until explicit approval.

## 9. What is not a gap

The following should not trigger uncontrolled expansion:

- obscure ruler lists with little exam value;
- unsupported “first/largest/oldest” trivia;
- exact army-size or casualty trivia;
- local micro-history without recurring competitive-exam value;
- alternate spellings treated as new facts;
- paraphrases of existing questions;
- World History, which remains separately scoped.

## 10. Closure gate

History must **not** be marked final/closed merely because CP001–CP016 are complete.

Final closure requires:

1. implement approved V2 gap checkpoints;
2. English review and approval;
3. run duplicate/fact-integrity/difficulty audits across V1 + V2;
4. run a second coverage-gap audit to verify that no major content family remains thin;
5. complete Hindi/Punjabi localization and parity audit;
6. create a new chapter freeze authority;
7. only then perform final Question Studio/runtime lifecycle integration.

## 11. Audit conclusion

**Decision: EXPAND BEFORE FINAL FREEZE.**

The 954-question corpus remains a valuable and stable V1 core. The next work should focus on missing content families, not on rewriting already-approved questions.
