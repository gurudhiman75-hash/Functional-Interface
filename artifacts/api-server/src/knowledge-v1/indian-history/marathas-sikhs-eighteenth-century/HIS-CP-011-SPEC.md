# HIS-CP-011 — Marathas, Sikhs & Eighteenth-Century India

## Scope
Review-only History checkpoint covering the political transition after Aurangzeb, the rise of Maratha and Sikh power, major eighteenth-century regional states, and the invasions that reshaped north Indian politics.

Primary coverage:
- weakening of Mughal central authority after 1707
- Shahu, Balaji Vishwanath and Baji Rao I
- chauth, sardeshmukhi and the Maratha Confederacy
- Bhonsles, Gaikwads, Holkars and Sindhias
- Sikh political consolidation in Punjab
- Hyderabad, Bengal, Awadh, Jaipur and Bharatpur
- Nadir Shah, Ahmad Shah Abdali and the Third Battle of Panipat
- political and administrative continuities between Mughal and regional states

## Historical hygiene
- Do not reduce the decline of Mughal authority to one ruler or one cause.
- Do not describe the Maratha Confederacy as a tightly centralised unitary state.
- Distinguish chauth and sardeshmukhi without oversimplified invented percentages unless directly needed and source-supported.
- Treat the Third Battle of Panipat as a major setback to Maratha northward expansion, not the instant disappearance of Maratha power.
- Do not portray eighteenth-century regional politics as a simple religious conflict.
- Keep detailed Company conquest, Plassey, Buxar and Anglo-Maratha wars for the next CP except where a boundary reference is unavoidable.
- Keep detailed art and culture outside this CP.
