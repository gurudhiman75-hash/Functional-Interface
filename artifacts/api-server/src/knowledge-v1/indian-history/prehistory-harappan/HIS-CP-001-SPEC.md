## 1. Objective

Establish the Indian History `knowledge-v1` pattern with a source-controlled first checkpoint covering high-value prehistoric foundations and the Harappan Civilization.

This checkpoint must prove:

- source-backed canonical facts rather than an MCQ answer bank;
- natural SSC/Railway/Banking/state-exam wording;
- same-class distractors;
- deterministic answer-position balancing;
- distinct learner tasks rather than superficial stem rewrites;
- concise beginner-friendly explanations;
- explicit handling of archaeological uncertainty;
- review-only lifecycle before Question Studio promotion.

## 2. Qualified scope

### A. Prehistory

- Palaeolithic, Mesolithic and Neolithic sequence;
- characteristic stone-tool traditions;
- cultivation, animal domestication and settled life in the Neolithic;
- Bhimbetka rock shelters and paintings;
- selected standard prehistoric sites: Langhnaj, Chopani Mando and Sanganakallu.

### B. Harappan Civilization

- Harappa and Mohenjo-daro discovery/excavation;
- Early, Mature and Late Harappan sequence;
- Mature Harappan chronology at school-exam precision;
- undeciphered script;
- major sites and present-day locations;
- Great Bath, Lothal dockyard identification, Kalibangan plough marks, Banawali plough model;
- Dholavira water management;
- Dancing Girl, woven cloth, bead making, crop evidence and selected metal/craft facts.

## 4. Source authority

Canonical review facts are grounded in:

- NIOS History 315 Lesson 2 for prehistoric periods, tools and selected sites;
- NIOS History 315 Lesson 3 for Harappan discovery, chronology, sites and material culture;
- NCERT Themes in Indian History I as the principal textbook cross-check for Harappan terminology and interpretation;
- UNESCO World Heritage Centre for Bhimbetka and Dholavira.

Every generated review item carries `sourceFactIds` and `sourceIds`.

## 5. Archaeological caution rules

The engine must not turn interpretation into certainty.

Do not generate unqualified questions on:

- the identity of the Harappan people;
- a single claimed cause of Harappan decline;
- unproven readings of the Harappan script;
- religious interpretations presented as certain fact;
- disputed river identifications;
- changing claims such as the single “largest”, “oldest” or “first” site unless the source and comparison scope are explicit.

For Lothal, learner-facing wording should say a structure was **identified/traditionally identified as a dockyard**, rather than treating every archaeological interpretation as beyond dispute.

## 6. Difficulty policy

Difficulty must come from reasoning structure and confusion density, not obscure trivia.

- **Easy:** direct, high-frequency relation recall.
- **Medium:** site/location/find confusion sets and material-culture relations.
- **Hard:** chronology and multi-statement composition across independently sourced facts.

## 7. Distractor policy

Distractors must stay within the same semantic class wherever possible:

- tool ↔ tool;
- period ↔ period;
- site ↔ site;
- state/location ↔ state/location;
- archaeologist ↔ archaeologist;
- metal/material ↔ metal/material;
- site-feature pair ↔ site-feature pair.

No random unrelated option padding.

## 8. Explanation policy

Explanations should:

1. state the correct relationship;
2. add one useful clarifying sentence where needed;
3. remain short and beginner-friendly;
4. avoid option-by-option commentary unless the question requires comparison.
