## Scope

CP015 covers Maharaja Ranjit Singh and the consolidation of the Sikh Empire: Sukerchakia background, Lahore and Amritsar, coronation, Treaty of Amritsar (1809), major territorial expansion, central/provincial administration, leading officials and army modernisation.

CP014 owns the Sikh Misl confederacy. CP016 owns the First and Second Anglo-Sikh Wars and British annexation. CP015 ends with Ranjit Singh's reign and death in 1839 and must not drift into the post-1839 succession crisis or Anglo-Sikh Wars.

## Qualified coverage

- Maharaja Ranjit Singh was born in 1780 at Gujranwala; his father was Mahan Singh of the Sukerchakia Misl.
- He succeeded to the Sukerchakia leadership and captured Lahore in 1799, making Lahore the capital of his kingdom.
- His coronation took place at Lahore on 12 April 1801; Sahib Singh Bedi conducted the ceremony.
- His government was called Sarkar-i-Khalsa in Punjab school-history treatment; coins were issued in the names of Guru Nanak Dev and Guru Gobind Singh.
- Ranjit Singh gained full control of Amritsar by 1802 and strengthened Gobindgarh Fort.
- The Treaty of Amritsar was concluded in 1809 with the British East India Company; Charles Metcalfe represented the British. It checked further Sikh expansion south of the Sutlej.
- Kangra came under Ranjit Singh in 1809.
- Attock was acquired in 1813.
- Ranjit Singh obtained the Koh-i-Noor from Shah Shuja in 1813.
- Multan was conquered in 1818; Misr Diwan Chand was a leading commander in the campaign.
- Kashmir was annexed in 1819.
- Peshawar was conquered in 1834 in the standard Punjab-board chronology; Hari Singh Nalwa later served as its governor.
- Lahore was the capital of the Sikh Empire.
- The empire was divided into four major provinces: Lahore, Multan, Kashmir and Peshawar.
- Raja Dhian Singh served as Prime Minister; Fakir Aziz-ud-Din as Foreign Minister; Diwan Bhiwani Das as Finance Minister in Punjab-board treatment; Hari Singh Nalwa is listed as Commander-in-Chief in the same review tradition.
- Imam Baksh served as Kotwal of Lahore; Khushal Singh held the office of Deorhiwala.
- A province (Suba) was headed by a Nazim; Mauza was the smallest administrative unit and its village head was the Muqaddam.
- Ranjit Singh modernised the army with European officers. Ventura, Allard and Avitabile were among the important foreign officers; Ventura commanded the Fauj-i-Khas, a model brigade trained on European lines.
- Ranjit Singh died in 1839.
