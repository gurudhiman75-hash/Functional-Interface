## Scope

CP010 begins the history wave. It covers Harappan sites in present-day Punjab, Vedic/ancient names for the Punjab region, Taxila and the north-western historical Punjab context, Alexander and Porus, the Beas limit of Alexander's advance, Mauryan-era relations, and Kushan/Sanghol archaeology.

Historical Punjab and present-day Indian Punjab must never be treated as the same geographic scope.

## Learner-facing rules

- Stems must be short, direct and recruitment-exam grade.

- Do not cite source names, departments, reports or websites in learner text.

## Qualified coverage

- Rupnagar/Ropar is an Indus/Harappan archaeological site in present-day Punjab.
- Sanghol in Fatehgarh Sahib has a long archaeological sequence from late Harappan levels through later periods and contains major Buddhist/Kushan remains.
- Rohira is in Sangrur; Sunet is in Ludhiana; Kotla Nihang Khan is in Rupnagar district and all are important ancient/Harappan references in Punjab GK.
- Vedic Punjab is referred to as `Sapta Sindhu`; `Panchnad` is another ancient name, while Greek writers used `Pentapotamia` for the land of five rivers.
- Taxila was a major ancient city of the north-western Punjab region; it is in present-day Pakistan.
- Alexander entered the Punjab region in 326 BCE.
- Porus resisted Alexander at the Hydaspes, the modern Jhelum River.
- Alexander's army refused to advance beyond the Hyphasis, generally identified with the Beas.
- Chandragupta Maurya established the Mauryan Empire around 321 BCE with the help of Chanakya/Kautilya.
- Chanakya/Kautilya was connected with Taxila as a teacher in standard school-level Punjab history treatment.
- `Arthashastra` is attributed to Kautilya/Chanakya; `Indica` was written by Megasthenes.
- Kanishka was the most famous Kushan ruler in standard school-level treatment.
- Sanghol became an important Buddhist and artistic centre in the Kushan period; its sculptures are strongly connected with the Mathura school of art.
