# PGK-001 CP020 — Census & Demography

Status: REVIEW CANDIDATE V1
Chapter: `PGK-001 — Punjab General Knowledge`
CP: `PGK-001-CP-020 — Census & Demography`
Permanent QLs: `PGK-001-QL-133` to `PGK-001-QL-139`

## Scope

CP020 covers Punjab demographic facts from the Census 2011 statistical snapshot. It includes population, density, decadal growth, sex ratio, child sex ratio, literacy, rural-urban composition, Scheduled Caste population and selected district contrasts.

All numeric demographic facts are explicitly source-versioned to Census 2011. They must not be presented as current 2026 population estimates.

## Learner-facing rules

- Use direct Punjab recruitment-exam stems.
- Explanations should normally contain two short, useful sentences.
- Where a numerical fact is asked, include `Census 2011` in the stem or explanation unless the QL context makes the snapshot unmistakable.
- Keep learner text source-free; source names stay internal.
- Avoid filler such as `associated with`, `linked with`, `known for`, `formed the framework`.
- Do not mix current district boundaries with the 20-district Census 2011 geography.
- Prefer rounded percentage questions when exact decimals add no exam value.
- Hard questions should test comparison, rural-urban contrast, chronology/growth or district-indicator relations rather than obscure counts.

## Qualified coverage

- Census 2011 population of Punjab: 27,743,338 persons; males 14,639,465 and females 13,103,873.
- Decadal population growth during 2001-2011: 13.89%.
- Population density: 551 persons per sq km in the Census 2011 tables.
- Overall sex ratio: 895 females per 1000 males; rural 907 and urban 875.
- Child sex ratio (0-6 years): 846; rural 844 and urban 852.
- Total literacy rate: 75.84%; male 80.44%; female 70.73%.
- Rural literacy: 71.42%; urban literacy: 83.18%.
- Rural population: 17,344,192 (62.52%); urban population: 10,399,146 (37.48%).
- Scheduled Caste population: 8,860,179, or 31.94% of Punjab's population.
- Within the 20-district Census 2011 geography, Ludhiana was the most populous district and Barnala the least populous.
- Hoshiarpur had the highest district sex ratio (961) and Bathinda the lowest (868) in the Census 2011 district tables.
- Hoshiarpur had the highest district literacy rate (about 84.6%) and Mansa the lowest (about 61.8%).
- Ludhiana had the highest population density (978 persons per sq km) and Muktsar the lowest (348 persons per sq km) among the Census 2011 districts.
- Literacy rate is calculated for population aged 7 years and above; children below 7 are not included in the effective literacy rate denominator.

## District-boundary guard

District questions in CP020 use Census 2011's Punjab district geography (20 districts). Pathankot, Fazilka and later-created districts must not be inserted into rank questions unless figures are explicitly recast to a common Census 2011 basis and separately qualified.

## Source authority

Internal verification prioritises Office of the Registrar General & Census Commissioner, India Census 2011 tables and District Census Handbooks, followed by Punjab Government statistical/economic-survey tables that cite the Directorate of Census Operations, Punjab.
