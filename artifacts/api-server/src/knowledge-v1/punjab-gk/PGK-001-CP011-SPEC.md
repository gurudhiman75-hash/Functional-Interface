## Scope

CP011 covers the main political and urban-history relations of medieval Punjab before the Sikh-Guru-specific layer: Ghaznavid/Ghurid Punjab, Lahore under the early Delhi Sultanate, Mongol pressure on Lahore, Lodi-Babur transition, Mughal Lahore, major Lahore monuments and medieval documentary use of the name Punjab.

CP012 owns Sikh Gurus and Guru-Mughal relations. CP011 must not duplicate that content.

## Qualified coverage

- Mahmud of Ghazni's campaigns brought much of northern Punjab into the Ghaznavid sphere; Lahore became a major Ghaznavid centre.
- Muhammad Ghori captured Lahore in 1186, ending Ghaznavid rule there; Khusrau Malik was the last Ghaznavid ruler of Lahore.
- Qutb-ud-din Aibak established independent rule in 1206, with Lahore as an early centre; he died at Lahore in 1210 after a polo/chaugan accident.
- Lahore was sacked by Mongols in 1241 during the Delhi Sultanate period.
- Bahlul Lodi founded the Lodi dynasty; Ibrahim Lodi was defeated by Babur at the First Battle of Panipat in 1526.
- Daulat Khan Lodi was governor of Punjab during the final Lodi phase and played a role in Babur's entry into north India.
- Babur captured Lahore during his campaigns before the establishment of Mughal rule.
- Lahore became a major Mughal political, commercial and cultural centre.
- Akbar held his court at Lahore from 1584 to 1598 and strengthened/rebuilt Lahore Fort and the city's defences.
- Jahangir's tomb and Nur Jahan's tomb are at Shahdara, Lahore.
- The Shalimar Gardens at Lahore belong to the reign of Shah Jahan.
- The Badshahi Mosque at Lahore was built during Aurangzeb's reign.
- The word `Punjab` is documented in the writings of Ibn Battuta in the fourteenth century and became more common in sixteenth-century records.
- `Tarikh-e-Sher Shah Suri` and `Ain-i-Akbari` are important later medieval/early modern textual references for the term Punjab.
