## Scope

CP007 covers Punjab's major forest formations, Shivalik/Bir/Mand forest geography, important wildlife sanctuaries, major Ramsar wetlands, protected-area categories and stable wetland–river/location relations.

## Qualified coverage

- Punjab forests are mainly tropical/sub-tropical, with major concentrations in the Shivalik belt and additional Bir and Mand forest tracts.
- Chir pine forests occur mainly in Pathankot and parts of Gurdaspur/Hoshiarpur; bamboo and scrub formations also occur in the Shivaliks.
- Bir forests are strongly associated with Patiala and adjoining plains; Mand forests occur around wetland/riverine tracts including Amritsar, Tarn Taran and Kapurthala.
- Important wildlife sanctuaries include Abohar, Harike, Nangal, Takhni-Rehampur, Jhajjar-Bacholi, Kathlaur-Kaushlian and the Patiala Bir sanctuaries.
- Stable Ramsar/protected-area relations include Harike, Kanjli, Ropar, Beas Conservation Reserve, Keshopur-Miani Community Reserve and Nangal Wildlife Sanctuary.
- Harike lies at the Beas–Sutlej confluence.
- Kanjli Wetland is on Kali Bein in Kapurthala district.
- Ropar Wetland is associated with the Sutlej at Rupnagar.
- Beas Conservation Reserve is a long protected stretch of the Beas River and supports the Indus river dolphin.
- Keshopur-Miani is a Community Reserve in Gurdaspur district.
- Nangal Wildlife Sanctuary is associated with the Bhakra-Nangal reservoir system in the Shivalik foothills.
