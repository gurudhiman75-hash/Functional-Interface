## Scope

CP013 covers Banda Singh Bahadur as early eighteenth-century Punjab history: his earlier names, meeting with Guru Gobind Singh at Nanded, mission to Punjab, first campaigns, Samana/Sadhaura, Chappar Chiri and Sirhind, Lohgarh, coinage and agrarian measures, Mughal counter-offensive, Gurdas Nangal, capture and execution.

## Qualified coverage

- Banda Singh Bahadur's childhood name is given as Lachhman Dev in Punjab school-history treatment; as an ascetic he was known as Madho Das.
- Guru Gobind Singh met Madho Das at Nanded in 1708 and sent him toward Punjab to lead the struggle against Mughal provincial power.
- Sonipat was among his early campaign points in 1709.
- Banda Singh Bahadur attacked Samana in 1709; Samana became one of his earliest important victories.
- Sadhaura was ruled by Usman Khan in this period and fell during Banda Singh Bahadur's early campaign.
- Kapuri was another early campaign target; Qadam-ud-Din is named as its ruler in Punjab school-history material.
- Wazir Khan was the governor of Sirhind.
- Banda Singh Bahadur defeated Wazir Khan's forces at Chappar Chiri in 1710; Wazir Khan was killed and Sirhind was captured.
- Lohgarh became Banda Singh Bahadur's capital.
- Banda Singh Bahadur issued early Sikh coinage; Punjab school-history material treats him as the first issuer of coins of the Sikh Panth.
- He abolished the zamindari system in areas under his control and recognised proprietary rights of cultivators/tillers.
- Gurdas Nangal was the site of his final major siege/capture in 1715.
- Banda Singh Bahadur and his companions were taken to Delhi after capture.
- Banda Singh Bahadur was executed in Delhi in 1716 during the reign of Farrukh Siyar.
- His son is named Ajay Singh in Punjab school-history material.
