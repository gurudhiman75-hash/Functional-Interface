## Scope

CP006 covers Punjab's broad seasonal climate, rainfall and temperature pattern, major alluvial-soil relations, old/new alluvium, south-western alkaline/brackish conditions, soil and groundwater stress, and basic soil-water conservation relations.

Forests, wildlife, wetlands and biodiversity belong to CP007. Agriculture and crop systems belong to CP008, so CP006 uses crops only when needed to explain a natural-resource relation.

## Qualified coverage

- Summer: roughly mid-April to end-June.
- Rainy/monsoon season: roughly early July to end-September.
- Winter begins around October and becomes distinctly cold from December.
- Rainfall is heavier toward the Shivalik/Himalayan foothill side and generally decreases away from the hills toward the drier south-west.
- Punjab experiences marked summer heat and winter cold.
- The broad plains are predominantly alluvial.
- Bangar: older alluvium away from active floodplains.
- Khadar: newer alluvium in low-lying river floodplains; in Punjab such floodplain tracts are also known by names such as Bet/Bela in local usage.
- South-western Punjab includes areas with alkaline soils and brackish/saline sub-soil water.
- Important soil-water problems include erosion, salt-affected soils, waterlogging in some tracts and excessive groundwater withdrawal in intensively cultivated areas.
- Soil-water conservation includes land levelling, drainage, rainwater harvesting, watershed treatment and efficient irrigation measures.
