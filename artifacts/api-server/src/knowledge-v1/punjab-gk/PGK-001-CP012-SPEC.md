## Scope

CP012 covers Sikh-Guru history as Punjab exam GK: the ten Gurus in correct succession, major Punjab places and institutions, Guru-period developments, Adi Granth/Harmandir Sahib, Akal Takht, selected Guru-Mughal relations, Guru Tegh Bahadur's martyrdom, Guru Gobind Singh and the creation of the Khalsa.

This CP is historical GK, not theology. CP013 begins Banda Singh Bahadur and the post-Guru military-political struggle.

## Learner-facing rules

- Use short, direct recruitment-exam stems.
- Do not use filler such as `associated with`, `linked with`, `linked historically with`, `known for`, `closely associated with` or `closely related to`.
- Do not cite SGPC, PSEB, departments, websites or source names in learner text.
- Keep explanations natural and normally 1–2 sentences.
- Avoid devotional interpretation, doctrinal testing and sectarian framing.
- Avoid disputed anecdotes where a stable institutional/date/place fact is available.
- Difficulty should come from succession, institution-place, chronology and multi-fact relations.

## Qualified coverage

- Ten-Guru succession from Guru Nanak Dev to Guru Gobind Singh.
- Guru Nanak Dev: Talwandi/Nankana Sahib; Kartarpur as a major later centre of his life.
- Guru Angad Dev: Khadur Sahib; development/standardisation and promotion of Gurmukhi in standard Punjab school-history treatment.
- Guru Amar Das: Goindwal; Manji system; Baoli at Goindwal.
- Guru Ram Das: foundation/development of Ramdaspur, later Amritsar; Masand system in standard school-history treatment.
- Guru Arjan Dev: completion of Harmandir Sahib, compilation of the Adi Granth, Baba Buddha as first granthi, martyrdom in 1606.
- Guru Hargobind: Akal Takht; Miri-Piri; imprisonment/release from Gwalior in standard historical treatment.
- Guru Har Rai: Kiratpur as an important centre.
- Guru Har Krishan: eighth Guru; Delhi/Bangla Sahib historical setting; death in Delhi in 1664.
- Guru Tegh Bahadur: ninth Guru; martyrdom at Delhi in 1675; Anandpur/Chak Nanki historical relation.
- Guru Gobind Singh: born at Patna in 1666; tenth Guru; creation of the Khalsa at Anandpur Sahib in 1699; Panj Pyare; Guru Granth Sahib as the continuing Guru after 1708 in standard historical treatment.
