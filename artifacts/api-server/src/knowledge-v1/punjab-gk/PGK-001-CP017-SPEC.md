## Scope

CP017 covers important reform and anti-colonial movements in Punjab from the later nineteenth century to the pre-independence period. It includes the Namdhari/Kuka movement, Singh Sabha movement, Pagri Sambhal Jatta agitation, Ghadar movement, Jallianwala Bagh and related Punjab protest, Gurdwara Reform/Babbar Akali strands, and chronology/synthesis.

CP016 owns the Anglo-Sikh Wars and annexation. CP018 owns Partition, PEPSU and post-independence reorganisation.

## Qualified coverage

- Baba Ram Singh led the Namdhari/Kuka movement; Bhaini Sahib in Ludhiana district became its important centre.
- The Kuka/Namdhari movement used boycott and non-cooperation methods against British institutions and goods.
- The Malerkotla Kuka outbreak occurred in January 1872; Baba Ram Singh was deported to Rangoon.
- Singh Sabha reform activity began in Amritsar in 1873 and expanded through later organisations, including Lahore.
- The 1907 Pagri Sambhal Jatta agitation was a Punjab peasant movement led prominently by Ajit Singh; Lala Lajpat Rai also became a major figure in the agitation. Ajit Singh and Lajpat Rai were deported to Mandalay in 1907.
- The Ghadar movement emerged among Indian emigrants in North America in 1913. Sohan Singh Bhakna served as president of the organisation; Yugantar Ashram in San Francisco became its headquarters.
- The Ghadar newspaper began publication in 1913; Kartar Singh Sarabha became one of its prominent young Punjab revolutionaries.
- A planned uprising in India in February 1915 was disrupted; many Ghadar activists were tried in Lahore Conspiracy Cases.
- The Jallianwala Bagh massacre occurred at Amritsar on 13 April 1919 after unrest connected with the Rowlatt Act and the arrest/deportation of local leaders Saifuddin Kitchlew and Dr Satyapal.
- Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer ordered troops to fire at Jallianwala Bagh.
- The Gurdwara Reform Movement developed in the early 1920s; the Sikh Gurdwaras Act of 1925 established a statutory framework for gurdwara management.
- The Babbar Akali movement arose in the early 1920s; Kishan Singh Gargaj and Karam Singh were among its important figures.
- Udham Singh assassinated Michael O'Dwyer in London in 1940; this belongs to the later memory of the Jallianwala Bagh episode.
