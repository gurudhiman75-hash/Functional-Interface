## Scope

CP021 covers stable Punjab-GK facts about the Punjabi language and Gurmukhi script. It focuses on official-language status, development of Gurmukhi, the traditional Painti Akhari, vowel bearers and matras, common orthographic signs, tonal/script features and synthesis.

This CP is language GK, not a full Punjabi grammar chapter. Punjabi literature belongs to CP022.

## Qualified coverage

- Punjabi is the official language of the State of Punjab.
- The Punjab Official Language Act, 1967 defines Punjabi as Punjabi in Gurmukhi script.
- Guru Angad Dev is credited in standard historical treatment with developing, standardising and popularising Gurmukhi.
- Khadur Sahib is an important historical centre in the development and teaching of Gurmukhi during Guru Angad Dev's period.
- The traditional Gurmukhi `Painti Akhari` contains 35 basic letters.
- The three traditional vowel bearers are `ੳ`, `ਅ` and `ੲ`.
- Common dependent vowel signs include kanna `ਾ`, sihari `ਿ`, bihari `ੀ`, aunkar `ੁ`, dulainkar `ੂ`, lavan `ੇ`, dulavan `ੈ`, hora `ੋ` and kanaura `ੌ`.
- Sihari is written to the left of the consonant base in normal Gurmukhi text.
- Bindi `ਂ` and tippi `ੰ` are used for nasalisation according to orthographic context.
- Addak `ੱ` marks consonant gemination/doubling.
- Pairin bindi / nukta `਼` is used to extend the alphabet for additional consonant sounds.
- Common additional dotted consonants are `ਸ਼`, `ਖ਼`, `ਗ਼`, `ਜ਼`, `ਫ਼` and `ਲ਼`.
- Punjabi is a tonal language. Modern Gurmukhi does not normally use a dedicated set of tone marks; tonal values are represented through consonantal/phonological patterns.
- Gurmukhi is written from left to right.
- Gurmukhi has its own decimal digits `੦` to `੯`.
