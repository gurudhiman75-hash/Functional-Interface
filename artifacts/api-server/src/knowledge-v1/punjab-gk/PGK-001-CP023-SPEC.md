## Scope

CP023 covers high-value Punjab folk-culture GK: Bhangra, Giddha, Jhumar, Sammi, Luddi and Kikli; folk-song forms such as boliyan and tappay; major folk instruments; selected rural-culture objects, dress and Phulkari; and synthesis across dance, music and cultural forms.

CP022 owns Punjabi literature. CP024 owns fairs, festivals and heritage sites, although a festival may appear here only when needed to explain the traditional setting of a dance.

## Qualified coverage

- Bhangra is a major Punjabi folk dance with strong agrarian and harvest roots; dhol is one of its central rhythmic instruments.
- Giddha is a native Punjabi song-and-dance tradition performed traditionally by women; boliyan are an important verbal/song element of Giddha.
- Kikli is a Punjabi folk form in which girls commonly pair up, hold hands and spin.
- Jhumar, Sammi and Luddi are recognised Punjabi folk-dance forms. Use broad identification and style relations rather than disputed origin claims.
- Jhumar is generally slower and more graceful in movement than the highly energetic Bhangra style.
- Sammi is traditionally presented as a women's folk dance; Luddi is a celebratory Punjabi folk dance.
- Dhol is a double-headed drum; dholak is a smaller hand-played drum used in folk singing and domestic celebrations.
- Algoza is a paired-flute wind instrument; tumbi/tumba is a plucked single-string folk instrument; chimta is a metal percussion instrument; sarangi is a bowed string instrument.
- Ministry of Culture material records chimta in Punjabi folk performance and identifies ghara as an important rhythm instrument in Punjab's folk music.
- Punjabi rural-culture displays preserve instruments such as dholak, sarangi, algoza and tumba/tumbi, along with Phulkari, jutti and rural household objects.
- Phulkari is Punjab's traditional embroidery; Punjabi jutti is traditional footwear.
- Punjab Government culture material identifies Punjabi kurta with tehmat as traditional men's dress and Punjabi salwar suit as traditional women's dress.
- Tappa is a fast semi-classical vocal form whose roots are traced to the folk music of Punjab and Sindh.
