### 1. Easy · Objective
What is the primary objective of monetary policy in India?

A. Maintain price stability while keeping growth in mind  
B. Maximise tax revenue  
C. Fix stock-market prices  
D. Eliminate all business risk

**Answer:** A. Maintain price stability while keeping growth in mind

**Explanation:** The statutory objective gives primacy to price stability while also keeping growth in mind. Monetary policy is not designed to control taxes or remove all business risk.

---

### 2. Medium · Monetary vs fiscal
Which statement best describes monetary policy rather than fiscal policy?

A. Government changes tax rates and expenditure  
B. RBI uses monetary instruments to influence liquidity and financial conditions  
C. Parliament approves the Union Budget  
D. Government fixes customs duties

**Answer:** B. RBI uses monetary instruments to influence liquidity and financial conditions

**Explanation:** Monetary policy works through central-bank instruments that affect liquidity, interest rates and credit conditions. Taxation and government spending belong to fiscal policy.

---

### 3. Easy · MPC
How many members are there in India's Monetary Policy Committee?

A. Four  
B. Five  
C. Six  
D. Eight

**Answer:** C. Six

**Explanation:** The statutory MPC has six members. Three are from RBI and three are external members appointed by the Central Government.

---

### 4. Medium · MPC voting
What happens if MPC votes are equally divided?

A. The Finance Minister decides  
B. The oldest member gets two votes  
C. The RBI Governor has a second or casting vote  
D. The meeting is cancelled

**Answer:** C. The RBI Governor has a second or casting vote

**Explanation:** Every MPC member has one vote. If the votes are tied, the RBI Governor gets a second or casting vote to resolve the tie.

---

### 5. Hard · MPC procedure
Which statement about MPC procedure is correct?

A. It meets only when inflation rises  
B. Only the Governor votes  
C. It has no minimum meeting requirement  
D. It must meet at least four times a year and each member has one vote

**Answer:** D. It must meet at least four times a year and each member has one vote

**Explanation:** The amended RBI Act requires at least four MPC meetings each year. Each member has one vote, with the Governor getting a casting vote only when votes are equally divided.

---

### 6. Easy · Repo
The policy repo rate is the rate at which RBI:

A. Accepts uncollateralised overnight deposits  
B. Provides liquidity under LAF against eligible collateral  
C. Mints coins for banks  
D. Collects income tax

**Answer:** B. Provides liquidity under LAF against eligible collateral

**Explanation:** Repo is a collateralised liquidity-injection operation. RBI supplies funds against eligible securities.

---

### 7. Medium · Repo direction
Other things equal, an increase in the policy repo rate is usually associated with:

A. Tighter monetary conditions  
B. Higher system liquidity by definition  
C. A reduction in all taxes  
D. Automatic higher government spending

**Answer:** A. Tighter monetary conditions

**Explanation:** A higher repo rate makes central-bank liquidity more expensive and signals tighter monetary conditions. This can help moderate credit and demand pressure.

---

### 8. Easy · SDF
The Standing Deposit Facility is mainly used by RBI to:

A. Inject liquidity against securities  
B. Absorb liquidity without collateral  
C. Mint coins  
D. Manage tax receipts

**Answer:** B. Absorb liquidity without collateral

**Explanation:** SDF allows eligible participants to place surplus funds with RBI without RBI providing collateral. It is therefore a liquidity-absorption facility.

---

### 9. Medium · SDF application
Banks have excess overnight liquidity and want to place it with RBI without receiving securities in return. Which facility is designed for this?

A. MSF  
B. Repo  
C. SDF  
D. OMO purchase

**Answer:** C. SDF

**Explanation:** SDF absorbs surplus overnight liquidity through uncollateralised deposits with RBI. Repo and MSF instead provide liquidity to banks.

---

### 10. Medium · MSF
A bank faces an unexpected overnight liquidity shortage after normal market options are insufficient. Which RBI facility is most directly relevant?

A. SDF  
B. MSF  
C. OMO sale  
D. CRR increase

**Answer:** B. MSF

**Explanation:** MSF is designed as an overnight liquidity safety valve for eligible banks. It supplies funds when a bank faces an unexpected shortage.

---

### 11. Hard · Repo vs MSF
Which distinction between repo and MSF is most accurate?

A. MSF absorbs liquidity while repo drains it  
B. Repo has no connection with collateral  
C. Repo is the central policy liquidity rate; MSF is a higher-cost overnight safety valve  
D. MSF is the floor of the corridor

**Answer:** C. Repo is the central policy liquidity rate; MSF is a higher-cost overnight safety valve

**Explanation:** Repo is the main policy rate for collateralised RBI liquidity under LAF. MSF is an emergency overnight borrowing window at the upper edge of the corridor.

---

### 12. Medium · LAF corridor
Which order correctly represents the LAF corridor from floor to ceiling?

A. MSF → Repo → SDF  
B. SDF → Repo → MSF  
C. Repo → SDF → MSF  
D. CRR → Repo → OMO

**Answer:** B. SDF → Repo → MSF

**Explanation:** SDF forms the lower bound, the policy repo rate lies in the middle and MSF forms the upper bound. The corridor helps guide short-term money-market rates.

---

### 13. Hard · Corridor application
A bank with surplus overnight funds and another bank with an unexpected shortage approach RBI. Which pairing is most appropriate?

A. Surplus bank — MSF; shortage bank — SDF  
B. Surplus bank — SDF; shortage bank — MSF  
C. Both — CRR  
D. Both — OMO sale

**Answer:** B. Surplus bank — SDF; shortage bank — MSF

**Explanation:** SDF absorbs excess liquidity, whereas MSF supplies overnight liquidity in shortage conditions. The two facilities therefore operate on opposite sides of the corridor.

---

### 14. Easy · CRR
CRR requires banks to keep a prescribed share of their liabilities as:

A. Gold with the Government  
B. Cash balances with RBI  
C. Equity shares with SEBI  
D. Foreign currency with customers

**Answer:** B. Cash balances with RBI

**Explanation:** CRR is maintained as cash balances with RBI. It directly affects how much liquidity remains available within the banking system.

---

### 15. Medium · CRR direction
If CRR is raised, other things unchanged, banks generally have:

A. More lendable liquidity  
B. Less lendable liquidity  
C. No reserve obligation  
D. Automatic higher tax revenue

**Answer:** B. Less lendable liquidity

**Explanation:** A higher CRR requires banks to keep more cash with RBI. This leaves a smaller share of their funds available for lending.

---

### 16. Easy · OMO
RBI purchases government securities through an open market operation. The immediate liquidity effect is generally:

A. Liquidity absorption  
B. Liquidity injection  
C. CRR automatically rises  
D. No payment is made

**Answer:** B. Liquidity injection

**Explanation:** When RBI buys securities, it pays funds into the financial system. The purchase therefore injects durable liquidity.

---

### 17. Medium · OMO sale
RBI sells government securities through an OMO. The immediate liquidity effect is generally:

A. Liquidity injection  
B. Repo borrowing  
C. Liquidity absorption  
D. SDF creation

**Answer:** C. Liquidity absorption

**Explanation:** Market participants pay RBI for the securities. Those payments withdraw funds from the banking system and absorb liquidity.

---

### 18. Hard · OMO application
RBI wants to absorb durable liquidity without changing CRR. Which action fits best?

A. Buy government securities through OMO  
B. Lower CRR  
C. Provide repo liquidity  
D. Sell government securities through OMO

**Answer:** D. Sell government securities through OMO

**Explanation:** An OMO sale takes funds from market participants in exchange for securities. This drains durable liquidity without requiring a change in CRR.

---

### 19. Medium · Bank Rate
Bank Rate is best described as:

A. The statutory RBI rate for buying or rediscounting eligible bills and commercial paper  
B. The rate paid on SDF deposits  
C. The CRR percentage  
D. The rate on household savings accounts

**Answer:** A. The statutory RBI rate for buying or rediscounting eligible bills and commercial paper

**Explanation:** Bank Rate is defined under the RBI Act in relation to RBI buying or rediscounting eligible bills and commercial paper. It should not be confused with repo or SDF.

---

### 20. Hard · Instrument distinction
Which distinction is correct?

A. CRR and OMO are identical  
B. OMO fixes the statutory reserve ratio  
C. CRR means RBI buys government securities  
D. CRR changes mandatory cash balances; OMO changes liquidity through securities purchases or sales

**Answer:** D. CRR changes mandatory cash balances; OMO changes liquidity through securities purchases or sales

**Explanation:** CRR works through the cash balance banks must maintain with RBI. OMOs work through outright transactions in government securities.

---

### 21. Medium · Expansionary policy
Which combination is generally expansionary?

A. Higher policy rate, higher CRR and OMO sale  
B. Lower policy rate, lower CRR and OMO purchase  
C. Higher CRR and liquidity absorption  
D. Higher policy rate and OMO sale

**Answer:** B. Lower policy rate, lower CRR and OMO purchase

**Explanation:** Lower rates, lower reserve requirements and OMO purchases all tend to make liquidity or credit conditions easier. They therefore point in an expansionary direction.

---

### 22. Hard · Contractionary policy
Inflationary demand pressure is strong. Which policy direction most directly works to restrain credit and demand?

A. Lower rates plus liquidity injection  
B. Lower CRR plus OMO purchase  
C. Tighter policy rate and liquidity conditions  
D. More liquidity with cheaper funding

**Answer:** C. Tighter policy rate and liquidity conditions

**Explanation:** Tighter monetary conditions raise the cost or reduce the ease of credit. This can moderate borrowing, spending and demand pressure.

---

### 23. Medium · Statements
Consider the statements:

I. SDF absorbs liquidity.  
II. MSF provides overnight liquidity support.

Which is correct?

A. I only  
B. II only  
C. Both I and II  
D. Neither I nor II

**Answer:** C. Both I and II

**Explanation:** Statement I is correct because SDF accepts surplus funds with RBI. Statement II is also correct because MSF is an overnight borrowing safety valve for eligible banks.

---

### 24. Hard · Transmission
A policy action first makes RBI funding more expensive, then market borrowing costs rise and credit demand weakens. This is an example of:

A. Barter exchange  
B. Fiscal federalism  
C. Monetary-policy transmission  
D. National-income accounting

**Answer:** C. Monetary-policy transmission

**Explanation:** Monetary-policy transmission is the chain through which a central-bank action affects financial conditions and then borrowing, spending or demand. The question describes that multi-step process.
