### 1. Easy · Direct tax
Which of the following is a direct tax?

A. Personal income tax  
B. GST  
C. Customs duty  
D. Tax collected on a retail supply

**Answer:** A. Personal income tax

**Explanation:** Personal income tax is imposed directly on the taxpayer's income. GST and customs duty are indirect taxes linked to supplies or goods.

### 2. Medium · Direct vs indirect
Which feature best distinguishes an indirect tax from a direct tax?

A. Its economic burden can generally be passed to another person through the price  
B. It is always imposed only on company profits  
C. It can never affect consumers  
D. It is collected only by State Governments

**Answer:** A. Its economic burden can generally be passed to another person through the price

**Explanation:** Indirect taxes are commonly collected from one person and passed through prices to another. Direct taxes are imposed directly on the income or profits of the liable taxpayer.

### 3. Easy · Progressive tax
In which tax structure does the tax rate rise as taxable income rises?

A. Progressive taxation  
B. Proportional taxation  
C. Regressive taxation  
D. Destination taxation

**Answer:** A. Progressive taxation

**Explanation:** A progressive tax applies a higher rate as the taxable income or base rises. It is used as a redistribution tool in public finance.

### 4. Hard · Regressive burden
Two taxpayers face the same statutory tax rate, but the tax absorbs a larger share of the poorer taxpayer's income. Which statement is most accurate?

A. The burden is regressive even though the statutory rate on the taxed transaction may be the same  
B. The tax is automatically progressive  
C. The tax is proportional to income by definition  
D. The tax becomes a direct tax

**Answer:** A. The burden is regressive even though the statutory rate on the taxed transaction may be the same

**Explanation:** Regressivity is about the tax burden relative to income. A uniform transaction tax can therefore have a regressive effect across households with different incomes.

### 5. Easy · Tax incidence
What does tax incidence refer to?

A. The person who ultimately bears the economic burden of the tax  
B. Only the officer who collects the tax  
C. The law that creates the tax  
D. The date on which a return is filed

**Answer:** A. The person who ultimately bears the economic burden of the tax

**Explanation:** Tax incidence concerns the final economic burden after any shifting has occurred. It may differ from the person on whom the tax is initially imposed.

### 6. Hard · Impact vs incidence
A producer is legally liable to collect a tax but raises the selling price so consumers bear most of the cost. How are impact and incidence separated here?

A. Impact is on the producer while incidence is largely on consumers  
B. Impact and incidence are both necessarily on Government  
C. Impact is on consumers while incidence is on the tax officer  
D. No tax shifting has occurred

**Answer:** A. Impact is on the producer while incidence is largely on consumers

**Explanation:** The producer is the initial collection point, so the impact begins there. Because the price rises, consumers ultimately bear much of the burden, so incidence shifts to them.

### 9. Easy · Destination principle
Why is GST described as a destination-based tax?

A. Revenue is intended to accrue where the goods or services are consumed  
B. Tax is always retained by the producing State  
C. Only imports are taxed  
D. The tax is based on the seller's place of birth

**Answer:** A. Revenue is intended to accrue where the goods or services are consumed

**Explanation:** GST follows the destination principle. The consuming jurisdiction is the intended destination of the tax revenue rather than the place of origin.

### 10. Medium · Input Tax Credit
What is the main role of Input Tax Credit under GST?

A. It allows eligible tax paid on inputs to be set off against output tax liability  
B. It converts GST into income tax  
C. It removes all tax from final consumption  
D. It applies only to customs exports

**Answer:** A. It allows eligible tax paid on inputs to be set off against output tax liability

**Explanation:** Input Tax Credit gives credit for eligible tax already paid at earlier stages. This helps tax only the value added at each stage and reduces cascading.

### 11. Easy · Intra-State GST
Which taxes generally apply together on a taxable intra-State supply?

A. CGST and SGST  
B. IGST only  
C. Customs duty and income tax  
D. Corporation tax and IGST

**Answer:** A. CGST and SGST

**Explanation:** Under India's dual GST model, an intra-State supply generally attracts the Central and State components together. In relevant Union Territories, UTGST operates in place of SGST.

### 12. Hard · Dual GST
Why is the intra-State GST model called dual rather than purely Central or purely State taxation?

A. The Centre and the State levy separate GST components concurrently on the same taxable supply base  
B. Only local bodies levy the tax  
C. The tax alternates between Centre and State each year  
D. The buyer chooses which Government receives the whole tax

**Answer:** A. The Centre and the State levy separate GST components concurrently on the same taxable supply base

**Explanation:** India's GST design allows concurrent Central and State components on intra-State supplies. That concurrent structure is the essence of the dual GST model.

### 13. Easy · IGST
Which GST is generally levied on an inter-State supply?

A. IGST  
B. SGST only  
C. CGST only

**Answer:** A. IGST

**Explanation:** Inter-State supplies are handled through Integrated GST. The IGST mechanism preserves credit flow across State borders and allows apportionment between Union and States.
