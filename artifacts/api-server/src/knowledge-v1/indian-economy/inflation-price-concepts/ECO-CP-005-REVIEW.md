# ECO-CP-005 — Inflation & Price Concepts
## Human Review Set V1

---

### 1. Easy · Inflation
A sustained rise in the general price level is called:

A. Deflation  
B. Inflation  
C. Disinflation  
D. Stagnation

**Answer:** B. Inflation

**Explanation:** Inflation means a sustained rise in the general price level.

---

### 2. Easy · Deflation
A sustained fall in the general price level is called:

A. Inflation  
B. Disinflation  
C. Deflation  
D. Reflation

**Answer:** C. Deflation

**Explanation:** Deflation means the general price level keeps falling.

---

### 3. Medium · Disinflation
Inflation falls from 8% to 5%, but prices are still rising. This is:

A. Deflation  
B. Disinflation  
C. Hyperinflation  
D. Revaluation

**Answer:** B. Disinflation

**Explanation:** Inflation has slowed but remains positive.

---

### 4. Easy · Demand-pull
Demand rises faster than the economy can increase output. This mainly causes:

A. Cost-push inflation  
B. Demand-pull inflation  
C. Deflation  
D. Disinflation

**Answer:** B. Demand-pull inflation

**Explanation:** Excess demand puts upward pressure on prices.

---

### 5. Medium · Demand-pull
Consumers and firms sharply increase spending while the economy has little spare capacity. The likely pressure is:

A. Demand-pull inflation  
B. Cost-push inflation  
C. Deflation  
D. Falling purchasing power because of deflation

**Answer:** A. Demand-pull inflation

**Explanation:** Spending is rising faster than available output.

---

### 6. Easy · Cost-push
Fuel and transport costs rise sharply across the economy. This can cause:

A. Demand-pull inflation  
B. Cost-push inflation  
C. Deflation  
D. Disinflation

**Answer:** B. Cost-push inflation

**Explanation:** Higher input costs can push selling prices up.

---

### 7. Medium · Cost-push
A major supply disruption makes an important industrial input much more expensive, even though demand is unchanged. This is mainly:

A. Demand-pull inflation  
B. Cost-push inflation  
C. Deflation  
D. Base effect

**Answer:** B. Cost-push inflation

**Explanation:** The price pressure comes from higher production costs.

---

### 8. Easy · Purchasing power
Prices rise while a person's money income stays unchanged. Purchasing power will:

A. Rise  
B. Fall  
C. Stay unchanged  
D. Double

**Answer:** B. Fall

**Explanation:** The same money now buys fewer goods and services.

---

### 9. Medium · Purchasing power
A person's income rises by 4%, while the general price level rises by 7%. Real purchasing power will generally:

A. Rise  
B. Fall  
C. Stay unchanged  
D. Become zero

**Answer:** B. Fall

**Explanation:** Income is rising more slowly than prices.

---

### 10. Medium · Fixed income
A pension remains fixed while prices continue to rise. The pensioner is mainly affected because:

A. Purchasing power falls  
B. Purchasing power rises  
C. Nominal income automatically increases  
D. Deflation occurs

**Answer:** A. Purchasing power falls

**Explanation:** A fixed money income buys less when prices rise.

---

### 11. Medium · Borrower/lender
Unexpected inflation occurs after a fixed-rate loan is made. Other things equal, who tends to benefit?

A. The lender  
B. The borrower  
C. Both equally  
D. Neither

**Answer:** B. The borrower

**Explanation:** The borrower repays in money with lower purchasing power.

---

### 12. Hard · Borrower/lender
A fixed-rate loan is agreed before an unexpected rise in inflation. Which statement is most accurate?

A. The lender gains and the borrower loses in real terms  
B. The borrower gains and the lender loses in real terms  
C. Both gain equally  
D. Inflation cannot affect fixed repayments in real terms

**Answer:** B. The borrower gains and the lender loses in real terms

**Explanation:** Unexpected inflation lowers the real value of fixed nominal repayments.

---

### 13. Easy · CPI
Which index mainly tracks prices of a household consumption basket?

A. CPI  
B. WPI  
C. GDP deflator  
D. IIP

**Answer:** A. CPI

**Explanation:** CPI tracks prices of goods and services bought by households for consumption.

---

### 14. Easy · WPI
Which index tracks price movement mainly at the wholesale level?

A. CPI  
B. WPI  
C. GDP deflator  
D. IIP

**Answer:** B. WPI

**Explanation:** WPI measures price movement at the wholesale level.

---

### 15. Medium · GDP deflator
Which measure covers price change for final goods and services included in GDP?

A. CPI  
B. WPI  
C. GDP deflator  
D. Core CPI only

**Answer:** C. GDP deflator

**Explanation:** The GDP deflator reflects prices of output covered by GDP.

---

### 16. Hard · Price indices
Which statement is correct?

A. CPI focuses on household consumption, WPI on wholesale prices, and the GDP deflator on output covered by GDP.  
B. CPI measures only wholesale goods.  
C. WPI and GDP deflator always cover exactly the same basket.  
D. GDP deflator measures only imported consumer goods.

**Answer:** A. CPI focuses on household consumption, WPI on wholesale prices, and the GDP deflator on output covered by GDP.

**Explanation:** The three measures differ mainly in their coverage.

---

### 17. Easy · Headline/Core
CPI inflation excluding food and fuel is commonly used as a measure of:

A. Headline inflation  
B. Core inflation  
C. Deflation  
D. WPI inflation

**Answer:** B. Core inflation

**Explanation:** A common core measure excludes food and fuel.

---

### 18. Medium · Headline/Core
Food prices rise sharply while most other prices change little. Which measure is more directly affected by the food shock?

A. Headline inflation  
B. Core inflation excluding food and fuel  
C. Neither  
D. GDP growth

**Answer:** A. Headline inflation

**Explanation:** Headline inflation includes food, while this core measure does not.

---

### 19. Medium · Inflation calculation
A price index rises from 100 to 108. The inflation rate is:

A. 5%  
B. 8%  
C. 10%  
D. 16%

**Answer:** B. 8%

**Explanation:** (108 - 100) / 100 × 100 = 8%.

---

### 20. Hard · Reverse index calculation
A price index is 150. If it rises by 8%, the new index is:

A. 158  
B. 162  
C. 170  
D. 142

**Answer:** B. 162

**Explanation:** 8% of 150 is 12, so the new index is 162.

---

### 21. Medium · Correct pair
Which pair is correctly matched?

A. CPI — only wholesale prices  
B. WPI — household services only  
C. Core inflation — commonly excludes food and fuel  
D. Deflation — slower positive inflation

**Answer:** C. Core inflation — commonly excludes food and fuel

**Explanation:** Excluding food and fuel is a common core-inflation measure.

---

### 22. Medium · Statements
Consider the statements:

I. Inflation reduces purchasing power if income does not keep pace with prices.  
II. Deflation means a sustained fall in the general price level.

Which is correct?

A. I only  
B. II only  
C. Both I and II  
D. Neither I nor II

**Answer:** C. Both I and II

**Explanation:** Both statements are correct.

---

### 23. Hard · Statements
Consider the statements:

I. Disinflation means inflation has slowed.  
II. Disinflation necessarily means the general price level is falling.

Which is correct?

A. I only  
B. II only  
C. Both I and II  
D. Neither I nor II

**Answer:** A. I only

**Explanation:** During disinflation, prices can still be rising, but at a slower rate.

---

### 24. Hard · Base effect
A high inflation reading partly occurs because the price level in the comparison period was unusually low. This illustrates:

A. Cost-push inflation  
B. Base effect  
C. Core inflation  
D. Purchasing power

**Answer:** B. Base effect

**Explanation:** The comparison-period price level can affect the measured inflation rate.
