### 1. Easy · Barter
Direct exchange of goods without using money is called:

A. Legal tender  
B. Barter exchange  
C. Time deposit  
D. Credit creation

**Answer:** B. Barter exchange

**Explanation:** Barter is the direct exchange of one good or service for another. Money is not used as the intermediate medium of exchange.

---

### 2. Medium · Double coincidence
A wheat farmer wants shoes, but the shoemaker does not want wheat. The barter exchange fails mainly because of:

A. Excess liquidity  
B. Lack of double coincidence of wants  
C. Time maturity  
D. Legal tender requirement

**Answer:** B. Lack of double coincidence of wants

**Explanation:** The farmer and shoemaker do not have matching wants. A generally accepted medium of exchange solves this barter problem.

---

### 3. Easy · Function of money
A laptop is priced at ₹50,000 and a book at ₹500. Which function of money is shown?

A. Store of value  
B. Unit of account  
C. Medium of exchange  
D. Standard of deferred payment

**Answer:** B. Unit of account

**Explanation:** Money provides a common unit for quoting prices. This makes the values of very different goods easy to compare.

---

### 4. Medium · Function of money
A loan contract states that ₹20,000 will be repaid next year. Which function of money is shown?

A. Medium of exchange  
B. Store of value  
C. Standard of deferred payment  
D. Unit of account

**Answer:** C. Standard of deferred payment

**Explanation:** Money provides the unit in which a future debt is stated and later settled. This allows payments to be postponed to a future date.

---

### 5. Easy · Fiat money
Money accepted because of the authority behind its issue rather than its material value is:

A. Barter good  
B. Time deposit  
C. Fiat money  
D. Legal tender

**Answer:** C. Fiat money

**Explanation:** Fiat money does not need intrinsic commodity value equal to its face value. Its acceptability comes from the issuing authority and the monetary system.

---

### 6. Hard · Fiat vs legal tender
Which statement correctly separates fiat money from legal tender?

A. Fiat money and legal tender always mean exactly the same thing.  
B. Fiat money refers to value based on issuing authority; legal tender refers to legal acceptability for settlement.  
C. Fiat money means only deposits; legal tender means only time deposits.  
D. Legal tender depends on intrinsic metal value.

**Answer:** B. Fiat money refers to value based on issuing authority; legal tender refers to legal acceptability for settlement.

**Explanation:** Fiat money describes why money can be accepted despite low intrinsic value. Legal tender describes its legal status in settling transactions.

---

### 7. Medium · Legal tender
Which is not legal tender even though it can be used to make payments?

A. A legal-tender coin  
B. A cheque drawn on a bank account  
C. A legal-tender currency note  
D. Currency accepted under law

**Answer:** B. A cheque drawn on a bank account

**Explanation:** A cheque can be refused by the person receiving payment, so it is not legal tender. Legal-tender currency has a different legal status for settlement.

---

### 8. Easy · Demand deposit
A balance can be withdrawn whenever the account holder asks for it. It is a:

A. Time deposit  
B. Demand deposit  
C. Reserve asset  
D. Barter balance

**Answer:** B. Demand deposit

**Explanation:** A demand deposit is payable by the bank when the account holder demands it. It does not require waiting for a fixed maturity date.

---

### 9. Medium · Time deposit
A fixed deposit matures after a stated period. It is a:

A. Demand deposit  
B. Legal tender  
C. Time deposit  
D. Reserve money

**Answer:** C. Time deposit

**Explanation:** A time deposit has a specified period to maturity. This is the main distinction from a demand deposit, which is payable on demand.

---

### 10. Easy · Money supply
Money supply is best described as:

A. A flow measured only over a financial year  
B. A stock measured at a point of time  
C. Only the value of newly printed notes  
D. Only bank credit issued during a month

**Answer:** B. A stock measured at a point of time

**Explanation:** Money supply tells us how much money exists with the public at a particular point of time. It is therefore a stock variable rather than a flow over a period.

---

### 11. Medium · Currency with public
Why is cash held by banks excluded from currency with the public?

A. Because it is foreign currency  
B. Because currency with the public excludes cash kept by banks themselves  
C. Because it is always a time deposit  
D. Because it is part of barter

**Answer:** B. Because currency with the public excludes cash kept by banks themselves

**Explanation:** The measure aims to capture currency held outside banks by the public. Cash still held by banks is therefore not counted as currency with the public.

---

### 12. Medium · M1
M1 is defined as:

A. M1 + post-office savings deposits  
B. M1 + time deposits with banks  
C. Currency with public + demand deposits + other deposits with RBI  
D. M3 + total post-office deposits excluding NSCs

**Answer:** C. Currency with public + demand deposits + other deposits with RBI

**Explanation:** M1 contains the most liquid core components: currency with the public, demand deposits with the banking system and other deposits with RBI. It is classified as narrow money.

---

### 13. Hard · M3
Which monetary aggregate adds time deposits with banks to M1?

A. M1  
B. M2  
C. M3  
D. M4

**Answer:** C. M3

**Explanation:** M3 = M1 + time deposits with the banking system. Adding these less-liquid deposits makes M3 broader than M1.

---

### 14. Hard · M4
Which monetary aggregate adds total post-office deposits, excluding NSCs, to M3?

A. M1  
B. M2  
C. M3  
D. M4

**Answer:** D. M4

**Explanation:** M4 contains M3 plus eligible post-office deposits. This gives it the broadest coverage among M1, M2, M3 and M4.

---

### 15. Easy · Narrow money
Which pair is classified as narrow money?

A. M1 and M2  
B. M3 and M4  
C. M1 and M4  
D. M2 and M3

**Answer:** A. M1 and M2

**Explanation:** M1 and M2 are the narrow-money measures. They contain more liquid components than the broader M3 and M4 measures.

---

### 16. Medium · Broad money
Why is M3 broader than M1?

A. Because M3 excludes all deposits  
B. Because M3 contains only coins  
C. Because M3 adds time deposits with banks to M1  
D. Because M3 removes demand deposits

**Answer:** C. Because M3 adds time deposits with banks to M1

**Explanation:** M1 contains currency and highly liquid deposits. M3 includes all of M1 and adds bank time deposits, so its coverage is broader.

---

### 17. Medium · Liquidity
Among M1, M2, M3 and M4, which is the most liquid?

A. M4  
B. M3  
C. M2  
D. M1

**Answer:** D. M1

**Explanation:** M1 contains the components that are most readily usable for transactions. Liquidity generally falls as less immediately spendable deposits are added in broader aggregates.

---

### 18. Hard · Liquidity order
Which ordering shows decreasing liquidity?

A. M4 → M3 → M2 → M1  
B. M1 → M2 → M3 → M4  
C. M1 → M3 → M2 → M4  
D. M2 → M1 → M4 → M3

**Answer:** B. M1 → M2 → M3 → M4

**Explanation:** M1 is the most liquid of these aggregates and M4 the least liquid. Each broader measure adds components that are generally less immediately usable for transactions.

---

### 19. Medium · Reserve money
High-powered money is another name for:

A. Time deposit  
B. M4 only  
C. Reserve money  
D. Barter money

**Answer:** C. Reserve money

**Explanation:** High-powered money, reserve money and the monetary base refer to the base money created by the monetary authority. This base supports the wider money-creation process.

---

### 20. Hard · Reserve money
Why is reserve money called high-powered money?

A. Because it is always the largest monetary aggregate  
B. Because it contains every financial asset  
C. Because it forms the base on which a larger money supply can be supported  
D. Because it has no relation to bank reserves

**Answer:** C. Because it forms the base on which a larger money supply can be supported

**Explanation:** Reserve money is the monetary base held as currency or reserves. Through the banking system, a given monetary base can support a larger stock of money.

---

### 21. Medium · Money multiplier
Reserve money is 100 and money supply is 400. The simple money multiplier is:

A. 2  
B. 3  
C. 4  
D. 5

**Answer:** C. 4

**Explanation:** Money multiplier = money supply ÷ reserve money. Here, 400 ÷ 100 = 4.

---

### 22. Hard · Money multiplier
Reserve money is 250 and money supply is 750. The simple money multiplier is:

A. 2  
B. 3  
C. 4  
D. 5

**Answer:** B. 3

**Explanation:** Divide total money supply by reserve money. Here, 750 ÷ 250 = 3, so the multiplier is 3.

---

### 23. Medium · Statements
Consider the statements:

I. Demand deposits are payable on demand.  
II. Demand deposits are legal tender.

Which is correct?

A. I only  
B. II only  
C. Both I and II  
D. Neither I nor II

**Answer:** A. I only

**Explanation:** Statement I is correct because demand deposits can be withdrawn on demand. Statement II is false because a cheque or deposit-based payment can be refused and is not legal tender.

---

### 24. Hard · Reserve money vs M3
Which statement correctly distinguishes reserve money from M3?

A. Reserve money and M3 are always identical.  
B. M3 contains only currency and no deposits.  
C. Reserve money is the monetary base, while M3 is a broad money aggregate that includes bank time deposits.  
D. Reserve money means only post-office deposits.

**Answer:** C. Reserve money is the monetary base, while M3 is a broad money aggregate that includes bank time deposits.

**Explanation:** Reserve money is central-bank base money. M3 is broader because it contains M1 plus time deposits with the banking system.
