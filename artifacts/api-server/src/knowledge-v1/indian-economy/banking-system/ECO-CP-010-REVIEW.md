### 1. Easy · Scheduled bank
A scheduled bank is one that is included in:

A. The Second Schedule of the RBI Act, 1934  
B. The Union Budget  
C. The Companies Act schedule  
D. The SEBI Act

**Answer:** A. The Second Schedule of the RBI Act, 1934

**Explanation:** Scheduled-bank status comes from inclusion in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act, 1934. It is a legal classification, not a ranking of bank size.

---

### 2. Medium · Scheduled-bank distinction
A bank changes ownership but remains in the Second Schedule of the RBI Act. Its scheduled-bank status:

A. Automatically ends because ownership changed  
B. Continues unless its scheduled status itself changes  
C. Becomes an RRB automatically  
D. Becomes a Payments Bank automatically

**Answer:** B. Continues unless its scheduled status itself changes

**Explanation:** Scheduled status depends on inclusion in the statutory schedule, not simply on who owns the bank. An ownership change alone does not redefine that classification.

### 3. Easy · Financial intermediation
A bank accepts deposits from savers and lends part of those funds to borrowers. This is called:

A. Financial intermediation  
B. Barter exchange  
C. Tax collection  
D. Equity underwriting only

**Answer:** A. Financial intermediation

**Explanation:** Banks connect savers who have surplus funds with borrowers who need funds. That linking function is financial intermediation.

### 4. Medium · Intermediation application
Households place savings in banks, while firms borrow for working capital. What connects these two sides?

A. Fiscal deficit  
B. Bank financial intermediation  
C. Foreign-exchange intervention  
D. Barter

**Answer:** B. Bank financial intermediation

**Explanation:** The bank pools deposits and supplies credit, linking household savings with the financing needs of firms. That is the intermediation process.

### 5. Easy · Deposit type
A business needs an account for frequent receipts and payments. Which deposit type best fits?

A. Fixed deposit  
B. Current deposit  
C. Recurring deposit  
D. Long-term bond

**Answer:** B. Current deposit

**Explanation:** Current deposits are transaction-oriented and suit frequent business payments and receipts. Fixed deposits instead place funds for a stated period.

### 6. Medium · Deposit distinction
A saver deposits the same amount every month for a chosen period. This is a:

A. Current deposit  
B. Cash-credit limit  
C. Recurring deposit  
D. Demand loan

**Answer:** C. Recurring deposit

**Explanation:** A recurring deposit accumulates savings through regular instalments over time. It differs from a one-time fixed deposit.

### 13. Hard · SFB vs Payments Bank
Which statement correctly distinguishes Small Finance Banks from Payments Banks?

A. Payments Banks can lend but Small Finance Banks cannot  
B. Neither may accept deposits  
C. Small Finance Banks can lend; Payments Banks cannot undertake lending  
D. Both are only payment-system operators

**Answer:** C. Small Finance Banks can lend; Payments Banks cannot undertake lending

**Explanation:** Both models support financial inclusion, but their credit powers differ sharply. Small Finance Banks lend, while Payments Banks are barred from lending.

### 14. Medium · Secured loan
A loan backed by pledged property or another specified asset is a:

A. Unsecured loan  
B. Secured loan  
C. Demand deposit  
D. Recurring deposit

**Answer:** B. Secured loan

**Explanation:** A secured loan is supported by specified collateral. The security gives the lender an additional recovery claim if the borrower defaults.

### 15. Hard · Collateral distinction
Two borrowers have identical repayment ability, but only one pledges a house to secure the loan. What is the key distinction?

A. Both loans are unsecured because repayment ability is identical  
B. The collateral-backed loan becomes a deposit  
C. The unsecured loan automatically becomes an NPA  
D. The loan backed by the house is secured because specific collateral is pledged

**Answer:** D. The loan backed by the house is secured because specific collateral is pledged

**Explanation:** The presence of specific collateral is what makes one loan secured. Creditworthiness and collateral are related risk factors but are not the same concept.

### 16. Medium · Credit creation
Why can bank lending lead to creation of additional deposits in the banking system?

A. Every loan is printed as new currency  
B. Banks can ignore all reserve needs  
C. Loan proceeds can be redeposited and support further lending after reserves are retained  
D. Deposits disappear when a loan is made

**Answer:** C. Loan proceeds can be redeposited and support further lending after reserves are retained

**Explanation:** A bank loan can reappear as a deposit when the borrower spends the funds and the recipient deposits them. After reserves are retained, part of that deposit can support another lending round.

### 17. Medium · First-round lending
A bank receives a deposit of 1,000 and keeps 20% as reserves. Ignoring other constraints, how much can it lend in the first round?

A. 200  
B. 800  
C. 1,000  
D. 1,200

**Answer:** B. 800

**Explanation:** Twenty per cent of 1,000 is 200 kept as reserves. The remaining 800 is available for first-round lending in this simplified example.

### 18. Hard · Deposit expansion
Which condition would reduce the amount of multiple deposit expansion, other things equal?

A. Banks retain a smaller share as reserves  
B. Every loan is fully redeposited and reserves fall  
C. Banks retain a larger share of deposits as reserves  
D. Borrowers increase redepositing while reserves are unchanged

**Answer:** C. Banks retain a larger share of deposits as reserves

**Explanation:** A larger reserve share leaves less of each deposit available for another lending round. The chain of deposit expansion therefore becomes smaller.

### 19. Easy · NPA
For an ordinary term loan, the standard NPA trigger is interest or principal overdue for:

A. More than 7 days  
B. More than 90 days  
C. Exactly one year in every case  
D. Only after court action

**Answer:** B. More than 90 days

**Explanation:** For ordinary term loans, interest or principal overdue for more than 90 days meets the standard NPA trigger. Special asset classes can have separate rules.

### 20. Hard · NPA vs collateral
Which statement is correct about collateral and NPA classification?

A. Collateral guarantees that a loan can never become an NPA  
B. Only unsecured loans can become NPAs  
C. NPA status depends only on collateral value  
D. A secured loan can still become an NPA if repayment performance meets the NPA rule

**Answer:** D. A secured loan can still become an NPA if repayment performance meets the NPA rule

**Explanation:** Asset classification is based on repayment and income-recognition rules, not merely on whether collateral exists. Security affects recovery risk but does not prevent NPA classification.

### 23. Medium · Deposit insurance aggregation
A depositor holds several accounts in the same bank in the same right and same capacity. For DICGC insurance, these balances are generally:

A. Aggregated together  
B. Always insured as completely separate banks  
C. Ignored if one is a fixed deposit  
D. Converted into shares

**Answer:** A. Aggregated together

**Explanation:** DICGC aggregates balances held in the same right and same capacity at the same bank for insurance purposes. Separate account numbers alone do not create separate coverage buckets.

### 24. Hard · Mixed distinction
Which statement correctly separates credit risk from deposit insurance?

A. DICGC classifies every overdue loan as NPA  
B. An NPA is a type of insured savings account  
C. Deposit insurance prevents every bank loan from defaulting  
D. An NPA concerns a bank's problem loan, while DICGC concerns protection of eligible deposits

**Answer:** D. An NPA concerns a bank's problem loan, while DICGC concerns protection of eligible deposits

**Explanation:** NPA classification relates to the performance of a bank asset or loan. DICGC deposit insurance instead protects eligible depositor balances under its rules.
