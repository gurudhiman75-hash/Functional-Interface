### 1. Easy · History
RBI was established under which law?

A. Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934  
B. Banking Regulation Act, 1949  
C. FEMA, 1999  
D. PSS Act, 2007

**Answer:** A. Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934

**Explanation:** RBI was established under the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. The Bank began operations on 1 April 1935.

---

### 2. Easy · History
RBI commenced operations on:

A. 1 January 1949  
B. 1 April 1935  
C. 15 August 1947  
D. 1 April 1937

**Answer:** B. 1 April 1935

**Explanation:** RBI began operations on 1 April 1935. The RBI Act had already been enacted in 1934.

---

### 3. Medium · Central Office
RBI's Central Office was initially in Calcutta. It moved permanently to Mumbai in:

A. 1935  
B. 1937  
C. 1947  
D. 1949

**Answer:** B. 1937

**Explanation:** RBI's Central Office started in Calcutta and was permanently shifted to Mumbai in 1937. Mumbai has remained the Central Office location since then.

---

### 4. Medium · Nationalisation
RBI was nationalised with effect from:

A. 1 April 1935  
B. 15 August 1947  
C. 1 January 1949  
D. 26 January 1950

**Answer:** C. 1 January 1949

**Explanation:** RBI was originally privately owned. Nationalisation took effect on 1 January 1949, after which ownership vested in the Government of India.

---

### 5. Easy · Ownership
Before nationalisation, RBI was originally:

A. A privately owned shareholders' bank  
B. A State Government bank  
C. A cooperative bank  
D. A department of Parliament

**Answer:** A. A privately owned shareholders' bank

**Explanation:** RBI began as a privately owned shareholders' institution. It became Government-owned after nationalisation in 1949.

---

### 6. Medium · History sequence
Which sequence is correct?

A. RBI Act 1934 → operations 1935 → Central Office move 1937 → nationalisation 1949  
B. Operations 1934 → RBI Act 1935 → nationalisation 1937 → office move 1949  
C. Nationalisation 1934 → operations 1935 → RBI Act 1937 → office move 1949  
D. RBI Act 1934 → nationalisation 1935 → operations 1937 → office move 1949

**Answer:** A. RBI Act 1934 → operations 1935 → Central Office move 1937 → nationalisation 1949

**Explanation:** The correct order is the 1934 Act, commencement in 1935, permanent move to Mumbai in 1937 and nationalisation in 1949. Establishment and nationalisation are separate milestones.

### 7. Easy · Banknote issue
Which institution has the statutory right to issue banknotes in India?

A. SEBI  
B. NABARD  
C. Reserve Bank of India  
D. State Bank of India

**Answer:** C. Reserve Bank of India

**Explanation:** Section 22 of the RBI Act gives RBI the sole right to issue banknotes. The one-rupee currency note is a special Government of India issue.

---

### 8. Medium · Coins
Who is responsible for minting coins in India?

A. Reserve Bank of India  
B. Government of India  
C. Commercial banks  
D. SEBI

**Answer:** B. Government of India

**Explanation:** Coins are minted by the Government of India. RBI mainly distributes the coins supplied by the Government.

---

### 9. Hard · One-rupee note
Which statement about the one-rupee currency note is correct?

A. It is issued exactly like other RBI banknotes and carries the RBI Governor's signature.  
B. It is a Government of India issue and carries the Finance Secretary's signature.  
C. It is minted by RBI as a coin.  
D. It is issued by commercial banks.

**Answer:** B. It is a Government of India issue and carries the Finance Secretary's signature.

**Explanation:** The one-rupee note is a special Government of India issue. Unlike ordinary RBI banknotes, it carries the Finance Secretary's signature.

---

### 10. Easy · Banker to Government
RBI acting as banker for the Central Government is known as its:

A. Banker to Government role  
B. Lender-of-last-resort role  
C. Foreign-exchange role  
D. Developmental role

**Answer:** A. Banker to Government role

**Explanation:** RBI carries out banking transactions for the Central Government. This is its banker-to-government function.

---

### 11. Medium · State Governments
RBI may act as banker to a State Government when:

A. Every district bank requests it  
B. An agreement exists with that State Government  
C. SEBI authorises it  
D. The State has no treasury

**Answer:** B. An agreement exists with that State Government

**Explanation:** RBI can undertake banking business for a State Government through an agreement. This is different from its statutory banking relationship with the Central Government.

---

### 12. Hard · Debt management
The Government plans an issue of government securities and needs the institution that manages the borrowing operation. Which RBI role applies?

A. Banker to banks  
B. Payment-system regulator  
C. Government debt manager  
D. Foreign-exchange manager

**Answer:** C. Government debt manager

**Explanation:** Government securities are used for public borrowing, so RBI's debt-management role applies. Banker-to-banks instead concerns bank accounts and inter-bank settlement.

---

### 13. Easy · Banker to banks
RBI maintains banking accounts used by banks for reserves and settlements. This is its role as:

A. Banker to Government  
B. Banker to banks  
C. Foreign-exchange manager  
D. Currency printer

**Answer:** B. Banker to banks

**Explanation:** Banks maintain accounts with RBI for reserve and settlement purposes. This makes RBI the common banker to the banking system.

---

### 14. Medium · Inter-bank settlement
Inter-bank obligations are settled through accounts maintained with RBI. Which function is being used?

A. Government debt manager  
B. Developmental role  
C. Banker to banks  
D. Currency issue

**Answer:** C. Banker to banks

**Explanation:** Settlement between banks uses their accounts with RBI. That activity belongs to RBI's banker-to-banks function.

---

### 15. Hard · Banking-role distinction
Which pair correctly distinguishes two RBI banking roles?

A. Government accounts — banker to Government; inter-bank settlement — banker to banks  
B. Government accounts — banker to banks; inter-bank settlement — debt manager  
C. Government accounts — payment regulator; inter-bank settlement — forex manager  
D. Government accounts — currency issuer; inter-bank settlement — developmental role

**Answer:** A. Government accounts — banker to Government; inter-bank settlement — banker to banks

**Explanation:** Government banking transactions belong to the banker-to-government role. Accounts and settlement between banks belong to the banker-to-banks role.

---

### 16. Medium · Lender of last resort
A solvent bank faces temporary liquidity stress and cannot obtain funds elsewhere. RBI assistance in this case is called:

A. Government debt management  
B. Lender of last resort  
C. Currency minting  
D. Foreign-exchange management

**Answer:** B. Lender of last resort

**Explanation:** The lender-of-last-resort function provides emergency liquidity when an otherwise viable bank faces temporary funding stress. It is part of RBI's banker-to-banks role.

---

### 17. Hard · Lender of last resort
Which statement is most accurate about RBI as lender of last resort?

A. It guarantees profits to every bank.  
B. It applies only to Government borrowing.  
C. It is an emergency liquidity role, not an automatic guarantee against every bank loss.  
D. It means RBI mints coins for banks.

**Answer:** C. It is an emergency liquidity role, not an automatic guarantee against every bank loss.

**Explanation:** Lender-of-last-resort support addresses liquidity stress in the banking system. It should not be confused with an unlimited guarantee against every loss or insolvency problem.

---

### 18. Easy · Regulation and supervision
Prescribing and supervising broad banking-system rules is part of RBI's role as:

A. Regulator and supervisor of the financial system  
B. Government tax authority  
C. Stock exchange  
D. Coin mint

**Answer:** A. Regulator and supervisor of the financial system

**Explanation:** RBI regulates and supervises banking and financial activities within its legal remit. This supports confidence in the system and protection of depositors' interests.

---

### 19. Hard · Supervision vs emergency liquidity
Which distinction is correct?

A. Bank supervision means minting coins; lender of last resort means issuing Government debt.  
B. Both terms mean exactly the same function.  
C. Bank supervision concerns regulated institutions; lender of last resort concerns emergency liquidity stress.  
D. Bank supervision is done only during a liquidity crisis.

**Answer:** C. Bank supervision concerns regulated institutions; lender of last resort concerns emergency liquidity stress.

**Explanation:** Supervision is an ongoing oversight function over regulated institutions. Lender-of-last-resort support is a specific emergency liquidity function.

---

### 20. Medium · Foreign exchange
Management of the FEMA, 1999 framework is part of RBI's role as:

A. Banker to banks  
B. Manager of foreign exchange  
C. Coin mint  
D. Tax authority

**Answer:** B. Manager of foreign exchange

**Explanation:** RBI manages the foreign-exchange framework under FEMA, 1999. The role supports orderly development and maintenance of India's foreign-exchange market.

---

### 21. Medium · Payment systems
Under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, which institution is the designated payment-system authority?

A. SEBI  
B. NITI Aayog  
C. Reserve Bank of India  
D. Finance Commission

**Answer:** C. Reserve Bank of India

**Explanation:** The PSS Act designates RBI as the authority for regulation and supervision of payment systems in India. This is separate from the monetary-policy function.

---

### 22. Easy · Monetary authority
RBI's role in formulating, implementing and monitoring monetary policy is its role as:

A. Monetary authority  
B. Coin mint  
C. Tax authority  
D. Stock exchange

**Answer:** A. Monetary authority

**Explanation:** RBI is India's monetary authority and performs the monetary-policy function. The specific policy instruments are covered separately in ECO-CP-009.

### 23. Hard · Statements
Consider the statements:

I. RBI's lender-of-last-resort role can address temporary liquidity stress in a solvent bank.  
II. It guarantees that no bank can ever suffer losses.

Which is correct?

A. I only  
B. II only  
C. Both I and II  
D. Neither I nor II

**Answer:** A. I only

**Explanation:** Statement I is correct because emergency liquidity is the core lender-of-last-resort idea. Statement II is false because the role is not an unlimited guarantee against every bank loss or insolvency problem.

### 24. Hard · Institutional distinction
Which statement correctly separates payment-system regulation from monetary policy?

A. Both mean minting coins.  
B. Payment regulation oversees payment systems; monetary policy manages monetary conditions.  
C. Payment regulation deals only with Government debt.  
D. Monetary policy means maintaining bank current accounts only.

**Answer:** B. Payment regulation oversees payment systems; monetary policy manages monetary conditions.

**Explanation:** Payment-system regulation focuses on safe and authorised payment infrastructure. Monetary policy is the separate central-bank function concerned with monetary conditions in the economy.
