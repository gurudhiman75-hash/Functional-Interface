### 1. Easy · Public finance
Public finance mainly deals with:

A. Government revenue, expenditure and borrowing  
B. Only household saving  
C. Only company profits  
D. Only stock-market trading

**Answer:** A. Government revenue, expenditure and borrowing

**Explanation:** Public finance studies how Government raises resources, spends them and borrows, along with the economic effects of these decisions.

---

### 2. Medium · Fiscal vs monetary policy
Which action belongs to fiscal policy rather than monetary policy?

A. RBI changes the repo rate  
B. Government increases public expenditure  
C. RBI changes CRR  
D. RBI conducts an SDF operation

**Answer:** B. Government increases public expenditure

**Explanation:** Government expenditure is a fiscal-policy instrument. Repo, CRR and SDF are monetary-policy instruments used by RBI.

---

### 3. Easy · Expansionary fiscal policy
Which is generally expansionary fiscal policy?

A. Higher Government spending or lower taxes  
B. Lower Government spending and higher taxes  
C. Higher CRR only  
D. Higher repo rate only

**Answer:** A. Higher Government spending or lower taxes

**Explanation:** Expansionary fiscal policy raises aggregate demand by increasing Government spending or reducing the tax burden.

---

### 4. Hard · Contractionary application
Demand is overheating and inflationary pressure is strong. Which fiscal action is most contractionary?

A. Increase spending and cut taxes  
B. Reduce Government spending and/or raise taxes  
C. Lower reserve requirements  
D. Buy securities through OMO

**Answer:** B. Reduce Government spending and/or raise taxes

**Explanation:** Reducing spending or raising taxes withdraws demand from the economy, which is the basic contractionary fiscal direction.

---

### 5. Easy · Revenue receipt
Income-tax receipts of Government are classified as:

A. Revenue receipts  
B. Capital receipts  
C. Capital expenditure  
D. Public-debt repayment

**Answer:** A. Revenue receipts

**Explanation:** Tax revenue is a revenue receipt because it does not create a new liability or reduce Government assets.

---

### 6. Easy · Capital receipt
Government borrowing is classified as:

A. Revenue receipt  
B. Capital receipt  
C. Revenue expenditure  
D. Tax revenue

**Answer:** B. Capital receipt

**Explanation:** Borrowing is a capital receipt because it creates a future repayment liability for Government.

---

### 7. Medium · Non-debt capital receipt
Recovery of a loan earlier given by Government is a:

A. Non-debt capital receipt  
B. Tax revenue receipt  
C. Revenue expenditure  
D. Interest payment

**Answer:** A. Non-debt capital receipt

**Explanation:** Loan recovery reduces a Government financial asset but does not create fresh debt, so it is a non-debt capital receipt.

---

### 8. Easy · Revenue expenditure
Payment of Government salaries is generally:

A. Revenue expenditure  
B. Capital expenditure  
C. Capital receipt  
D. Non-debt receipt

**Answer:** A. Revenue expenditure

**Explanation:** Salaries are part of normal Government operations and do not directly create a Government asset.

---

### 9. Easy · Capital expenditure
Government spending on constructing a new highway is generally:

A. Capital expenditure  
B. Revenue receipt  
C. Revenue expenditure  
D. Tax receipt

**Answer:** A. Capital expenditure

**Explanation:** A new highway creates a long-lived public asset, so the spending is capital expenditure.

---

### 10. Hard · Expenditure distinction
Which distinction is correct?

A. Revenue expenditure always creates physical assets  
B. Capital expenditure creates assets or reduces liabilities; revenue expenditure mainly supports current operations  
C. Capital expenditure means only tax collection  
D. Revenue expenditure is a type of borrowing

**Answer:** B. Capital expenditure creates assets or reduces liabilities; revenue expenditure mainly supports current operations

**Explanation:** Capital expenditure is linked to asset creation or liability reduction, whereas revenue expenditure mainly finances current services and obligations.

---

### 11. Medium · Fiscal deficit
Fiscal deficit indicates the Government's:

A. Overall borrowing requirement  
B. Tax revenue only  
C. Interest payment only  
D. Capital expenditure only

**Answer:** A. Overall borrowing requirement

**Explanation:** Fiscal deficit is the gap between total expenditure and total non-debt receipts. That gap broadly represents the Government's borrowing requirement.

---

### 12. Medium · Fiscal-deficit calculation
Total expenditure is 1,000 and total non-debt receipts are 760. Fiscal deficit is:

A. 100  
B. 240  
C. 760  
D. 1,760

**Answer:** B. 240

**Explanation:** Fiscal deficit = 1,000 − 760 = 240.

---

### 13. Hard · Debt receipts and fiscal deficit
Why are debt receipts excluded when calculating fiscal deficit?

A. Because borrowing is used to finance the deficit and should not be counted as a non-debt resource  
B. Because borrowing is tax revenue  
C. Because all debt is revenue expenditure  
D. Because fiscal deficit ignores total expenditure

**Answer:** A. Because borrowing is used to finance the deficit and should not be counted as a non-debt resource

**Explanation:** Fiscal deficit measures the gap before new borrowing fills it. Including debt receipts would hide the actual borrowing requirement.

---

### 14. Medium · Revenue deficit
Revenue expenditure is 700 and revenue receipts are 620. Revenue deficit is:

A. 80  
B. 620  
C. 700  
D. 1,320

**Answer:** A. 80

**Explanation:** Revenue deficit = 700 − 620 = 80.

---

### 15. Hard · Revenue vs fiscal deficit
Revenue deficit is zero, but capital expenditure exceeds non-debt capital receipts. Which can still be positive?

A. Revenue deficit by definition  
B. Fiscal deficit  
C. Tax revenue only  
D. Automatic stabiliser

**Answer:** B. Fiscal deficit

**Explanation:** Even with no revenue deficit, Government may still need borrowing to finance capital expenditure. Fiscal deficit can therefore remain positive.

---

### 16. Medium · Primary deficit
Fiscal deficit is 300 and interest payments are 120. Primary deficit is:

A. 120  
B. 180  
C. 300  
D. 420

**Answer:** B. 180

**Explanation:** Primary deficit = fiscal deficit − interest payments = 300 − 120 = 180.

---

### 17. Hard · Zero primary deficit
What does a zero primary deficit imply?

A. Government has no public debt  
B. The fiscal deficit is entirely accounted for by interest payments  
C. Revenue receipts equal total expenditure  
D. All taxes have been abolished

**Answer:** B. The fiscal deficit is entirely accounted for by interest payments

**Explanation:** If primary deficit is zero, fiscal deficit equals interest payments. Current non-interest spending is not adding to the deficit beyond the interest burden.

---

### 18. Medium · Public debt
Why can persistent fiscal deficits raise future interest burdens?

A. They automatically eliminate all debt  
B. They can require additional borrowing, increasing the stock of public debt  
C. They turn taxes into capital assets  
D. They make interest payments disappear

**Answer:** B. They can require additional borrowing, increasing the stock of public debt

**Explanation:** Financing repeated fiscal deficits through borrowing adds to public debt, which can raise future interest-payment obligations.

---

### 19. Easy · FRBM
FRBM stands for:

A. Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management  
B. Financial Regulation and Banking Management  
C. Federal Revenue and Budget Mechanism  
D. Fiscal Reserve and Borrowing Method

**Answer:** A. Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management

**Explanation:** FRBM refers to the Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management framework created by the 2003 Act.

---

### 20. Hard · FRBM principle
Which approach is most consistent with FRBM principles?

A. Hiding borrowing outside fiscal statements  
B. Ignoring long-term debt sustainability  
C. Managing deficits and debt within a transparent medium-term fiscal framework  
D. Changing targets without disclosure

**Answer:** C. Managing deficits and debt within a transparent medium-term fiscal framework

**Explanation:** FRBM is built around medium-term fiscal management, debt sustainability and transparency.

---

### 21. Easy · Automatic stabiliser
An automatic stabiliser works:

A. Without a new discretionary policy decision each time  
B. Only after Parliament changes the law during every slowdown  
C. Only through RBI repo changes  
D. Only through currency printing

**Answer:** A. Without a new discretionary policy decision each time

**Explanation:** Automatic stabilisers respond as economic conditions change without requiring a fresh policy decision each time.

---

### 22. Medium · Automatic stabiliser application
During a slowdown, tax collections fall automatically as incomes weaken. This is an example of:

A. Automatic stabilisation  
B. Open market operation  
C. Bank credit creation  
D. Currency depreciation

**Answer:** A. Automatic stabilisation

**Explanation:** Lower incomes reduce some tax collections automatically, cushioning the fall in disposable income without a new tax-law change.

---

### 23. Hard · Statements
Consider the statements:

I. Borrowing is a debt-creating capital receipt.  
II. Loan recovery is a revenue receipt.

Which is correct?

A. I only  
B. II only  
C. Both I and II  
D. Neither I nor II

**Answer:** A. I only

**Explanation:** Statement I is correct because borrowing creates a liability. Statement II is false because loan recovery is a non-debt capital receipt.

---

### 24. Hard · Deficit distinction
Which deficit removes the effect of interest payments on past debt?

A. Revenue deficit  
B. Fiscal deficit  
C. Primary deficit  
D. Trade deficit

**Answer:** C. Primary deficit

**Explanation:** Primary deficit subtracts interest payments from fiscal deficit, isolating the current non-interest fiscal imbalance.
