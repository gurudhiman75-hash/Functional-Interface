### 1. Easy · Labour force
Employed persons plus unemployed persons available for work make up the:

A. Labour force  
B. Worker population only  
C. Total population  
D. Casual labour force

**Answer:** A. Labour force

**Explanation:** The labour force includes people who are working and people who are unemployed but available for work. It does not include the whole population automatically.

---

### 2. Medium · Unemployed
A person has no work but is available for work under the survey definition. The person is:

A. Self-employed  
B. Unemployed  
C. Regular salaried  
D. Outside the population

**Answer:** B. Unemployed

**Explanation:** An unemployed person is without work but is available for work under the survey definition. Being without a current job alone is not enough if the person is not part of the labour force.

---

### 3. Easy · Unemployment rate
The unemployment rate is calculated as unemployed persons divided by:

A. Total population  
B. Labour force  
C. Employed persons only  
D. Number of employers

**Answer:** B. Labour force

**Explanation:** The unemployment rate measures unemployed persons as a share of the labour force. The labour force includes both employed and unemployed persons.

---

### 4. Medium · LFPR
A population has 60 persons in the labour force out of 100 persons. LFPR is:

A. 40%  
B. 60%  
C. 100%  
D. 6%

**Answer:** B. 60%

**Explanation:** LFPR = labour force ÷ population × 100. Here, 60 ÷ 100 × 100 = 60%.

---

### 5. Easy · Employment status
A person runs her own small grocery shop. She is best classified as:

A. Self-employed  
B. Regular wage/salaried  
C. Casual labour  
D. Unemployed

**Answer:** A. Self-employed

**Explanation:** She works in her own enterprise rather than for a separate employer. That makes her self-employed.

---

### 6. Medium · Employment status
A construction worker is hired and paid only for the days on which work is available. This person is:

A. Self-employed  
B. Regular wage/salaried  
C. Casual labour  
D. Unemployed

**Answer:** C. Casual labour

**Explanation:** The worker is hired for short periods and paid for the days worked. This differs from a regular salaried job with continuing employment.

---

### 7. Easy · Seasonal unemployment
A farm worker gets work during sowing and harvesting but remains without work in the off-season. This is:

A. Seasonal unemployment  
B. Disguised unemployment  
C. Structural unemployment  
D. Frictional unemployment

**Answer:** A. Seasonal unemployment

**Explanation:** The job exists only during certain parts of the year. Unemployment caused by this regular seasonal pattern is seasonal unemployment.

---

### 8. Medium · Seasonal unemployment
A worker in a seasonal sugar mill loses work when the crushing season ends. This is:

A. Cyclical unemployment  
B. Seasonal unemployment  
C. Structural unemployment  
D. Disguised unemployment

**Answer:** B. Seasonal unemployment

**Explanation:** The worker loses employment because the production season has ended. The cause is the seasonal nature of the activity, not a recession or skill mismatch.

---

### 9. Medium · Disguised unemployment
Six family members work on a small farm, but output would stay the same if two stopped working. This shows:

A. Seasonal unemployment  
B. Frictional unemployment  
C. Disguised unemployment  
D. Cyclical unemployment

**Answer:** C. Disguised unemployment

**Explanation:** Some workers are not adding to total output because more people are working than needed. If they leave without reducing output, the unemployment is disguised.

---

### 10. Hard · Disguised unemployment
Workers appear employed, but the marginal contribution of some of them is effectively zero. Which type is indicated?

A. Disguised unemployment  
B. Structural unemployment  
C. Seasonal unemployment  
D. Frictional unemployment

**Answer:** A. Disguised unemployment

**Explanation:** The workers may look employed, but some are surplus to production. Their removal would not reduce output, which is the key feature of disguised unemployment.

---

### 11. Medium · Frictional unemployment
A worker leaves one job and spends a short period searching for a better one. This is:

A. Structural unemployment  
B. Frictional unemployment  
C. Cyclical unemployment  
D. Seasonal unemployment

**Answer:** B. Frictional unemployment

**Explanation:** The person is temporarily unemployed while moving between jobs. This short job-search period is frictional unemployment.

---

### 12. Hard · Structural unemployment
Jobs exist, but unemployed workers are trained for occupations that employers no longer need. This is:

A. Frictional unemployment  
B. Seasonal unemployment  
C. Structural unemployment  
D. Cyclical unemployment

**Answer:** C. Structural unemployment

**Explanation:** Jobs and workers both exist, but the skills do not match. A persistent mismatch between worker skills and available jobs is structural unemployment.

---

### 13. Easy · Absolute poverty
Poverty judged against a minimum level needed for basic needs is:

A. Absolute poverty  
B. Relative poverty  
C. Multidimensional poverty  
D. Disguised unemployment

**Answer:** A. Absolute poverty

**Explanation:** Absolute poverty compares a person's resources with a minimum standard needed for basic needs. The comparison is with a threshold, not with richer people in society.

---

### 14. Medium · Multidimensional poverty
A poverty measure combines deprivations in health, education and living standards. This is:

A. Relative poverty only  
B. Absolute poverty only  
C. Multidimensional poverty  
D. Seasonal poverty

**Answer:** C. Multidimensional poverty

**Explanation:** Multidimensional poverty looks at several forms of deprivation together. It therefore goes beyond a single income or consumption measure.

---

### 15. Medium · Poverty line
What is the main purpose of a poverty line?

A. To calculate GDP growth  
B. To set a threshold for identifying the poor under a chosen method  
C. To measure only unemployment  
D. To fix market prices

**Answer:** B. To set a threshold for identifying the poor under a chosen method

**Explanation:** A poverty line is a threshold used to classify people or households as poor under a chosen methodology. It does not directly measure unemployment or economic growth.

---

### 16. Hard · Poverty measurement
Two households have the same income, but one also lacks schooling, sanitation and adequate nutrition. Which approach captures this broader deprivation better?

A. Worker Population Ratio  
B. Unemployment Rate  
C. Multidimensional poverty approach  
D. A single monetary threshold only

**Answer:** C. Multidimensional poverty approach

**Explanation:** A multidimensional approach considers several disadvantages together, including health, education and living standards. A single monetary threshold may miss these additional deprivations.

### 17. Easy · Poverty committees
Which expert group is linked with India's 2009 review of poverty-estimation methodology?

A. Lakdawala Expert Group  
B. Tendulkar Expert Group  
C. Rangarajan Expert Group  
D. Y. K. Alagh Task Force

### 18. Medium · Poverty committees
The Rangarajan Expert Group is mainly associated with:

A. Reviewing the methodology for measurement of poverty  
B. Measuring only unemployment  
C. Preparing the Wholesale Price Index  
D. Setting the repo rate

**Answer:** A. Reviewing the methodology for measurement of poverty

**Explanation:** The Rangarajan Expert Group is associated with the 2014 review of poverty measurement. Its role belongs to the history of monetary poverty estimation in India.

### 19. Easy · MGNREGA
MGNREGA was enacted in:

A. 1979  
B. 1991  
C. 2005  
D. 2014

**Answer:** C. 2005

**Explanation:** MGNREGA is a 2005 law. Its important static-GK feature is the legal rural employment guarantee, while current wage rates can change.

---

### 20. Medium · MGNREGA
MGNREGA provides a statutory guarantee of up to how many days of wage employment to an eligible rural household?

A. 50 days  
B. 100 days  
C. 150 days  
D. 365 days

**Answer:** B. 100 days

**Explanation:** The Act provides for up to 100 days of wage employment to an eligible rural household. This is a stable feature of the law, unlike current wage rates or annual allocations.

---

### 21. Medium · Statements
Consider the statements:

I. LFPR uses population as the denominator.  
II. The unemployment rate uses the labour force as the denominator.

Which is correct?

A. I only  
B. II only  
C. Both I and II  
D. Neither I nor II

**Answer:** C. Both I and II

**Explanation:** LFPR is labour force divided by population, while the unemployment rate is unemployed persons divided by the labour force. The different denominators are the key distinction.

---

### 22. Hard · Statements
Consider the statements:

I. Relative poverty compares resources with others in the same society.  
II. Multidimensional poverty is measured only by unemployment status.

Which is correct?

A. I only  
B. II only  
C. Both I and II  
D. Neither I nor II

**Answer:** A. I only

**Explanation:** Statement I is correct because relative poverty is comparative. Statement II is false because multidimensional poverty combines several deprivations such as health, education and living standards.

---

### 23. Medium · Close distinction
Which statement best distinguishes seasonal from disguised unemployment?

A. Seasonal unemployment follows the time of year; disguised unemployment has more workers than needed.  
B. Seasonal unemployment is always urban; disguised unemployment is always rural.  
C. Seasonal unemployment means job search; disguised unemployment means recession.  
D. They are the same concept.

**Answer:** A. Seasonal unemployment follows the time of year; disguised unemployment has more workers than needed.

**Explanation:** Seasonal unemployment occurs because work is available only during certain periods. Disguised unemployment exists when surplus workers can leave without reducing output.

---

### 24. Hard · Close distinction
Which statement best distinguishes LFPR from WPR?

A. LFPR counts only unemployed persons; WPR counts the labour force.  
B. LFPR counts the labour force; WPR counts employed persons, both relative to population.  
C. LFPR and WPR always have the same numerator.  
D. WPR uses unemployed persons as its numerator.

**Answer:** B. LFPR counts the labour force; WPR counts employed persons, both relative to population.

**Explanation:** LFPR uses employed plus unemployed persons in the labour force as its numerator. WPR uses only employed persons, while both are expressed relative to population.
