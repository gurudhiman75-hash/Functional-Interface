# GEO-RIV-001-CP004 — Brahmaputra River System — Source Audit V1

## Scope

This checkpoint covers durable, repeatedly tested geography of the Brahmaputra system. It deliberately avoids unstable measurements, current flood/project status, and source-name precision where official publications use different conventions.

## Primary authorities

### 1. Central Water Commission — Water Resources Assessment of India 2024
Use for basin extent, broad source region and principal tributaries. It places the Brahmaputra source in the Kailash ranges and lists principal tributaries on the two sides of the main river.

### 2. CWC/IMD — PMP Atlas for Brahmaputra River Basin
Use for the mainstream course: Tibetan Plateau source, Tsangpo name in Tibet, entry into India, Siang course, Dibang/Lohit confluence and Brahmaputra naming, Assam valley, and major tributary set.

### 3. CWC — Brahmaputra Morphology Report
Use for north-bank/south-bank tributary classification and the trans-Himalayan north-bank trio: Subansiri, Jia Bharali and Manas. Also supports Ranganadi, Dikrong and Jiadhol as Subansiri tributaries.

### 4. CWC — Siang cumulative-impact report
Use for the Siang/Dihang → Dibang + Lohit → Brahmaputra transition and Kameng/Jia Bharali naming.

### 5. Government of Assam — Water Resources Department
Use as the Assam-side bank classification authority. It independently lists major north-bank and south-bank tributaries.

### 6. CWC lower-Ganga report
Use only for the Bangladesh naming relation: Brahmaputra is locally called Jamuna before joining the Ganga/Padma system.

### 7. NCERT — North East India
Use as educational framing and exam-convention evidence, not as the sole authority where lower-course naming differs from newer CWC material.

## Locked durable relations

- source region: Tibetan Plateau / Kailash ranges
- source-glacier convention: Chema Yundung glacier
- Tibet name: Tsangpo / Yarlung Tsangpo
- India entry: Arunachal Pradesh
- Indian upper-course name: Siang; Dihang is also used in the lower Arunachal/upper Assam transition
- Dibang and Lohit join the Siang/Dihang; downstream the river is called Brahmaputra
- Brahmaputra flows through the Assam valley
- Bangladesh local name: Jamuna
- major northern/right-side tributaries include Subansiri, Jia Bharali/Kameng, Manas, Sankosh and others
- major southern/left-side tributaries include Burhi Dihing, Disang, Dikhow, Dhansiri (South) and Kopili
- Subansiri, Jia Bharali and Manas are the three major trans-Himalayan north-bank tributaries identified by CWC
- Ranganadi, Dikrong and Jiadhol are important tributaries of Subansiri
- Teesta is a major Brahmaputra-system tributary joining the Brahmaputra/Jamuna in Bangladesh

## Discrepancies and exclusions

### Source/glacier wording
Official documents use slightly different source-region/glacier wording (Chema Yundung/Chema Yungdang and Kailash-range formulations). Store the stable exam relation and preserve source wording in provenance; do not generate precision beyond the locked convention.

### Siang / Dihang naming
Use both as accepted upper-course Indian names, but do not generate a false rigid boundary claiming that one exact point universally changes Siang to Dihang.

### Formation location
Official material variously describes the Dibang/Lohit joining area with Pasighat/Sadiya-area wording. Generate the river relation, not an over-precise town claim.

### Bangladesh naming
Use CWC's explicit `locally called Jamuna` relation. Do not use the simplified NCERT line that directly labels the entering Brahmaputra as Meghna.

### Do not generate
- exact total length or catchment-area values
- exact source altitude
- changing flood statistics
- current dam/project status
- folklore/religious etymology as physical geography
- claims that Majuli is currently the world's largest river island; such rankings depend on definition and change over time
