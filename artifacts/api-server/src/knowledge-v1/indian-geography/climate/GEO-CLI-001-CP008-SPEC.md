## Source authority

Primary authority: NCERT `India: Physical Environment`, Climate chapter, especially relief as a climatic control and the Arabian Sea / Bay of Bengal monsoon branch sections.

Supporting authority: NCERT `Contemporary India-I`, Climate chapter.

Key governed facts include:

- Relief affects the amount and distribution of rainfall by changing wind direction and forcing moist air to rise.
- The Western Ghats strongly lift the Arabian Sea branch. Their windward western slopes and coastal plain receive heavy monsoon rainfall.
- After crossing the Western Ghats, winds descend, warm and lose relative humidity, creating a lower-rainfall rain-shadow region to the east.
- An Arabian Sea branch moves inland north of Mumbai through the Narmada and Tapi valleys and contributes rain over central India.
- Another Arabian Sea branch passes through Saurashtra-Kachchh and western Rajasthan along the Aravalli region, where rainfall is scanty.
- A Bay of Bengal branch moves westward along the Ganga plains; rainfall broadly decreases from east to west as the branch loses moisture.
- Another Bay branch moves through the Brahmaputra valley, and a sub-branch strikes the Garo-Khasi hills of Meghalaya, producing very heavy orographic rain.
- Mawsynram lies on the Khasi Hills and is noted for exceptionally high average annual rainfall.
- During the southwest monsoon, the Tamil Nadu coast is comparatively dry because it lies parallel to the Bay branch and on the leeward side of the Arabian Sea branch after the Western Ghats.
