## Source authority

Supporting authority: NCERT `Contemporary India-I`, Chapter 4 — Climate.

Key governed comparisons:

- Northern India changes from relatively higher-pressure cool winter conditions to strong summer heating and lower pressure before the southwest monsoon.
- The first southwest monsoon rains follow the pre-monsoon heat and commonly lower temperature.
- June–September is the main southwest-monsoon rainy season for most of India; October–November is the retreating-monsoon season and is especially important for Tamil Nadu.
- Northwestern winter rain comes mainly from western disturbances, whereas important cool-season rain on the Tamil Nadu coast comes from retreating/northeast-monsoon and Bay systems.
- Coastal locations have smaller seasonal temperature ranges than far-inland locations because the sea heats and cools more slowly than land.
- Latitude affects solar heating; altitude lowers temperature with height.
- Relief strongly redistributes rainfall between windward and leeward slopes; distance from sea strongly affects seasonal temperature range.
- Seasonal land–sea heating changes pressure patterns and helps reverse the prevailing monsoon winds.
- QL099 integrates these controls and seasonal comparisons without creating new climate facts.
