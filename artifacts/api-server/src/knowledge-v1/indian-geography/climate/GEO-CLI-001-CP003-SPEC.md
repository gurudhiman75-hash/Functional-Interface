## Scope

CP003 covers the cold weather season over India: its broad timing, north-south temperature pattern, winter pressure conditions, northeast trade winds, generally dry continental flow, clear-sky/low-humidity weather, frost and Himalayan snowfall, maritime moderation over peninsular India, and the basic winter-rain exceptions relevant to the season.

Detailed western-disturbance mechanism remains reserved for CP009. Retreating-monsoon and northeast-monsoon cyclonic-rain depth remains reserved for CP006.

## Source authority

Primary educational authority: NCERT `Contemporary India-I`, Chapter 4 — Climate.

Supporting authority where a mechanism needs clearer framing: NCERT `India: Physical Environment`, Climate chapter, and static IMD educational material.

The checkpoint uses only stable climatological facts. Live forecasts, current-season departures, named current systems and changing annual statistics are excluded.

## Ownership boundary

- CP003 may state that winter cyclonic disturbances from the west/northwest bring rain to the northern plains and snow to the Himalayas, but detailed western-disturbance dynamics belong to CP009.
- CP003 may test the basic Tamil Nadu winter-rain exception because it explains why the cold season is not completely dry everywhere; detailed retreating/northeast-monsoon rainfall belongs to CP006.
- CP003 does not own hot-weather local winds, southwest-monsoon advance, regional monsoon branches, ENSO or jet streams.

## Exam-grade stem contract

- Every stem must read like a normal SSC, Banking, Railway or similar objective-exam question.
- Keep wording concise, natural and self-contained.
- Easy questions test one stable fact; Medium questions test a relation, comparison or regional exception; Hard questions integrate two or more cold-season facts.
- Difficulty must come from geography relations, not difficult English.
- Standard statement instructions may repeat, but statement format must not dominate the batch.
- Avoid generator-like filler such as `associated with`, `described as`, `in the context of`, `with reference to the above`, or vague prompts such as `Which is correct?`.
- Explanations must state the decisive fact in simple connected language; avoid option-by-option analysis unless genuinely necessary.
- Learner-facing text must not contain source names, internal review terminology or generator language.
