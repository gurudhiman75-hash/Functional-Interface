## Source authority

Primary authority: NCERT `Contemporary India-I`, Chapter 4 — Climate.

Supporting authority: NCERT `India: Physical Environment`, Climate chapter.

Key governed facts:

- Western Rajasthan has very low rainfall and high rainfall variability; the Aravalli alignment gives little orographic uplift to the Arabian Sea branch.
- Kerala is among the first mainland areas reached by the southwest monsoon; windward Western Ghats receive heavy orographic rain and the nearby sea moderates temperature.
- The Tamil Nadu coast receives relatively little rain during much of the southwest monsoon but gains important rainfall in October-November during the retreating monsoon; Bay depressions and cyclones contribute substantially.
- Meghalaya, especially the Khasi Hills around Mawsynram, receives very heavy relief-enhanced monsoon rainfall.
- Punjab and Haryana receive useful winter rain from western disturbances; the same northwestern plains may experience hot, dry loo winds before the summer monsoon.
- Interior parts of Maharashtra and Karnataka lie in the rain shadow east of the Western Ghats and are drier than the windward west coast.
- Across much of the northern plains, monsoon rainfall decreases from east to west as the Bay of Bengal branch loses moisture inland.
- Himalayan highlands are colder because of altitude; higher elevations commonly receive winter snowfall, and the Himalayan barrier limits direct penetration of very cold continental winds.
- QL090 integrates these canonical region-climate links without creating new regional facts.
