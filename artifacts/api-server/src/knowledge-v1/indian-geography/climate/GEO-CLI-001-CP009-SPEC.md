Key governed facts:

- Western disturbances are shallow temperate cyclonic systems that commonly originate around the eastern Mediterranean and travel eastward through West Asia, Iran, Afghanistan and Pakistan before reaching northwestern India.
- They may gain additional moisture from the Caspian Sea and Persian Gulf.
- They bring useful winter rain to northwestern India; the amount is usually modest but is important for rabi crops.
- In the Himalayas their precipitation can fall as snow; later snowmelt helps sustain Himalayan river flow in summer.
- The winter westerly jet lies south of the Himalayas and helps steer western disturbances.
- Before the southwest monsoon becomes established, the westerly jet withdraws from its winter position south of the Himalayas.
- The tropical easterly jet sets in near 15°N after the westerly jet withdraws and is linked with the burst of the monsoon.
- Southern Oscillation is tracked through large-scale pressure changes, commonly represented by the Tahiti–Darwin pressure difference.
- El Niño is the warm phase of ENSO, involving abnormal warming of the central/eastern equatorial Pacific, classically including waters off Peru.
- ENSO is a coupled ocean–atmosphere phenomenon. It can influence Indian monsoon variability, but the relationship is not one-to-one and ENSO is not the sole control of monsoon rainfall.
