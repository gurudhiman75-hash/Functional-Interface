# POL-CP-016 — State Legislature

**Lifecycle:** REVIEW CANDIDATE — V1  
**Runtime registration:** BLOCKED pending English approval

## Scope

This CP covers the State Legislature centred on Articles 168–212: structure, composition, membership, presiding officers, conduct of business, disqualifications, privileges, legislative procedure, Money Bills and State financial procedure. Governor-specific assent/reservation and Ordinance powers remain principally covered in POL-CP-014.

## Core constitutional coverage

- Articles 168–169 — unicameral/bicameral structure and creation/abolition of Legislative Councils
- Articles 170–171 — Assembly and Council composition
- Articles 172–173 — duration and membership qualifications
- Articles 174–177 — sessions, Governor addresses/messages and participation rights
- Articles 178–187 — Speaker/Deputy Speaker, Chairman/Deputy Chairman, salaries and legislative secretariat
- Articles 188–189 — oath, voting, casting vote, vacancies and quorum
- Articles 190–193 — vacation of seats, disqualifications, decision mechanism and constitutional penalty
- Articles 194–195 — legislative privilege and member salaries
- Articles 196–197 — ordinary Bills, lapse rules and Legislative Council delaying power
- Articles 198–199 — Money Bills and Speaker certification
- Articles 202–207 — annual financial statement, grants, appropriation, supplementary/additional/excess grants, vote on account, vote of credit, exceptional grant and financial Bills
- Articles 208–212 — procedural rules, financial-business regulation, language, judicial-discussion restriction and procedural-irregularity protection

## Accuracy guardrails

- Article 169 uses majority of total Assembly membership plus at least two-thirds present and voting;
- Article 169 law is not treated as an Article 368 amendment;
- Assembly size is generally 60–500, subject to constitutional exceptions;
- Council size is ordinarily at most one-third of Assembly strength and at least 40;
- Council is continuing; approximately one-third retires every two years;
- minimum ages are 25 for Assembly and 30 for Council;
- Speaker removal uses majority of all the then Assembly members plus 14 days' notice;
- ordinary quorum is 10 members or one-tenth of total House strength, whichever is greater, unless State law provides otherwise;
- Article 192(1) disqualification questions are decided by Governor after obtaining and acting according to Election Commission opinion;
- an ordinary State Bill has no joint-sitting deadlock mechanism; Article 197 uses Assembly repassage and Council delay limits;
- Money Bills originate only in Assembly; Council has 14 days for recommendations;
- Speaker decides and certifies State Money Bills;
- charged expenditure is discussed but not voted by the Assembly;
- demands for grants are voted only by the Assembly and require Governor recommendation;
- Article 207 financial-Bill recommendation rules are kept distinct from Money Bill procedure;
- Article 211 bars discussion of Supreme Court/High Court judicial conduct in discharge of duties;
- Article 212 protects proceedings from challenge merely for procedural irregularity.
