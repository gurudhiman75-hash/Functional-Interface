# POL-CP-012 — Supreme Court

**Lifecycle:** REVIEW CANDIDATE — V1  
**Runtime registration:** BLOCKED pending English approval

## Scope

This CP covers the Supreme Court as the Union's apex constitutional court, centred on Articles 124–147 with a limited Article 32 link for Fundamental Rights enforcement. Current Judges, current vacancies and current sanctioned strength are excluded.

## Core constitutional coverage

- Article 124 — establishment, appointment, qualifications, retirement, resignation and removal
- Articles 125–128 — conditions, acting CJI, ad hoc and retired Judges
- Article 129 — court of record and contempt
- Article 130 — seat of Supreme Court
- Article 131 — exclusive original federal jurisdiction
- Articles 132–134A — constitutional, civil and criminal appeals and High Court certificates
- Article 136 — special leave to appeal
- Articles 137–140 — review, enlargement, transfer and ancillary powers
- Article 141 — binding law declared by Supreme Court
- Article 142 — complete justice and nationwide enforcement
- Article 143 — advisory jurisdiction
- Articles 144–147 — aid by authorities, rules, staff/expenses and interpretation
- Article 32 — Fundamental Rights enforcement link

## Current appointment-system guardrail

The official 2026 Constitution edition retains amendment-history text relating to NJAC but footnotes that the Ninety-Ninth Amendment changes were struck down. Official Department of Justice material confirms that the pre-NJAC collegium system became operative again. Questions therefore distinguish collegium recommendation from formal presidential appointment.

## Accuracy guardrails

- Supreme Court retirement age is 65 years;
- Article 124 does not prescribe a separate numerical minimum appointment age;
- removal requires proved misbehaviour or incapacity and separate qualifying addresses by both Houses;
- Article 131 federal disputes require a legal-right element;
- Article 136 special leave is discretionary and excludes Armed-Forces courts/tribunals covered by clause (2);
- Article 141 binding precedent is kept distinct from Article 142 complete justice;
- Article 143 is advisory jurisdiction;
- Article 32 Fundamental Rights jurisdiction is kept distinct from Article 131 federal original jurisdiction;
- the NJAC amendments are not treated as currently operative.
