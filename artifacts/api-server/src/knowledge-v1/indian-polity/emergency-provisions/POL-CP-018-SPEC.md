# POL-CP-018 — Emergency Provisions

**Lifecycle:** REVIEW CANDIDATE — V1  
**Runtime registration:** BLOCKED pending English approval

## Scope

This CP covers Part XVIII of the Constitution, centred on Articles 352–360, with limited cross-links to Articles 83, 172 and 250 where National Emergency changes legislative terms or Parliament's State List power.

## Constitutional coverage

- Article 352 — National Emergency grounds, imminent danger, written Union Cabinet decision, approval, continuation and Lok Sabha disapproval safeguards
- Article 353 — effect on Union executive directions and parliamentary legislative power
- Article 354 — temporary modification of specified revenue-distribution provisions
- Article 355 — Union duty to protect States and ensure constitutional government
- Article 356 — failure of constitutional machinery in States; proclamation, effects, parliamentary approval and duration
- Article 357 — exercise/delegation of State legislative powers during President's Rule and survival of laws afterwards
- Article 358 — Article 19 during Emergency based on war or external aggression
- Article 359 — suspension of court enforcement of specified Part III rights, excluding Articles 20 and 21
- Article 360 — Financial Emergency, approval and financial directions
- Articles 83 and 172 — extension of Lok Sabha/State Assembly terms during National Emergency
- Article 250 — temporary parliamentary power over State List during National Emergency

## Accuracy guardrails

- Article 352 grounds are war, external aggression and armed rebellion;
- Article 352 can be used on imminent danger before the event occurs;
- written Union Cabinet decision is required before Article 352 proclamation/variation;
- Article 352 requires approval within one month and special-majority approval for continuation in six-month blocks;
- one-tenth of Lok Sabha membership can trigger the special disapproval-sitting mechanism; the sitting must be held within 14 days;
- Article 356 may be based on a Governor's report or otherwise;
- Article 356 cannot transfer High Court powers to the President or suspend constitutional provisions relating to High Courts;
- Article 356 requires approval within two months, renews in six-month blocks and is normally capped at three years;
- continuation of President's Rule beyond one year requires the relevant National Emergency condition plus Election Commission certification of election difficulty;
- Article 358 applies only to Emergency based on war or external aggression and requires the statutory recital for its special protection;
- Article 359 cannot suspend court enforcement of Articles 20 and 21;
- Financial Emergency requires approval within two months and has no six-month renewal cycle after approval;
- Article 360 directions may cover financial propriety, specified salary reductions and reservation of State financial Bills for Presidential consideration;
- Lok Sabha and State Assembly terms may be extended during National Emergency by up to one year at a time, but not beyond six months after the Emergency ends.
