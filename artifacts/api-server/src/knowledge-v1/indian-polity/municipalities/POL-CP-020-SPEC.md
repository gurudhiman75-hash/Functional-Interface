# POL-CP-020 — Municipalities

**Lifecycle:** APPROVED / CONTENT-FROZEN — V1  
**Runtime registration:** READY FOR POLITY PACKAGE BINDING

## Scope

This CP covers the constitutional framework for urban local government created by the Constitution (Seventy-fourth Amendment) Act, 1992, principally Part IXA (Articles 243P–243ZG) and the Twelfth Schedule.

## Constitutional coverage

- Article 243P — definitions and 10-lakh metropolitan-area threshold
- Article 243Q — Nagar Panchayat, Municipal Council, Municipal Corporation and industrial-township exception
- Article 243R — direct ward elections and additional representation
- Article 243S — Wards Committees and 3-lakh threshold
- Article 243T — SC/ST, women and backward-class reservation
- Article 243U — five-year term and re-election after dissolution
- Article 243V — membership disqualifications and age protection at 21
- Article 243W — powers, planning, social justice and State-law devolution
- Article 243X — taxes, assigned revenues, grants and funds
- Article 243Y — State Finance Commission review of municipal finances
- Article 243Z — municipal accounts and audit
- Article 243ZA — municipal elections under the State Election Commission
- Article 243ZB — Union-territory application
- Article 243ZC — Scheduled/tribal-area exceptions
- Article 243ZD — District Planning Committee
- Article 243ZE — Metropolitan Planning Committee
- Article 243ZF — transitional continuation of older laws and Municipalities
- Article 243ZG — bar to ordinary court interference in municipal electoral matters
- Twelfth Schedule — all 18 functional matters

## Accuracy guardrails

- 74th Amendment municipal provisions came into force on 1 June 1993;
- Part IXA covers Articles 243P–243ZG;
- metropolitan area requires population of 10 lakh or more plus the other Article 243P conditions;
- Article 243Q distinguishes transitional, smaller urban and larger urban areas and permits an industrial-township exception;
- municipal seats are generally filled by direct election from wards;
- Wards Committees are constitutionally required in Municipalities with population of 3 lakh or more;
- at least one-third of directly elected municipal seats are reserved for women;
- Municipality term is five years, with normal reconstitution within six months after dissolution;
- age below 25 alone does not disqualify a municipal candidate who has attained 21;
- Article 243W enables State-law devolution and does not automatically transfer all 18 Twelfth Schedule functions;
- State Finance Commission under Article 243I also reviews municipal finances under Article 243Y;
- municipal elections are under the State Election Commission;
- District Planning Committee elected share is at least four-fifths;
- Metropolitan Planning Committee elected share is at least two-thirds;
- Article 243ZG channels municipal election challenges into election petitions;
- Twelfth Schedule contains 18 matters.
