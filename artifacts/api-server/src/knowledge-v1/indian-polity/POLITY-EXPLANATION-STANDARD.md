# Indian Polity — Explanation Value Standard

## Qualification / eligibility questions

Whenever a question tests the qualification or eligibility of an important constitutional or legislative post, the explanation must give the complete high-yield qualification set in a small, easy-to-scan list.

Use this format only where it adds value:

**Qualifications:**
- short condition;
- short condition;
- short alternative/condition.

### Rules

- Keep each bullet short and exam-useful.
- Prefer plain language over constitutional drafting language.
- Give the full high-yield set even if the stem tests only one part of it.
- Use the Constitution or the governing statute as the factual source.
- Do not invent educational, professional, age or experience requirements where the Constitution/law does not prescribe them.
- If no specific qualification is constitutionally prescribed, say so clearly when that fact is relevant.
- Do not add qualification bullets to unrelated questions; normal explanations should remain concise and coherent.
- The structured qualification note may be slightly longer than the ordinary explanation-length target when necessary for completeness.

## Priority posts for chapter-wide backfill

During the final Polity audit, apply this rule wherever qualification/eligibility questions occur for high-yield posts, including:

- President and Vice-President;
- Governor;
- Member of Parliament / State Legislature where eligibility is directly tested;
- Supreme Court and High Court judges;
- Attorney-General for India;
- Advocate-General for the State;
- other constitutional posts only where the Constitution or governing law actually specifies qualification conditions.

## CP022 examples

### Attorney-General for India

Article 76 requires the Attorney-General for India to be qualified for appointment as a Supreme Court judge. The useful explanation should unpack Article 124(3):

**Qualifications:**
- citizen of India;
- at least 5 years as a High Court judge; **or**
- at least 10 years as a High Court advocate; **or**
- a distinguished jurist in the President's opinion.

### Advocate-General for the State

Article 165 requires the Advocate-General for the State to be qualified for appointment as a High Court judge. The useful explanation should unpack Article 217(2):

**Qualifications:**
- citizen of India;
- at least 10 years in judicial office in India; **or**
- at least 10 years as an advocate of a High Court (or two or more High Courts in succession).


## Final audit application — 2026-09-19

The full qualification-note rule has been applied to the approved English review generators for:

- President (Article 58);
- Vice-President (Article 66);
- Parliament membership (Article 84);
- Supreme Court Judges (Article 124);
- High Court Judges (Article 217);
- Governor (Article 157; Article 158 conditions kept separate);
- State Legislature membership (Article 173);
- Attorney-General and Advocate-General (already implemented in POL-CP-022 V2).

The POL-001 Question Studio integration includes a cross-chapter regression gate so these explanations cannot silently revert to single-condition notes.
