## Scope

This CP covers the constitutional Fundamental Rights framework in Part III with an exam-oriented map of Articles 12–35, including the saving provisions in Articles 31A–31C and the present constitutional status of the right to property under Article 300A.

## Core content

- Article 12 definition of State
- Article 13 and laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights
- Right to Equality: Articles 14–18
- Right to Freedom: Articles 19–22, including Article 21A
- Article 19 six freedoms and selected Article 19(2) restriction grounds
- Article 20: ex post facto criminal law, double jeopardy and self-incrimination
- Article 22 arrest safeguards and preventive-detention distinction
- Right against Exploitation: Articles 23–24
- Freedom of Religion: Articles 25–28
- Cultural and Educational Rights: Articles 29–30
- saving of certain laws: Articles 31A, 31B and currently operative Article 31C
- Article 32 and the five constitutional writs
- Articles 33–35 special Part III provisions
- citizen-only versus person-based rights
- right to property as a constitutional right under Article 300A, not a Fundamental Right

## Accuracy safeguards

- Article 21A is framed for all children aged six to fourteen, not as a citizens-only question family.
- Article 19 is treated as a citizens-only right family.
- Article 14, 20, 21 and 25 person-based distinctions are preserved.
- Article 31 is not presented as a current Fundamental Right.
- Article 31B is mapped to the Ninth Schedule without implying immunity from all constitutional judicial review.
- Article 31C uses the currently operative pre-Forty-second-Amendment scope tied to Article 39(b) and 39(c), supported by the 2024 Supreme Court Constitution Bench ruling.
- Article 32 writ scenarios distinguish Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari and Quo Warranto.
