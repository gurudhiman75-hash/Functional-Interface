## Scope

This CP covers the Governor as the constitutional head of the State executive, centred on Articles 153–167, with Governor-specific legislative powers under Articles 174–176, 200–201 and 213. General State Legislature structure and procedure remain for POL-CP-016.

## Core constitutional coverage

- Articles 153–156 — office, State executive power, appointment and tenure
- Articles 157–160 — qualifications, conditions, oath and contingencies
- Article 161 — clemency within the scope of State executive power
- Article 162 — extent of State executive power
- Article 163 — aid and advice and constitutionally required discretion
- Article 164 — Chief Minister and Ministers, collective responsibility, ministry size and six-month rule
- Article 165 — Advocate-General for the State
- Articles 166–167 — government business and Chief Minister's information duties
- Articles 174–176 — sessions, dissolution, messages and special address
- Articles 200–201 — assent, reconsideration and reservation of State Bills
- Article 213 — Governor's Ordinance power

## Accuracy guardrails

- the same person may be Governor of two or more States;
- appointment is by the President; term is five years subject to the President's pleasure;
- minimum age is 35 years;
- a Governor cannot remain a member of Parliament or a State Legislature and cannot hold another office of profit;
- Article 161 is stated by its exact State-executive-power scope rather than oversimplified death-sentence shortcuts;
- Article 163 discretion is not treated as a general personal policy power;
- State ministry size is capped at 15% of Assembly membership but cannot be fewer than 12;
- a non-member Minister has six consecutive months to enter the Legislature;
- collective responsibility is to the Legislative Assembly;
- a non-Money Bill may be returned under Article 200; after repassage the Governor shall not withhold assent;
- Article 201 reserved-Bill reconsideration period is six months;
- Article 213 can operate in a bicameral State when both Houses are not simultaneously in session;
- Ordinances normally cease six weeks after reassembly, counted from the later date where the two Houses reassemble on different dates.
