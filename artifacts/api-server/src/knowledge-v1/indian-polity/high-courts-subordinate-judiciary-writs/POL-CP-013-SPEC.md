## Scope

This CP covers Articles 214–237: High Courts, writ jurisdiction, supervisory powers, and the subordinate judiciary. Current office-holder, vacancy and sanctioned-strength facts remain excluded.

## Core constitutional coverage

- Articles 214–216 — High Courts for States, court-of-record status and composition
- Article 217 — appointment, retirement, resignation, removal and qualifications of High Court Judges
- Articles 218–221 — removal linkage, oath, post-retirement practice and salaries
- Articles 222–224A — transfer, acting Chief Justice, additional/acting Judges and retired Judges
- Article 225 — existing High Court jurisdiction
- Article 226 — writ jurisdiction, five writs, Fundamental Rights plus other purposes, territorial cause-of-action
- Article 227 — superintendence over courts and tribunals
- Article 228 — withdrawal of cases involving substantial constitutional interpretation questions
- Article 229 — High Court staff and administrative expenses
- Articles 230–231 — Union territory jurisdiction and common High Courts
- Article 233 — district judges
- Article 234 — recruitment below district-judge rank
- Article 235 — High Court control over subordinate courts
- Articles 236–237 — definitions and application to classes of magistrates

## Frozen stem standard

Use proper SSC/Banking/Railway/State-exam wording:
- `Which Article...?`
- `Who...?`
- `Under Article 226...?`
- short legal situations for writ selection
- concise statement-based integrated questions

Avoid database-style fragments such as `Article 226 deals with:` or unnatural stem endings.

## Frozen explanation standard

- simple but useful;
- exact constitutional rule first;
- one short distinction or exam trap where it helps;
- normally 13–32 words;
- no stock `Correct answer` opening;
- no generic filler ending;
- no unnecessary option-by-option analysis.

## Accuracy guardrails

- High Court Judge retirement age is 62 years;
- Article 217 has ten-year judicial-office or advocacy routes and no separate numerical minimum age;
- Article 226 covers Fundamental Rights and `any other purpose`;
- Article 226(2) preserves territorial jurisdiction when cause of action arises wholly or partly within the territory;
- Article 226 is kept distinct from Article 32 and Article 227;
- prohibition is preventive while certiorari is corrective in the standard exam distinction;
- Article 233 outsider district-judge eligibility requires at least seven years as advocate/pleader plus High Court recommendation;
- Article 234 consultation requires State PSC and High Court;
- Article 235 vests subordinate-judiciary control in the High Court.
