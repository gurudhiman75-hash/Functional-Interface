# POL-CP-017 — Centre–State Relations

**Lifecycle:** REVIEW CANDIDATE — V1  
**Runtime registration:** BLOCKED pending English approval

## Scope

This CP covers Centre–State relations through the constitutional distribution of legislative power, administrative coordination/control, and financial relations. Emergency mechanics remain principally reserved for POL-CP-018; this CP uses Article 250 only to explain its effect on legislative competence.

## Constitutional coverage

### Legislative relations
- Articles 245–246 — territorial extent and Union/State/Concurrent List distribution
- Article 246A — GST legislative competence and Parliament's exclusive inter-State GST role
- Article 248 — residuary legislative and taxation power
- Article 249 — Rajya Sabha national-interest route into State List legislation
- Articles 250–251 — Emergency-based parliamentary State List power and conflict rule
- Article 252 — parliamentary legislation for consenting States
- Article 253 — implementation of treaties and international obligations
- Article 254 — Concurrent List repugnancy and Presidential-assent exception

### Administrative relations
- Articles 256–257 — State compliance obligations and Union directions
- Articles 258–258A — reciprocal entrustment of executive functions
- Article 261 — full faith and credit and nationwide execution of civil judgments
- Article 262 — inter-State river-water disputes and possible exclusion of court jurisdiction
- Article 263 — Inter-State Council

### Financial relations
- Articles 268–270 — assignment/apportionment/distribution of specified revenues
- Article 269A — inter-State GST
- Article 271 — Union surcharge
- Article 275 — grants-in-aid
- Articles 279A and 280 — GST Council and Finance Commission links relevant to fiscal federalism
- Article 282 — public-purpose grants beyond legislative competence
- Article 285 — Union-property immunity from State taxation
- Article 289 — State property/income immunity from Union taxation, subject to trade/business qualification
- Article 293 — State borrowing and Union-consent condition

## Accuracy guardrails

- Parliament's law is not invalid merely because it has extra-territorial operation under Article 245;
- Article 246A gives Parliament exclusive power over GST on inter-State supplies;
- residuary power under Article 248 lies with Parliament, subject to Article 246A;
- Article 249 requires at least two-thirds of Rajya Sabha members present and voting and a resolution lasting at most one year, renewable one year at a time;
- laws dependent on Articles 249 or 250 normally cease six months after the enabling resolution/Emergency ends, to the extent of the special competence;
- Article 252 consent legislation cannot be independently amended or repealed by an applying State Legislature;
- Article 254(2) Presidential assent may make a repugnant State Concurrent List law prevail within that State, but Parliament may later override it;
- Article 256 compliance directions are distinct from Article 258 consensual entrustment;
- Article 262 may permit exclusion of Supreme Court and other court jurisdiction over covered river-water disputes;
- Article 263 Inter-State Council is established by Presidential order when public interest would be served;
- Article 268 separates Union levy from State collection/appropriation of specified duties;
- Article 269A inter-State GST is levied and collected by Government of India and apportioned under parliamentary law on GST Council recommendations;
- Article 271 surcharge excludes GST under Article 246A;
- Article 282 grants are not limited by the grantor's legislative competence;
- Articles 285 and 289 tax immunities are reciprocal but not identical;
- Article 293 State borrowing is within India, secured on the State Consolidated Fund, with Union consent required where specified Union debt/guarantees remain outstanding.
