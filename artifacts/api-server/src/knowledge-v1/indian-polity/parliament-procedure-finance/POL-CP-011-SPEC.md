## Scope

This CP covers the parliamentary law-making and financial-procedure block centred on Articles 107–117. It continues directly from POL-CP-010, which covered Parliament structure and officers.

## Constitutional coverage

- Article 107 — introduction, passage and lapse of Bills
- Article 108 — joint sitting of both Houses
- Article 109 — special Money Bill procedure
- Article 110 — Money Bill definition and Speaker certification
- Article 111 — presidential assent and return of non-Money Bills
- Article 112 — Annual Financial Statement
- Article 113 — estimates and demands for grants
- Article 114 — Appropriation Bills
- Article 115 — supplementary, additional and excess grants
- Article 116 — vote on account, vote of credit and exceptional grants
- Article 117 — special provisions for financial Bills

## Procedural coverage from official Parliament sources

- Government Bill vs Private Member's Bill
- three readings of a Bill
- first, second and third reading roles
- Speaker of Lok Sabha presiding over a joint sitting
- Disapproval of Policy Cut, Economy Cut and Token Cut

## V2 explanation standard

Continue the approved Polity style, but make the explanation slightly more useful for revision:
- start with the exact rule behind the answer;
- add one short reason, distinction or exam trap where it helps understanding;
- keep simple wording and avoid legal-heavy prose;
- normal explanation range: 18–32 words;
- do not add filler merely to reach a length target;
- every explanation in the 88-question review remains unique;
- no stock opening or generic filler ending;
- no unnecessary option-by-option analysis.

Typical useful additions include:
- why a Bill lapses or survives on dissolution/prorogation;
- why Rajya Sabha has a limited role on Money Bills;
- the difference between supplementary, additional and excess grants;
- the distinction between discussion and voting of charged expenditure;
- the timing difference between vote on account and excess grant;
- the separate Article 117 recommendation rules.

## Accuracy guardrails

- prorogation does not lapse a pending Bill;
- dissolution rules distinguish Bills pending in Rajya Sabha from Bills pending in or passed by Lok Sabha;
- Article 108 joint sitting excludes Money Bills, and Constitution Amendment Bills cannot use joint sitting;
- the Article 108 six-month count excludes prorogation and adjournment exceeding four consecutive days;
- Rajya Sabha has recommendation power, not equal amendment power, over Money Bills and has fourteen days;
- Article 110 uses an `only provisions` test and preserves the fines/fees/local-tax exclusions;
- Speaker certification and the Speaker's Money Bill decision are preserved;
- a Money Bill cannot be returned by the President for reconsideration;
- charged expenditure may be discussed but is not voted;
- demands for grants are voted only in Lok Sabha;
- Appropriation Bill amendments cannot vary grant amount/destination or charged expenditure;
- vote on account, vote of credit and exceptional grant are kept distinct;
- cut-motion amounts remain Re. 1 for Policy Cut and Rs. 100 for Token Cut;
- Article 117 distinctions are stated without treating textbook category labels as constitutional wording.
