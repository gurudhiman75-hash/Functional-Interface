## Scope

POL-CP-006 covers three linked Static GK domains:

1. Article 368 and the constitutional amendment procedure;
2. the basic structure doctrine and its highest-yield Supreme Court cases;
3. all twelve Schedules and high-yield constitutional amendments.

## Canonical coverage

### Amendment procedure
- Article 368 subject and scope
- introduction in either House of Parliament
- special majority formula
- State-ratification requirement for specified federal provisions
- no joint sitting for a Constitution Amendment Bill
- presidential assent after due passage
- selected constitutional changes made by ordinary law outside Article 368

### Basic structure
- Kesavananda Bharati (1973)
- Minerva Mills (1980)
- S. R. Bommai (1994)
- I. R. Coelho (2007)
- supremacy of Constitution
- republican/democratic government
- secularism
- federal character
- separation of powers
- judicial review
- limited amending power
- harmony/balance between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles

### Schedules
Complete map of First through Twelfth Schedules, including:
- Seventh Schedule Lists
- Eighth Schedule 22 languages
- Ninth Schedule/basic-structure boundary
- Tenth Schedule anti-defection
- Eleventh Schedule 29 Panchayat subjects
- Twelfth Schedule 18 Municipality subjects

### High-yield amendments
Includes selected exam-relevant amendments from the First through the One Hundred and Sixth Amendment, with emphasis on the 24th, 42nd, 44th, 52nd, 61st, 73rd, 74th, 86th, 91st, 101st, 103rd and 106th Amendments.

## Explanation standard

Every explanation must do more than restate the correct option. It should normally:

1. state the governing constitutional rule or case principle;
2. explain why that rule leads to the answer;
3. mention the key distinction or common trap where it improves understanding;
4. stay concise and beginner-friendly rather than becoming a mini legal note.

Mechanical explanation gates:
- minimum 20 words;
- explanation must not equal the canonical answer;
- Article 368 special-majority explanations must mention both `total membership` and `present and voting`;
- Amendment Bill procedure must preserve the `no joint sitting` distinction;
- Fifth/Sixth Schedule explanations must distinguish the two frameworks;
- Eleventh/Twelfth Schedule explanations must distinguish Panchayats/29 subjects from Municipalities/18 subjects;
- legalistic boilerplate is blocked.

## Safety/accuracy boundaries

- basic structure is not represented as a closed constitutional list;
- Ninth Schedule questions preserve the post-Kesavananda judicial-review qualification;
- State-ratification questions are limited to clearly supported Article 368 federal areas;
- the 106th Amendment is described with its constitutional commencement/delimitation framework rather than as an already fully operational seat allocation.
