## Scope

Included:
- purpose of the IUCN Red List
- IUCN categories EX, EW, CR, EN, VU, NT, LC, DD and NE
- threatened-category grouping: CR, EN and VU
- broad extinction-risk ordering among CR, EN and VU
- distinction between Extinct and Extinct in the Wild
- distinction between Near Threatened, Least Concern, Data Deficient and Not Evaluated
- in-situ conservation
- ex-situ conservation
- standard ex-situ examples: zoos, botanical gardens, seed/gene banks and captive breeding
- applied distinction between in-situ and ex-situ approaches

Explicitly deferred:
- current IUCN status of named species
- changing population estimates
- protected-area categories and legal details (ENV-CP-009 onward)
- individual national parks, sanctuaries and reserves (ENV-CP-010)
- CITES and international conventions (ENV-CP-017)

## Learner-facing rules

1. Stems use competitive-exam formats: direct fact, abbreviation, correct/incorrect pair, statements and applied identification.
2. Explanations use one short fact or distinction.
3. No current status of a named species is asked because such assessments can change.
4. CR, EN and VU are the IUCN threatened categories.
5. CR means extremely high risk, EN very high risk and VU high risk of extinction in the wild.
6. EW means surviving only in cultivation, captivity or qualifying populations outside the past range; EX is stronger and means the taxon is extinct.
7. DD means available information is inadequate for a proper extinction-risk assessment; it does not mean low risk.
8. NE means the taxon has not yet been evaluated against the criteria.
9. In-situ conservation maintains species in natural surroundings; ex-situ conservation keeps biodiversity components outside natural habitats.
10. Review generators remain `runtimeRegistered: false` until project-owner approval.
