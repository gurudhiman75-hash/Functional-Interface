## Scope

Included:
- meaning and major types of pollution
- air, water, soil and noise pollution
- common source categories
- point and non-point water-pollution sources
- primary and secondary air-pollutant distinction at concept level
- preliminary/basic sewage-treatment stage distinction: primary, secondary and tertiary
- basic air-pollution control devices: cyclone separator, bag filter, electrostatic precipitator and scrubber
- source reduction and basic waste-management hierarchy
- applied pollution-source and control-method identification

Explicitly deferred:
- detailed pollutant–effect associations, heavy metals, pesticides, biomagnification, eutrophication and BOD/COD detail (ENV-CP-012)
- ozone depletion, greenhouse effect and atmospheric chemistry (ENV-CP-013)
- climate-change mechanisms and impacts (ENV-CP-014)
- detailed environmental laws (ENV-CP-015)
- current air-quality readings, city rankings or changing standards

## Learner-facing rules

1. Stems use direct competitive-exam formats from V1.
2. Explanations use one short fact or distinction.
3. Air, water, soil and noise questions focus on source/type/control concepts rather than long effect lists.
4. Point source means a discernible, confined or discrete discharge source; non-point pollution comes from diffuse sources such as runoff.
5. Primary pollutants are emitted directly; secondary pollutants form in the environment from reactions involving other pollutants.
6. Primary sewage treatment is mainly physical; secondary treatment is mainly biological; tertiary treatment provides further advanced polishing/removal.
7. ESP, cyclone separators and bag filters are particulate-control devices; scrubbers can remove particles and/or gaseous contaminants depending on design.
8. No learner question uses mutable city pollution rankings or current numerical standards.
9. Review generator remains `runtimeRegistered: false`; shared runtime integration is a separate workflow.
