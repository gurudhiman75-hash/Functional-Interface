## Scope

Included:
- five major atmospheric layers and their order
- troposphere: lowest layer, most weather, most atmospheric water vapour
- basic dry-air composition at exam level
- stratosphere and the ozone layer
- mesosphere, thermosphere and exosphere identifiers
- protective role of stratospheric ozone against harmful ultraviolet radiation
- ground-level ozone versus stratospheric ozone
- ozone-depleting substances and the chlorine/bromine depletion mechanism
- common ODS examples such as CFCs, HCFCs, halons and methyl bromide
- distinction between HFCs and ozone-depleting substances
- greenhouse effect and its role in keeping Earth warmer than it would otherwise be
- major greenhouse gases at concept level: water vapour, carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, ozone and CFCs
- water vapour as an important greenhouse-gas feedback
- correct/incorrect pairs, statements and applied identification

Explicitly deferred:
- climate-change impacts, mitigation, adaptation and detailed forcing trends (ENV-CP-014)
- environmental treaty history and detailed Montreal/Vienna Convention provisions (ENV-CP-017)
- current ozone-hole size, recovery-year projections and annual atmospheric concentration records
- current greenhouse-gas concentration values

## Learner-facing rules

1. Stems use direct competitive-exam formats from V1.
2. Explanations use one short fact or distinction.
3. Layer questions focus on stable order and signature features, not fragile altitude cut-offs.
4. Troposphere is treated as the lowest layer and the main weather layer.
5. Stratosphere is treated as the layer containing most atmospheric ozone.
6. Stratospheric ozone is protective; ground-level ozone is an air pollutant and a component of smog.
7. ODS questions focus on stable mechanism: compounds reach the stratosphere, UV breaks them down, and chlorine/bromine can destroy ozone.
8. HFCs are not treated as ozone-depleting substances; they are greenhouse gases.
9. Greenhouse effect questions distinguish the natural heat-trapping process from later climate-change impacts.
10. No learner question uses current ozone-hole area, current atmospheric concentration or projection-year trivia.
11. Review generators remain `runtimeRegistered: false`; shared runtime/package integration is handled separately.
