### WHI-CP006-Q001 · Easy · Revolutions of 1848

Which political ideas were commonly linked with the European revolutions of 1848?

- **A.** Liberal constitutional government and national self-determination
- **B.** Restoration of the conservative order established after the Napoleonic Wars
- **C.** Expansion of dynastic rule without representative institutions
- **D.** Preservation of existing empires by rejecting national claims
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Many 1848 movements combined demands for constitutional or representative government with calls for national unity or self-rule. Their aims differed across regions.
**Source:** CP006-S02

### WHI-CP006-Q002 · Easy · German national movement

Which city hosted the German National Assembly that met in 1848?

- **A.** Berlin
- **B.** Frankfurt
- **C.** Munich
- **D.** Vienna
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Delegates to the first German National Assembly met in Frankfurt in 1848 and worked on a constitution for a united Germany.
**Source:** CP006-S02

### WHI-CP006-Q003 · Medium · Frankfurt Assembly

What was a central task undertaken by the Frankfurt Assembly in 1848–49?

- **A.** Restoring the German Confederation created after the Congress of Vienna
- **B.** Creating the Austro-Hungarian Dual Monarchy
- **C.** Forming the North German Confederation under Prussian leadership
- **D.** Establishing a constitution for a proposed German nation-state
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** The assembly debated and drafted a constitution for a unified German state. Its project did not establish the empire later proclaimed in 1871.
**Sources:** CP006-S01, CP006-S02

### WHI-CP006-Q004 · Medium · Nationalism and liberalism

Which statement best describes the relationship between nationalism and liberalism in many 1848 movements?

- **A.** Nationalism always took priority over constitutional reform
- **B.** Liberalism required the breakup of every multinational empire
- **C.** Nationalist and liberal demands could overlap, but did not coincide in every movement
- **D.** They could overlap, as activists sought both national unity and constitutional rights
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** In several regions, national unity and constitutional government were connected demands. The movements were diverse, so the two aims did not always coincide.
**Source:** CP006-S02

### WHI-CP006-Q005 · Medium · Limits of the Frankfurt project

Which event showed that the Frankfurt Assembly could not secure the proposed German crown through its own authority?

- **A.** The assembly lacked its own army and depended on German rulers to enforce its decisions
- **B.** The assembly excluded all Prussian delegates from its debates
- **C.** The assembly transferred its constitutional project to the Congress of Berlin
- **D.** The Prussian king declined the crown offered by the assembly
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** The assembly lacked the power to impose its settlement on German rulers. Frederick William IV of Prussia rejected the imperial crown offered by the delegates in 1849.
**Sources:** CP006-S01, CP006-S02

### WHI-CP006-Q006 · Hard · Comparing routes to unification

Which comparison between the 1848 Frankfurt project and German unification in 1871 is accurate?

- **A.** Both were completed by the same elected assembly
- **B.** The 1871 empire included Austria as its leading state
- **C.** The Frankfurt project relied on a popular parliament, while the 1871 state emerged under Prussian leadership after wars and political agreements
- **D.** The Frankfurt Assembly created the 1871 empire before the Danish-Prussian War
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The Frankfurt Assembly’s constitutional project failed. The later empire was created under Prussian hegemony, following wars against Denmark, Austria and France; Austria was outside the new German state.
**Sources:** CP006-S01, CP006-S02

### WHI-CP006-Q007 · Easy · Young Italy

Who founded the nationalist organisation Young Italy?

- **A.** Camillo Cavour
- **B.** Giuseppe Mazzini
- **C.** Giuseppe Garibaldi
- **D.** Victor Emmanuel II
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Giuseppe Mazzini founded Young Italy, which promoted a united Italian nation and republican ideas.
**Sources:** CP006-S03, CP006-S04

### WHI-CP006-Q008 · Easy · Piedmont-Sardinia

Which kingdom became the principal state base for the political unification of Italy?

- **A.** Kingdom of the Two Sicilies
- **B.** Papal States
- **C.** Kingdom of Piedmont-Sardinia
- **D.** Kingdom of Lombardy-Venetia
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Piedmont-Sardinia, ruled by the House of Savoy, became the main state platform for the unification process.
**Sources:** CP006-S03, CP006-S07

### WHI-CP006-Q009 · Medium · Cavour

Which role is most closely associated with Camillo Cavour in Italian unification?

- **A.** He led the Expedition of the Thousand to Sicily
- **B.** He served as prime minister of Piedmont-Sardinia and used diplomacy to advance unification
- **C.** He founded Young Italy as a republican organisation
- **D.** He became the first king of unified Italy
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Cavour was a leading statesman of Piedmont-Sardinia. His diplomacy and political strategy helped expand the kingdom and advance unification.
**Sources:** CP006-S03, CP006-S07

### WHI-CP006-Q010 · Medium · Expedition of the Thousand

What was the outcome of Garibaldi’s Expedition of the Thousand in 1860?

- **A.** It restored Austrian control over Lombardy
- **B.** It established the German Empire
- **C.** It transferred Rome to the Kingdom of Italy
- **D.** It helped overthrow Bourbon rule in Sicily and southern Italy
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Garibaldi’s volunteer force landed in Sicily and helped bring the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies under the unification movement. Rome joined Italy later.
**Sources:** CP006-S03, CP006-S04, CP006-S07

### WHI-CP006-Q011 · Medium · Italian unification leaders

Which leader is correctly matched with his role in the Italian unification movement?

- **A.** Mazzini — led the Expedition of the Thousand
- **B.** Garibaldi — founded Young Italy
- **C.** Cavour — prime minister of Piedmont-Sardinia
- **D.** Victor Emmanuel II — led the Frankfurt Assembly
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Cavour served as prime minister of Piedmont-Sardinia. Mazzini founded Young Italy, while Garibaldi led the Expedition of the Thousand.
**Sources:** CP006-S03, CP006-S04, CP006-S07

### WHI-CP006-Q012 · Easy · Kingdom of Italy

In which year was the Kingdom of Italy proclaimed?

- **A.** 1848
- **B.** 1859
- **C.** 1861
- **D.** 1871
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed on 17 March 1861, with Victor Emmanuel II as its king.
**Sources:** CP006-S03, CP006-S04

### WHI-CP006-Q013 · Medium · Italian chronology

Arrange these events in chronological order:

1. Proclamation of the Kingdom of Italy
2. Garibaldi’s Expedition of the Thousand
3. Rome becomes part of the Kingdom of Italy

- **A.** 1 → 2 → 3
- **B.** 3 → 2 → 1
- **C.** 2 → 3 → 1
- **D.** 2 → 1 → 3
**Answer:** D
**Explanation:** Garibaldi’s expedition took place in 1860. The kingdom was proclaimed in 1861, and Rome was incorporated in 1870.
**Sources:** CP006-S03, CP006-S04, CP006-S07

### WHI-CP006-Q014 · Medium · Completing unification

Which territory was incorporated into the Kingdom of Italy in 1870?

- **A.** Sicily
- **B.** Lombardy
- **C.** Rome
- **D.** Venetia
**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** Italian forces entered Rome in 1870, and the city became part of the Kingdom of Italy. The kingdom had been proclaimed in 1861.
**Sources:** CP006-S03, CP006-S04

### WHI-CP006-Q015 · Hard · Unification sequence

Which sequence correctly traces key stages of Italian unification?

- **A.** Kingdom proclaimed (1861) → Rome incorporated (1870) → Venetia incorporated (1866)
- **B.** Garibaldi’s expedition (1860) → Kingdom proclaimed (1861) → Venetia incorporated (1866) → Rome incorporated (1870)
- **C.** Rome incorporated (1861) → Venetia incorporated (1860) → Kingdom proclaimed (1870)
- **D.** Young Italy founded (1861) → Kingdom proclaimed (1870) → Garibaldi’s expedition (1871)
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The expedition helped bring the south into the movement before the kingdom was proclaimed. Venetia joined in 1866, and Rome in 1870.
**Sources:** CP006-S03, CP006-S04, CP006-S07

Who became the first king of the Kingdom of Italy in 1861?

- **A.** Victor Emmanuel II
- **B.** Francis Joseph I
- **C.** Frederick William IV

### WHI-CP006-Q017 · Medium · Mazzini and Cavour

Which distinction between Mazzini and Cavour is accurate?

- **A.** Mazzini promoted a republican national movement; Cavour advanced unification through Piedmont-Sardinian statecraft and diplomacy
- **B.** Mazzini founded Young Italy; Cavour led the Expedition of the Thousand
- **C.** Mazzini served as prime minister of Piedmont-Sardinia; Cavour promoted a republican movement
- **D.** Mazzini led the southern campaign; Cavour opposed the expansion of Piedmont-Sardinia
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Mazzini’s Young Italy promoted a republican vision. Cavour worked through the government of Piedmont-Sardinia and diplomatic alliances.
**Sources:** CP006-S03, CP006-S04, CP006-S07

### WHI-CP006-Q018 · Medium · Garibaldi and Cavour

What best describes the relationship between Garibaldi’s campaign in southern Italy and Cavour’s policy?

- **A.** Garibaldi’s military successes were incorporated into a wider state-led unification process
- **B.** Garibaldi’s expedition permanently divided the peninsula into rival kingdoms
- **C.** Cavour led the expedition and Garibaldi negotiated the 1861 proclamation
- **D.** Both sought to place southern Italy under Austrian rule
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Garibaldi’s campaign changed control in the south, while Cavour’s government helped bring those gains into the expanding Kingdom of Italy. Their methods and political aims were not identical.
**Sources:** CP006-S03, CP006-S04, CP006-S07

### WHI-CP006-Q019 · Hard · Incomplete unification

Which statement about the proclamation of the Kingdom of Italy in 1861 is correct?

- **A.** It immediately included Rome and Venetia
- **B.** It marked a major stage of unification, but Rome and Venetia joined later
- **C.** It followed the Congress of Berlin
- **D.** It ended the role of Piedmont-Sardinia before a national state formed
**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The 1861 kingdom did not yet include all territories later associated with Italy. Venetia joined in 1866 and Rome in 1870.
**Sources:** CP006-S03, CP006-S04

### WHI-CP006-Q020 · Medium · Italian unification

Which statement best explains why Italian unification is associated with more than one leader?

- **A.** Mazzini, Cavour and Garibaldi contributed through different political, diplomatic and military approaches
- **B.** Their political aims and methods were identical throughout the movement
- **C.** Italian unification was achieved by a single leader without diplomacy or popular campaigns
- **D.** The movement began only after the Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed
**Answer:** A
**Explanation:** Mazzini promoted national ideas, Cavour used state diplomacy, and Garibaldi led volunteer campaigns. Unification resulted from multiple actors and stages.
**Sources:** CP006-S03, CP006-S04, CP006-S07
